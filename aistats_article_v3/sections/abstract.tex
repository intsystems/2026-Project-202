\begin{abstract}
\claude{Neural network dynamics can simplify during learning, but measuring this change often requires access to high-dimensional states or costly diagnostics. We develop a method that detects simplification from a single scalar log by combining delay reconstruction with the pooled MacKay--Ghahramani (MG) dimension estimator. Delay-embedding theory motivates interpreting the reconstructed geometry in terms of active components. Synthetic experiments test this interpretation, including the distinction between independent phases and harmonics. In learned systems, MG tracks restricted parameter motion and latent information loss. It also supports model and policy selection; in self-supervised learning, a gradient-norm log ranks configurations better than several embedding-based proxies in the tested setting. Once the log is available, analysis requires no model access. A sampled-query implementation further reduces computation while preserving forecast choices in our benchmark. Exact dimensional interpretation requires suitable recurrence and observation conditions. For general training logs, we evaluate MG as a change statistic against independent measurements and competing scalar methods. This yields a low-cost geometric diagnostic with demonstrated uses across distinct learning tasks.}
\end{abstract}
