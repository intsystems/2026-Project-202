\begin{abstract}
Within any window of training, the parameter trajectory of a neural network occupies a subspace
far smaller than the parameter count. We treat the size of that subspace, the active dimension,
as a count of components rather than a score, and ask how much of it survives the projection
onto a single scalar log. The estimator is standard, delay reconstruction followed by a maximum
likelihood intrinsic dimension; the contribution is knowing when its output is a count. On five
systems of increasing realism, from a prescribed oscillation to a network trained on image
data, whose active dimension is constructed and then verified by measurement, recovery is
accurate to under one component up to eight directions on withheld ranks and seeds, and the
ordering survives to twenty. Two diagnostics then decide from the series alone whether the
value may be read as a count. Applied to grokking they place both standard experimental
settings outside the admissible regime, for two different reasons which they identify
correctly, while the estimate still separates generalising from non-generalising runs in four
label-matched pairs. Measured directly from the trajectory covariance, the active dimension
collapses to essentially one at the onset of generalisation and also falls in runs that never
generalise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A neural network is trained in a parameter space of dimension $P$, but the trajectory the
optimiser follows does not fill it. Within any window the parameter increments concentrate in
a subspace of much smaller dimension, and the size of that subspace changes over the course of
a run. We call it the \emph{active dimension} of the window and define it in \cref{eq:act} as
the participation ratio of the trajectory covariance, which counts the directions carrying
comparable weight. It is therefore a count of components rather than a score: it states that
one window excites four directions and the next excites one, on a scale that does not depend
on the observer, the parameterisation or the task. Delay-embedding statistics return a
plausible number whether or not it is such a count, and most of the work below goes into
deciding which of the two one has.

It is not the dimension of the subspace the optimiser is confined to, which the
parameterisation fixes \citep{li2018measuring}, nor the rank of the map from that subspace to
the model's outputs. Only the active dimension varies over a run, and \cref{sec:three} shows
the three to differ measurably.

Measuring it directly requires storing the trajectory in parameter space, which is expensive; a
scalar log costs nothing, and a run already keeps several. We therefore ask how much of the
active dimension survives the projection onto one such series. The instrument is standard,
delay reconstruction \citep{takens1981detecting, sauer1991embedology} followed by the pooled
Levina--Bickel estimator \citep{levina2004maximum, mackay2005comments}, and its hazard is that
it returns a number when no invariant set exists at all: noise with a power-law spectrum yields
a finite and reproducible dimension set by its spectral exponent \citep{osborne1989finite}, and
a decaying transient, which is what a converging run looks like, yields a large one.
\Cref{sec:protocol} therefore fixes a validation protocol before any result. Two of our five
systems fail one of its controls, reproducing their reported result with no training taking
place, and are kept in \cref{sec:decoder} rather than removed.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item We separate three quantities that are frequently identified with one another: the
dimension of the subspace to which the optimiser is confined, the rank of the map from that
subspace to the outputs, and the number of directions excited within a window
(\cref{sec:three}). Only the third is estimated here, and it is measured rather than assumed
throughout.
\item We evaluate the estimator on five systems of increasing realism, ending with a network
trained on image data (\cref{sec:ladder}). It recovers the active dimension to $0.87$
components up to eight directions, uncalibrated, on withheld ranks and seeds, and orders it to
twenty.
\item We measure the conditions the absolute value requires (\cref{sec:validity}) and validate
two diagnostics that decide admissibility from the series alone.
\item Applied to grokking (\cref{sec:grokking}), those diagnostics place both published
experimental settings outside the admissible regime; measured directly from the trajectory
covariance, the active dimension collapses to essentially one at the onset of generalisation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Delay reconstruction.} Takens' theorem \citep{takens1981detecting}, its prevalence
form in terms of the box-counting dimension \citep{sauer1991embedology} and its extensions to
externally forced systems \citep{stark1999delay, stark2003delay} license the reconstruction used
here; empirical dynamic modelling applies the same machinery to ecological and physiological
series \citep{sugihara1990nonlinear, sugihara2012detecting}. The lag and the embedding dimension
are conventionally chosen by mutual information \citep{fraser1986independent} and false nearest
neighbours \citep{kennel1992determining, cao1997practical}, and the linear alternative to the
whole procedure is the singular spectrum of the delay matrix \citep{broomhead1986extracting},
which we report throughout as $\PR_{\mathrm{delay}}$.

\paragraph{Intrinsic dimension, and when it is spurious.} The dimension of the reconstructed
cloud is estimated by the maximum likelihood estimator of \citet{levina2004maximum} with the
pooling correction of \citet{mackay2005comments}, whose bias relative to the per-point average
is known in closed form and is verified in \cref{sec:matrix}; the correlation dimension
\citep{grassberger1983measuring} and the neighbour-ratio estimator \citep{facco2017estimating}
are the standard alternatives. Two spurious regimes are documented: temporally adjacent
neighbours inflate the estimate on short records \citep{theiler1986spurious}, and noise with a
power-law spectrum produces a finite reproducible value \citep{osborne1989finite}. The usual
defence, a surrogate-data test against a linear-stochastic null \citep{theiler1992testing,
schreiber1996improved}, answers whether nonlinear structure is present rather than how many
components there are, and does not cover the regime that dominates optimisation logs: a
deterministic transient has neither an attractor nor noise, and returns a large stable number
where the true active dimension is one.

\paragraph{Dimension in optimisation.} Random-subspace training \citep{li2018measuring} fixes
the available dimension and reports the smallest $k$ at which a task is still solvable, a
property of the task rather than of a window of a run; we borrow the parameterisation and
measure a different quantity inside it. The closest existing statement to the active dimension
is that gradient descent occurs largely in a small subspace spanned by the top Hessian
eigenvectors \citep{gurari2018gradient}, measured in parameter space from stored gradients,
whereas the intrinsic dimension of data and of representations \citep{ansuini2019intrinsic,
pope2021intrinsic} is that of a cloud of points in activation space rather than of a
trajectory; the participation ratio we adopt is the standard dimensionality measure for neural
population activity \citep{gao2017theory}. All of these need the weights, the gradients or the
activations, and the nearest precedent for a scalar computed during training and then used to
decide something is the gradient noise scale \citep{mccandlish2018empirical}. The recurrent
regime our estimate requires is not exotic, since full-batch descent typically operates at the
edge of stability \citep{cohen2021gradient}, while mini-batch training near a minimum is well
described as a stochastic process \citep{mandt2017stochastic} whose delay map embeds the state
with its innovations \citep{stark2003delay}.

\paragraph{Grokking.} We reproduce the two settings in which delayed generalisation
\citep{power2022grokking} is usually studied: the regularised stochastic one of
\citet{liu2022omnigrok}, and the unregularised full-batch one of \citet{gromov2023grokking} and
\citet{doshi2024grokking}, whose closed-form solution supplies both label-matched target pairs
and an analytic reference for the dimension of the learned representation. Progress measures and
explanations for the transition are defined in weight or activation space
\citep{nanda2023progress, varma2023explaining, kumar2024grokking, liu2022towards}. The work
closest to ours reads the loss curve alone: \citet{notsawo2023predicting} classify
hyperparameter configurations from spectral features of the first few hundred steps. We estimate
instead a dynamical quantity with an independently measured ground truth, on systems where the
answer is known before it is applied to runs where it is not.

\section{Problem statement}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Available, functional and active dimension}\label{sec:three}

For a non-negative spectrum $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ we write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pr}
  \PR(\lambda) \;=\; \frac{\bigl(\sum_i \lambda_i\bigr)^2}{\sum_i \lambda_i^2}
\end{equation}
for its participation ratio, which counts the components carrying comparable weight: it equals
$m$ exactly when $m$ of the $\lambda_i$ are equal and the rest vanish, and it is invariant to a
rescaling of the spectrum.

Let $\vtheta_t \in \R^P$ be the parameter vector at step $t$, and let the optimiser be
confined, by construction or by the dynamics, to an affine subspace
$\vtheta = \vtheta_0 + \mV^{\top} \vc$ with $\mV \in \R^{k \times P}$ orthonormal and
$\vc_t \in \R^k$. Over a window $\gW$ the \emph{available} dimension is $k$; the
\emph{functional} dimension is the rank of $\partial \vf(\vc)/\partial \vc$, where $\vf$
collects the outputs on a fixed probe set, bounding how many available directions can change
the computed function; and the \emph{active} dimension is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:act}
  d^{\mathrm{act}}(\gW) \;=\; \PR\bigl(\operatorname{spec} \Cov \{\vc_t : t \in \gW\}\bigr),
\end{equation}
together with the same quantity computed on the increments $\Delta \vc_t$.

The three are distinct, and measurably so. In the system of \cref{sec:digits} the available
dimension is $10$ and the output Jacobian has rank $10$; removing the preconditioner from the
update rule leaves both unchanged, and the injected rank intact, while the active dimension
falls from $7.99$ to $6.43$ under stochastic forcing, since anisotropic curvature redistributes
the excitation across the available directions. Only the active dimension is estimated here,
always against its measured value.

\subsection{Observation model and estimator}\label{sec:estimator}

We observe a scalar series $x_t = \phi(\vs_t)$, with $\vs_t$ the full optimiser state and $\phi$
fixed before the run, and reconstruct
$\vy_t = (x_t, x_{t-\tau}, \dots, x_{t-(E-1)\tau}) \in \R^E$. For a deterministic flow on a
compact invariant set of dimension $d$ this map is generically an embedding once $E > 2d$, so
the reconstructed set is diffeomorphic to the original and carries its dimension
\citep{takens1981detecting, sauer1991embedology}.

We estimate the intrinsic dimension of $\{\vy_t\}$ by maximum likelihood on nearest-neighbour
distances \citep{levina2004maximum}. With $r^{(i)}_1 \le \dots \le r^{(i)}_m$ the distances
from point $i$ to its $m$ nearest neighbours, subject to a Theiler exclusion $W$ that forbids
neighbours closer than $W$ steps in time \citep{theiler1986spurious}, the local estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb}
  \hat{d}_i \;=\; \Biggl[ \frac{1}{m-1} \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}
  \log \frac{r^{(i)}_m}{r^{(i)}_j} \Biggr]^{-1},
\end{equation}
and the two ways of combining the $N$ local estimates differ. Averaging them, as
\citet{levina2004maximum} propose, gives $\hat{d}_{\mathrm{LB}} = N^{-1} \sum_i \hat{d}_i$.
Pooling the likelihood instead, as \citet{mackay2005comments} argue one should, averages the
reciprocals:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg}
  \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}^{\,-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{N(m-1)} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}
  \log \frac{r^{(i)}_m}{r^{(i)}_j} .
\end{equation}
We write LB for the average of \cref{eq:lb} and MG for the pooled form of \cref{eq:mg}. Since
$\hat{d}_i^{\,-1}$ is a sum of $m-1$ exponential variates it is unbiased where $\hat{d}_i$ is
not, so the two differ by the factor $(m-1)/(m-2)$, which \cref{sec:matrix} confirms accounts
for the whole gap between them; Appendix~\ref{app:alg} states the algorithm.

Three further statistics accompany every MG value, because each can reproduce an apparent
dimension signal without any geometry: the \emph{roughness}
$\operatorname{std}(\Delta x)/\operatorname{std}(x)$; the \emph{linear participation ratio}
$\PR_{\mathrm{delay}}$, \cref{eq:pr} applied to $\Cov\{\vy_t\}$; and the \emph{spectral
participation ratio}, \cref{eq:pr} applied to the power spectrum of $x$.

\subsection{Dynamical regimes}\label{sec:classes}

The embedding theorems require a deterministic flow on a compact invariant set the trajectory
revisits. An optimiser near a solution may be in any of the three regimes of
\cref{tab:classes}, and only the first meets that requirement.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{What a delay reconstruction of a scalar observation contains in each dynamical
regime. Only the first admits a dimension.}
\label{tab:classes}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
regime & reconstructed object & its dimension \\
\midrule
deterministic, recurrent & diffeomorphic copy of the $r$-torus & $r$ \\
deterministic, transient & a one-dimensional curve in $t$ & $1$, for every $r$ \\
stochastically driven & state together with $E-1$ innovations & full rank in $\R^E$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The third regime is what mini-batch gradient noise produces. Embedding theorems for
stochastically forced systems exist \citep{stark1999delay, stark2003delay}, but what they embed
is the state together with its noise history, a skew product rather than an $r$-dimensional
attractor, and coloured noise alone yields a finite reproducible dimension fixed by its
spectral exponent \citep{osborne1989finite, theiler1986spurious}. The second regime is equally
consequential and less often noticed: a decaying transient traces a curve, so its active
dimension is $1$ whatever the number of available directions.

\subsection{Admissibility diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostics}

Both failure regimes can be detected without a ground truth, by two statistics and one flag. The
\textbf{identifiability ratio}
$\rho_{\mathrm{ident}} = \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}(2E_{\max}) / \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}(E_{\max})$ is
near unity when a dimension is resolvable and grows when the estimate is a property of the
embedding space rather than of the data. The \textbf{recurrence count}, the number of times the
series crosses its own local trend inside a window, identifies the transient regime, in which
$\rho_{\mathrm{ident}}$ is near unity for the wrong reason. The \textbf{degeneracy flag}
returns no value when the neighbour distances or the log-ratio sum fall below their numerical
floors. \Cref{sec:diagnostics-validated} gives their reference values.

\section{Validation protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

Delay-embedding statistics return a finite number on data satisfying none of their hypotheses.
Six requirements are fixed in advance, and every result here is held to them.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt\parsep0pt
\item \emph{The ground truth is measured, not assumed}, from \cref{eq:act} on the recorded
trajectory and increments.
\item \emph{The estimator configuration is selected once and frozen}, on a grid of estimator
parameters only, since a grid over system parameters changes the data when a configuration is
chosen.
\item \emph{The split is disjoint in seed and in rank}, since withholding seeds alone leaves
the excitation geometry shared whenever it is a function of the rank.
\item \emph{A zero learning rate must silence the observer}, which otherwise reads the external
drive through the residual rather than through the optimiser.
\item \emph{The three statistics of \cref{sec:estimator} are reported on every call}, and a
result in which one of them performs comparably is reported as such.
\item \emph{Excitation bandwidth is matched across ranks}, or a construction in which each
added direction also adds a higher frequency lets the roughness ratio alone order the rank and
the experiment distinguishes nothing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Evaluation on systems with a known active dimension}\label{sec:ladder}

The estimator is evaluated on five systems of increasing realism, from a prescribed oscillation
with no optimiser to a network trained on image data. In each, $r$ is fixed by construction and
then verified by \cref{eq:act}, and the estimator sees only a scalar series
(\cref{tab:ladder}).

\subsection{An oscillating diagonal matrix}\label{sec:matrix}

The first system has no optimiser and no feedback, which makes a negative result decisive and a
positive one weak. Let $\mW(t) = \operatorname{diag}(b_i + \delta_i(t))$ with $b_i \ne 0$ and
exactly $r$ coordinates oscillating at rationally independent frequencies inside one octave.
The orbit closure is an $r$-torus, so the active dimension is exactly $r$; the offsets must be
non-zero or the squared Frobenius norm is not injective on it. One configuration was frozen on
a single seed, and two further seeds and a non-monotone schedule of ranks were withheld.
Recovery on the withheld data is accurate to within half a component up to ten directions and
saturates above about nine when the range is extended to twenty.

This system also supplies the control that decides whether the estimate reflects geometry at
all. Driving $r$ coordinates from one phase with distinct fixed offsets gives $r$ visibly
moving coordinates and a single degree of freedom; the estimate separates that case from four
independent oscillators by a factor of $3.4$, where the roughness ratio separates them by
$5\,\%$. That is the clearest evidence available that the geometry does work smoothness cannot.
On the same data LB carries a bias of $+1.02$ against $-0.08$ for MG, which is the
$(m-1)/(m-2)$ correction of \cref{sec:estimator} and nothing else.

\subsection{Online linear and logistic regression}\label{sec:regression}

The second and third systems introduce an optimiser. A $D = 64$ linear model, and then a
logistic model trained with binary cross-entropy, are fitted by full-batch gradient descent on
one-hot inputs, with exactly $r$ target coordinates moving quasiperiodically and the rest
constant and already fitted, so the active dynamics have dimension $r$. The best observers, the
parameter Frobenius norm in the linear model and the output probability norm in the logistic
one, both order the rank almost perfectly to twenty and both saturate above about twelve
directions, which places the saturation point of \cref{sec:matrix} in a system with feedback
and a nonlinear link. Their configuration was selected in breach of requirement~2, so the
errors in \cref{tab:ladder} locate that point but do not measure recovery.

\subsection{A frozen nonlinear decoder and a constrained perceptron}\label{sec:decoder}

The fourth and fifth systems add nonlinearity and backpropagation: a trainable $\vz \in \R^r$
optimised through a frozen nonlinear decoder whose linear skip connection keeps the map full
rank, and a two-layer perceptron of $P = 243$ parameters restricted to
$\vtheta = \vtheta_0 + \mU \vz$ with $\mU$ orthonormal and only $\vz$ trained, so that it has
hundreds of parameters and exactly $r$ available directions.

Read naively both succeed, and the numerical rank of the map from $\vz$ to the predictions
equals $r$ in every withheld run. Both nevertheless fail requirement~4: with the learning rate
set to zero, so that no training occurs, the perceptron experiment reproduces its reported
result almost exactly, the two series correlating above $0.98$ at every rank. The drive acts on
the targets and therefore reaches the observers through the residual, without passing through
the optimiser. \Cref{sec:digits} rebuilds the design with the drive acting on the loss weights
instead, keeping the nonlinearity and the backpropagation that motivate these two systems and
passing the control they fail.

\subsection{A network trained on image data}\label{sec:digits}

The fifth system is the parameterisation of \cref{sec:decoder} on real inputs. A tanh perceptron
$64 \to 96 \to 96$ is trained on the sklearn digits dataset and frozen, and a linear head on
top of it is confined to a $k$-dimensional affine subspace
$\vtheta = \vtheta_0 + \mV^{\top}\vc$. The loss is softmax cross-entropy, so the curvature is
data-dependent and anisotropic; all dynamics occur in $\vc$ and the logs are scalar functions
of it. The excitation is made $r$-dimensional rather than nominally so by modulating
the loss weights of $r$ of twelve fixed data groups with rationally independent sinusoids and
orthogonalising the resulting gradient directions (Appendix~\ref{app:constructions}), after
which the measured active dimension equals $r$ to within $0.008$ components over the whole
range. Six excitation regimes are run: two deterministic and recurrent, at a fast and a slow
drive period; injected rank-$r$ gradient noise; real mini-batch noise projected to rank $r$;
plain mini-batch descent; and a decaying transient. The last two are results rather than
settings, since the active dimension of a transient is one whatever $r$ is, and the noise rank
of ordinary mini-batch descent is fixed by the gradient covariance of the data.

\paragraph{Recovery.} Twelve observers were used, in five families: loss, norms, gradient,
fixed random projections of the parameters, and function-space quantities. The estimator's five
parameters were selected once, on seeds and ranks reserved for that purpose, and frozen. On the
withheld ranks and seeds, over the ten usable state-only observers, the mean absolute error in
the deterministic recurrent regime is $0.87$ components taken run by run, and a median of
$0.47$ when the four seeds at each rank are averaged first, at a median slope of $0.92$ and a
perfect rank correlation for every one of the ten. Errors by observer are in Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}; the
parameter norms, which are what a run ordinarily logs, sit in the middle of the range, and are
also the two observers whose roughness is not flat against the rank, so part of their ordering
may be spectral rather than geometric. The instantaneous mini-batch loss
is excluded from every aggregate despite scoring among the best three, because it alone fails
requirement~4: frozen at zero learning rate, with the measured active dimension identically one
at every rank, its estimate still rises with the nominal rank, since it contains the loss
weights and therefore the drive.

\paragraph{Twenty directions.} Repeating the design with twenty available directions gives the
same picture with a lower ceiling: recovery is close to exact to about six directions and then
saturates, reaching $15.1$ at a true $20.0$, while the ordering survives throughout at Spearman
$+0.97$. Above about eight directions the spread across seeds is comparable to the error, so
the estimate is an ordinal score rather than a count, and that configuration was scored on the
seeds it was selected on, so its split satisfies requirement~3 in rank only.
Appendix~\ref{app:nulls} gives the per-rank values and the two comparisons that matter: MG is
behind the uncorrected average LB at both ranges, and its advantage over
$\PR_{\mathrm{delay}}$ is a factor of about two at eight directions and about $1.4$ at
twenty. A second construction, defining the subspace in function space instead
(Appendix~\ref{app:constructions}), is the most accurate row of \cref{tab:ladder}, but its rank
is imposed by an external teacher.

\section{Conditions of validity}\label{sec:validity}

\subsection{Dynamical regime}\label{sec:regime}

\Cref{fig:regimes} evaluates the estimator in each regime of \cref{tab:classes} at the same
nominal rank, observer and matched octave. Under stochastic excitation it is flat in the rank,
responding to $E_{\max}$, the correlation time and the record length rather than to the truth,
and the error exceeds ten components. Weak noise added to a recurrent trajectory destroys the
absolute value while leaving the ordering: at a quarter of the pure noise amplitude, injected
in the same $r$ directions and leaving the measured active dimension exactly $r$, the error
rises tenfold while the Spearman correlation stays above $+0.96$. A training log is a weak
deterministic signal inside stochastic gradient fluctuation, so that is the operative regime in
\cref{sec:grokking}. A decaying transient returns a large reproducible estimate above $27$
components where the truth is one, and is the one regime in which $\rho_{\mathrm{ident}}$ does
not fire.

\subsection{The delay lag}\label{sec:tau}

The delay window spans $(E_{\max}-1)\tau$ samples and must cover a real fraction of the
oscillation period, or the delay coordinates are collinear and the torus does not unfold. On a
fixed torus of period $400$ at eight active directions the estimate reads $2.60$ when the
window spans a twentieth of a period, $8.53$ at four tenths and $63.35$ at one and a half: with
the system unchanged, $\tau$ alone moves it by more than sixty components. The correct lag is a
function of the oscillation period, which a controlled experiment knows and a training log does
not; \cref{sec:grokking} estimates it from the autocorrelation time.

\subsection{Nuisance factors}\label{sec:nuisance}


At a fixed active dimension of four we vary seven factors that leave it unchanged, over five
seeds and twelve observers, and compare the shift against a real four-component change; the
seed-to-seed variability of an unchanged configuration is $0.21$ components, a twentieth of
that reference (Appendix~\ref{app:validity}). The smoothness control is the decisive one and it
passes: moving the drive band by an octave changes the autocorrelation time and the roughness
fourfold, yet moves the estimate only to the level of the baseline. A constant rescaling of the
observer leaves it exactly invariant, verified to machine precision at twenty directions; a
\emph{ramped} gain does not, and moves it by about a third of a real four-component change. A
run whose parameter norm grows can therefore drift by order one component with nothing
dimensional occurring, which is the nuisance factor that matters most in
\cref{sec:grokking}.

\subsection{Validation of the diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostics-validated}

Using no ground truth, the identifiability ratio separates the regimes in which the estimate is
accurate, where it lies between $1.00$ and $1.09$, from those in which it is not, where it lies
between $1.32$ and $1.57$; under the slow drive it separates within a regime too, reading
$0.99$ where the estimate is correct and $1.52$ where it has saturated. It is not sufficient
alone, since it reads $1.00$ on the transient regime, and must be paired with the recurrence
count, which is near zero there. The degeneracy flag fires on eleven of the thirty-five
run-by-column cells of the logs of \cref{sec:grokking}.

\section{Application to grokking}\label{sec:grokking}

Grokking \citep{power2022grokking} is studied in two experimental settings that differ in every
relevant respect, and we reproduce both (every run is listed in Appendix~\ref{app:runs}): the
regularised stochastic one of
\citet{liu2022omnigrok}, a one-layer transformer trained on modular addition and on composition
in $S_5$ with AdamW, weight decay $1.0$, cross-entropy and mini-batches; and the unregularised
full-batch one of \citet{gromov2023grokking} and \citet{doshi2024grokking}, a two-layer
perceptron with quadratic activation trained on mean squared error. The second supplies
label-matched pairs of targets differing by one monomial, of which one generalises and the
other does not while both fit the training set completely (Appendix~\ref{app:repr}).

\subsection{Separation of label-matched pairs}\label{sec:pairs}

The perceptron setting supplies controls the first cannot construct: both members of a pair
reach full training accuracy under identical optimisation on identical data budgets and differ
by one monomial in the target, so anything separating their logs is a property of the solution
found. On the training loss the estimate is about three units higher for the non-generalising
member in all four pairs, with no overlap, from a single scalar log (Appendix~\ref{app:pairs}).
It is not, however, a dimension: \cref{tab:grok-diagnostics} places all six runs in the
transient regime, where the estimate follows the shape of the curve rather than any attractor,
and the mechanism is in the standard log, since the representable targets drive the training
error to zero while the perturbed ones stop three orders of magnitude short despite full
training accuracy, fitting the argmax but not the regression.

\subsection{Direct measurement of the active dimension}\label{sec:direct}

\begin{figure}[t]

\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_dip.pdf}

The active dimension of \cref{eq:act} needs no delay reconstruction when the trajectory itself
can be stored. We store a sketch of it, in parameter space and in function space, at every
logged step of a transformer trained to grok (Appendix~\ref{app:sketch}). At generalisation the
trajectory passes through a transient one-dimensional bottleneck
(\cref{fig:dip}): in function space the participation ratio falls by a factor of five to twelve,
in parameter space to between $1.09$ and $1.60$ some six hundred to a thousand steps later, and
then re-expands well above its plateau. Three observations support the reading: it sits at
generalisation in seeds whose generalisation step differs by a factor of $3.7$ and in a second
task, so it is not a function of training progress; the displacement falls as well but reaches
its floor three thousand steps later and never recovers while the rank does, so it is not the
trajectory coming to rest; and the runs without weight decay do not show it.

The same measurement on the perceptron setting returns nothing, for a reason that is a
resolution limit rather than a result: over a $600$-step window a deterministic trajectory is
straight to within the measurement precision, so the statistic is pinned near unity. Lengthening
the window to $12\,000$ steps gives it range, and what appears is a maximum at memorisation that
the non-generalising control shares (Appendix~\ref{app:fb}).

\subsection{What falls, and when}\label{sec:whatfalls}

A fall of the estimate is not specific to generalisation. In a $200\,000$-step run at $p = 211$
with no weight decay, which memorises at step $2\,250$, ends at $3.6\,\%$ validation accuracy
and grows its parameter norm from $43$ to $126$, a fall is detected at step $49\,250$, for
which the amplitude control of \cref{sec:nuisance} gives the mechanism. Nor is the absence of
generalisation within a budget evidence that a fall was a false alarm: two reduced-data
configurations against which earlier specificity figures were computed generalise once the
budget is extended, at steps $39\,600$ and $110\,940$. The picture is consistent with
\cref{sec:direct}: the trajectory simplifies sharply and transiently at generalisation and also
simplifies elsewhere, so a fall is evidence of a change of regime rather than of
generalisation.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

One scalar log of a suitable observer recovers the number of directions an optimiser excites
within a window to under one component up to eight directions, and orders it to twenty. What
makes that number usable is not the estimator but the diagnostics beside it, which decide
without a ground truth whether it counts components; applied to grokking they place both
standard settings outside the admissible regime. Measured directly, the trajectory simplifies
sharply at generalisation and also in runs that never generalise. Three limits bound this: the
absolute value requires the deterministic recurrent regime and a lag matched to the oscillation
period, it is ordinal rather than a count above eight directions, and the measurement of
\cref{sec:direct} is a signature at generalisation rather than an early warning, and is not
reproduced in the second setting.

\section{The estimator}\label{app:alg}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Pooled Levina--Bickel intrinsic dimension of a delay reconstruction}
\label{alg:mg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require series $x_{1:n}$; lag $\tau$; embedding dimension $E$; neighbours $m$; Theiler window $W$
\State $z \gets (x - \operatorname{mean} x) / \operatorname{std} x$
\State $\vy_t \gets (z_t, z_{t-\tau}, \dots, z_{t-(E-1)\tau})$ for $t = (E-1)\tau+1, \dots, n$
\State $S \gets 0$, $N \gets 0$
\For{each $\vy_i$}
  \State $r_1 \le \dots \le r_m \gets$ distances to the $m$ nearest $\vy_j$ with $|i-j| > W$
  \If{$r_1 < \varepsilon_{\mathrm{dist}}$ \textbf{or} $\sum_j \log(r_m/r_j) < \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ratio}}$}
    \State mark window degenerate; \textbf{return} undefined
  \EndIf
  \State $S \gets S + \sum_{j=1}^{m-1} \log(r_m / r_j)$; \quad $N \gets N + 1$
\EndFor
\State \Return $N(m-1) / S$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The Theiler window $W$ is set from the autocorrelation time of the series and capped. No
clamping of the returned value to $E$ is applied: a divergent estimate is reported as
divergent, since clamping converts the transient regime of \cref{tab:classes} into a
plausible number.

\emph{Both sit at a grid boundary.} The eight-direction configuration is at the top of all
five swept axes and the twenty-direction one at the top of both. That is less alarming than it
sounds for the first, since four of its five axes have only two levels, so every configuration
in that grid is at a boundary in four of them; $\tau$ is the only axis with an interior value,
and the winner is at its top end. It does mean neither configuration is interior, and that a
wider grid could move the level.

\emph{The selection error spans a factor of five on identical data.} Across the
eight-direction grid the error runs from $0.324$ to $1.499$ on the same logs, so the level is a
choice as much as a measurement, and only the withheld numbers of \cref{sec:digits} may be read
as recovery.

\emph{The Theiler axis is inert on this data.} The autocorrelation rule returns the larger of
the embedding span $(E_{\max}-1)\tau$ and the measured autocorrelation time. On these logs the
autocorrelation time is one to three samples while the embedding span is nine or more, so the
two settings return bit-identical values at all $24$ points of the grid where they are
compared. The $48$-point grid therefore contains $24$ distinct measurements, and the choice of
Theiler rule is not a choice at all here.

\emph{The twenty-direction configuration does not use the window its parameters name.} At
$E_{\max}=40$ and $\tau=16$ the requested exclusion is $624$ samples, which the implementation
truncates to its cap of $150$ for the cost reason given in Appendix~\ref{app:alg}. The
eight-direction configuration is uncapped.

\paragraph{Detecting a change.} Recovery of a static level does not establish that a change is detected. Running three
consecutive segments $r_{\mathrm{high}} \to r_{\mathrm{low}} \to r_{\mathrm{high}}$ with
nothing else altered, and re-measuring the ground truth on each segment, a true fall of $3.99$
components is observed as $3.95$ in the deterministic recurrent regime, is detected by every
usable observer in both directions, and recovers to the level before it to within $0.01$
components. Under stochastic excitation the same true change is observed as $0.15$.

The rule fires when the estimate falls below the median of the pre-switch segment by three
times that segment's window-to-window scatter, for two consecutive windows. The threshold uses
the
pre-switch segment alone, never the midpoint of the two observed levels, which would hand the
detector the answer and would decide the arm where the two levels coincide by a coin flip. On
the parameter norm the level pairs read $4.77 \to 1.22 \to 4.78$ for a true $4 \to 1 \to 4$ and
$6.90 \to 2.12 \to 6.91$ for a true $6 \to 2 \to 6$; at $8 \to 3 \to 8$ they read
$6.97 \to 3.64 \to 6.97$, under-reporting the high level by a component, which is the
saturation of \cref{tab:k20} seen from another direction. The level error is $0.89$ at the high
level and $0.22$ at the low one: the estimate is more accurate the fewer directions are active.

The response time cannot be quoted from this experiment. With a window of $8\,000$ samples and
a stride of $2\,000$ the two observed lags, $999$ and $1\,999$ steps, are the two floors of the
measurement grid rather than measured values. What the experiment does establish is that the
estimate crosses the threshold on a window still containing $87.5\,\%$ pre-switch data.

\section{System constructions}\label{app:constructions}

\paragraph{The image-data system of \cref{sec:digits}.} The data is partitioned into twelve
fixed groups, the same partition at every rank so that the partition is not itself a function
of $r$, and the loss weights of $r$ of them are modulated by $r$ rationally independent
sinusoids filling one octave. Modulating group $j$ tilts the gradient along a direction
$\vphi_j$. These directions are neither orthogonal nor of equal effect, since random data
groups have correlated gradients, so a mixing matrix $\mathrm{pinv}(\Phi) Q$ with $Q$ an
orthonormal basis of $\operatorname{range}(\Phi)$ orthogonalises them, and the per-mode scalar
response gain of the linearised update is divided out. Without this correction the trajectory
participation ratio falls well below $r$.

\Cref{tab:gt} verifies that the construction achieves what it intends, and two of its rows are
results rather than settings. The active dimension of a decaying transient is one whatever $r$
is, since a converging trajectory is a curve; and plain mini-batch descent sits at $6.2$
regardless of $r$, because its noise rank is fixed by the per-example gradient covariance of
the data and is not a free parameter of the experiment. Projecting real mini-batch noise onto
$r$ directions recovers only part of the intended rank, reaching $5.6$ at $r=8$, which is why
that regime is scored against its measured value and not against $r$.

The last row is the control of requirement~4. With the learning rate multiplied by zero the
active dimension is identically one at every nominal rank, so an observer whose estimate still
tracks $r$ there is reading the drive rather than the optimiser.

The functional rank is $10$ at every rank, seed and regime, with a participation ratio between
$8.40$ and $9.03$ across the four seeds; it is a property of the frozen backbone and does not
depend on the excitation. The condition number of the drive-direction matrix is $1.00$ at
$r=1$ by construction and has a median of $13.3$ at $r=8$, which is the anisotropy the mixing
matrix exists to remove.

\paragraph{The function-space construction.} A local adapter is built around a trained
$64 \to 16 \to 10$ network and its function-space Jacobian is QR-whitened, so that its first
$r$ columns have rank $r$ at equal local scale. A quasiperiodic teacher excites every adapter
coordinate and the student tracks it by exact backpropagation through both layers. The
functional, trajectory-covariance and update-covariance ranks were all verified to equal $r$.

\paragraph{The twenty-direction system.} The same construction at twenty available directions
reproduces the nominal rank to within $0.008$ components over $r = 1, \dots, 20$ in the
recurrent regime, and to three decimals up to $r = 10$. The other regimes behave as at ten
directions: injected noise tracks $r$ closely, projected mini-batch noise reaches only $9.4$
at $r = 20$, and the decaying transient stays between $1.02$ and $1.09$ throughout.

\section{The trajectory sketch}\label{app:sketch}

The direct measurement of \cref{sec:direct} needs the trajectory itself, which for the
transformer is $226\,816$ doubles at each of two to three thousand logged steps. Every logged
step is therefore compressed by a CountSketch \citep{charikar2002finding} to $\R^{1024}$, with
two independent hash families, which preserves the Gram matrix and so the covariance spectrum
to $O(\sqrt{\log T / D})$, about nine per cent here. Two spaces are recorded: the parameters,
and the probe logits after centring each example over classes and normalising to unit length.
The normalisation is not cosmetic. The raw logit scale is mechanically coupled to weight decay,
so a signal read off it could not be distinguished from a detector of weight decay.

Three checks stand behind the numbers. Pushing synthetic trajectories of known rank through
the same sketch and the same participation ratio returns $1.00$, $1.98$, $4.46$ and $8.63$ for
true ranks $1$, $2$, $5$ and $10$, so the compression does not manufacture or destroy rank in
the range of interest. On a real run of the perceptron architecture, where the parameter count
is small enough to allow it, the participation ratio computed exactly on all $145\,500$ raw
parameters agrees with the sketched value to three decimals. And the observer is verified not
to perturb training by requiring the log to be bit-identical with and without it, which matters
because the task module seeds one global random stream that the train--validation split, the
initialisation and the mini-batch order all continue: a single stray draw changes the initial
weights and can destroy grokking.

Windows are sixty logged rows, which is $600$ optimiser steps for the modular runs and $300$
for $S_5$, and are labelled by their centre so that the position of a feature is not an
artefact of the labelling. Four statistics are computed per window in each space: the
participation ratio of the positions after removing a linear trend, since a steady drift is
rank one and would otherwise mask everything else; the participation ratio of the increments
block-averaged over five logged steps, to suppress the mini-batch noise floor; and the total
displacement, which is the null for a rank that falls simply because the trajectory stopped
moving.

\section{The full-batch setting}\label{app:fb}

Repeating the direct measurement on the perceptron setting gives a result that must be read as
a limit of the measurement rather than as a negative. Under full-batch gradient descent every
window of every run has a participation ratio between $1.000$ and $1.362$. This is not an
artefact of the sketch: computed exactly on all $145\,500$ raw parameters the value is the
same to three decimals, and the sketch recovers synthetic trajectories of known rank up to
ten. A deterministic update makes the trajectory a smooth curve, and over a $600$-step window
a smooth curve is straight to within the measurement precision, so the statistic reports the
window length rather than the dynamics. It has no range in which a collapse could appear, and
the absence of a feature is therefore not evidence of its absence: the window must be set in
units of the curvature scale of the trajectory.
Repeating the measurement against window length confirms the diagnosis and withdraws the
opening it appeared to leave. Two runs of $150\,000$ steps, a generalising one and its
label-matched control, are measured over windows spanning $600$ to $120\,000$ optimiser
steps, with the window lengthened by spreading sixty samples further apart rather than by
adding samples, so that the bound on the statistic and its noise floor stay exactly where
the published measurement put them and only the span changes. \Cref{tab:prwindow} gives
the result. The statistic does leave the floor, at a scale of about $10^4$ steps, two
orders of magnitude above the window that produced the $1.000$--$1.362$: the absence of a
feature there was a resolution limit and not a fact about the run.

It is not, however, a hidden signal. What appears at $12\,000$ steps is one localised
maximum, and both runs have one, of the same size and in the same place: $1.877$ centred
at step $12\,800$ for the run that memorises at $9\,200$ and generalises at $11\,950$, and
$1.843$ centred at $9\,350$ for the control, which memorises at $9\,800$ and never
generalises. The feature tracks memorisation, which both runs do. Function space agrees
with more range and no more separation, $2.618$ against $2.145$. Beyond $30\,000$ steps
the ordering reverses and the control moves further than the generalising run, because the
window is then a large fraction of a run whose weight norm is still climbing and the
statistic is reading that drift. The full-batch trajectory therefore carries no rank
signature of generalisation at any window length between $600$ and $120\,000$ steps, which
is the stronger form of the same conclusion: the collapse of \cref{sec:direct} belongs to
the regularised stochastic setting, and a trajectory participation ratio must be controlled
for window length as well as for batch size before anything is read from it.

\section{The representation dimension in closed form}\label{app:repr}

The perceptron setting of \cref{sec:grokking} gives the dimension of the learned representation
a closed form, since the generalising solution uses $(p+1)/2$ Fourier modes and nothing else.
The order parameter is the mean inverse participation ratio of the first layer's Fourier
spectrum, which is $1.000$ for that solution because each neuron carries a single frequency,
and about $0.041$ at random initialisation. Two things follow from measuring it, and both
qualify readings of the representation dimension elsewhere.

\emph{The representation is not complete at the transition.} For modular addition the order
parameter reads $0.067$ just after memorisation at step $9\,170$, $0.189$ just before
generalisation at step $11\,950$, $0.263$ when validation accuracy first reaches $100\,\%$, and
then rises for a further $87\,000$ steps to $0.595$ against an analytic $1.000$. It does not
jump at the transition; it begins rising at memorisation and passes smoothly through.
Generalisation therefore arrives well before the representation is complete, and requires it
only to be periodic enough. The effective rank of the same layer is far weaker as a signal: it
spans $132.6$ to $150.9$ over the run against an analytic $148.8$, is not monotone, and
overshoots the reference before falling back, so landing near the reference is not evidence.

\emph{The order parameter is only interpretable against its own reference.} \Cref{tab:ipr}
gives the measured value beside each task's own closed form. Two of the tasks that generalise
have a reference near the floor, because the representation is periodic in $g_1(n)$ while the
spectrum is taken over the raw index $n$, and $g_1$ is not linear there. Read against modular
addition's $1.000$, a measured $0.044$ says nothing was learned; read against that task's own
$0.062$, it says the run has essentially converged. The tasks that never generalise sit at the
initialisation floor, and the analysis returns no reference for them rather than substituting
another task's.
