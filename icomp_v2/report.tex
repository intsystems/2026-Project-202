\begin{abstract}
Over a window of training, a neural network's parameter trajectory moves on a set far smaller
than the parameter space. We call the dimension of that set the active dimension of the window: a
number of independent components, distinct from the number of directions the optimiser may move
in, from the rank of the map to the outputs, and from the effective rank of the trajectory
covariance. A stored trajectory measures it directly but is expensive, where a scalar log costs
nothing, so we ask what survives the projection onto one. The instrument is standard, delay reconstruction
followed by a maximum likelihood intrinsic dimension, and its hazard is that it returns a
plausible number when no invariant set exists. On six constructed systems, ending with a linear
head on a frozen image classifier, it recovers that number on withheld ranks and seeds to within
about one component, up to a ceiling near eight directions; two diagnostics computed from the
series alone identify the regimes in which its value may not be read as the active dimension.
Applied to grokking they place both standard settings outside the admissible regime. Paying instead
for the trajectory, the effective rank collapses near generalisation in four regularised runs and
then re-expands, the simplification the mechanistic accounts of grokking lead one to expect; their
controls decline too, so what marks the transition is not its depth but that it is sharp,
locked to the transition and reversed. Re-run at a window matched to that transition rather than to
the record, the log estimate drops at the same step in the same four runs and in neither control, by
more than surrogates preserving the observer's shape produce: two signatures of one event, the
second already written by the run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A neural network is trained in a parameter space of dimension $P$, but the trajectory the optimiser
follows does not fill it. Over a window it moves on a much smaller set, and that set grows and
shrinks over a run. We call its dimension the \emph{active dimension} of the window
(\cref{eq:act}). It is a count of independent components: if the optimiser is carried by $r$
independent quasiperiodic phases the orbit closes onto an $r$-torus and the active dimension is
$r$, with no tunable scale. It is not the dimension of the subspace the optimiser is confined to,
which the parameterisation fixes \citep{li2018measuring}. It is not the rank of the map from that
subspace to the outputs. And it is not the effective rank of the trajectory covariance, which
anisotropy depresses below it. \Cref{sec:three} separates the four.

Knowing it would settle questions currently argued with whichever of those four quantities is
least expensive to compute: whether an apparent simplification of training is a real reduction in
degrees of freedom or an artefact of a shrinking scale, and whether a phase transition can be seen
without instrumenting the run. Measuring it directly means storing the trajectory, which is
expensive. A scalar log costs nothing, and a run already keeps several, so we ask how much of the
active dimension survives the projection onto one.

The instrument is standard: delay reconstruction \citep{takens1981detecting, sauer1991embedology}
followed by the pooled Levina--Bickel estimator \citep{levina2004maximum, mackay2005comments}. Its
hazard is that it returns a plausible number when no invariant set exists. Power-law noise yields a
finite reproducible dimension set by its spectral exponent \citep{osborne1989finite}, and a
decaying transient, which is what a converging run looks like, yields a large one. Most of the work
below therefore goes into deciding which case one has, beginning with a validation protocol fixed
before any result (\cref{sec:protocol}).

\paragraph{Contributions.} We separate four quantities that are frequently identified with one
another --- the dimension of the subspace the optimiser may move in, the rank of the map from it to
the outputs, the number of independent components of the set it visits, and the effective rank of
its covariance --- and estimate only the third, measuring the fourth beside it everywhere so that
the two cannot be conflated (\cref{sec:three}). We evaluate that estimate on six systems of
increasing realism, ending with a constrained head on a network trained on image data: with no
per-system calibration it recovers the active dimension on withheld ranks and seeds to within about
one component, up to a ceiling we then locate (\cref{sec:ladder}). We measure what the absolute
value requires, and validate two diagnostics that identify the two failure regimes from the series
alone (\cref{sec:validity}). Finally we apply all of it to grokking (\cref{sec:grokking}): the
diagnostics refuse both published settings, the trajectory effective rank collapses at
generalisation and re-expands, and at a window matched to that transition the estimate on an
ordinary log follows it down, in the generalising runs and in neither control (\cref{sec:matched}).

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reconstruction and intrinsic dimension.} Delay reconstruction rests on Takens' theorem
and its extensions \citep{takens1981detecting, sauer1991embedology, stark1999delay,
stark2003delay}, is used on empirical series \citep{sugihara1990nonlinear, sugihara2012detecting},
and takes its lag and dimension from mutual information, false nearest neighbours or singular
spectra \citep{fraser1986independent, kennel1992determining, cao1997practical,
broomhead1986extracting}. We use pooled maximum likelihood \citep{levina2004maximum,
mackay2005comments}, not the alternatives \citep{grassberger1983measuring, facco2017estimating,
camastra2016intrinsic}; a finite record bounds them all \citep{eckmann1992fundamental}.
Oversampling \citep{theiler1986spurious} and power-law noise \citep{osborne1989finite} produce
spurious dimensions; surrogate tests \citep{theiler1992testing, schreiber1996improved} detect
nonlinear structure but not its amount, missing the transient regime entirely.

\paragraph{Dimension in optimisation.} Random-subspace training \citep{li2018measuring,
aghajanyan2021intrinsic} reports the smallest $k$ that still solves a task, a property of the task
and not of a window; we borrow the parameterisation and measure something else inside it. Nearest
to the active dimension is the finding that descent occurs largely in a small subspace spanned by
the top Hessian eigenvectors \citep{gurari2018gradient, sagun2018empirical}. The intrinsic
dimension of data or of representations \citep{ansuini2019intrinsic, pope2021intrinsic} is a point
cloud's, not a trajectory's, and the participation ratio we adopt \citep{gao2017theory} is not the
spectral-entropy quantity of the same name \citep{roy2007effective}. The one prior measurement of
our object we know of is incidental: nearly all of a grokking trajectory's variance lies in two
principal components \citep{notsawo2023predicting}. All of these need weights, gradients or
activations; the nearest precedent for a scalar computed during training and acted on is the
gradient noise scale \citep{mccandlish2018empirical}. Mini-batch descent near a minimum is modelled
as a stochastic process \citep{mandt2017stochastic}, our third regime; whether the edge of
stability \citep{cohen2021gradient} supplies the recurrence the first regime needs, we measure
(\cref{app:eos}).

\paragraph{Grokking.} Delayed generalisation \citep{power2022grokking} is studied in two settings
and we use both. The first is regularised and, in our version, stochastic: the transformer
configuration of \citet{nanda2023progress}, also used by \citet{liu2022omnigrok}, on modular
addition and on the $S_5$ task of \citet{power2022grokking}. The second is the unregularised
full-batch gradient descent of \citet{gromov2023grokking}, whose closed form supplies an analytic
reference, on the modular polynomials and representability criterion of \citet{doshi2024grokking},
which supply label-matched pairs; \cref{app:runs} states where each departs from its source.
Explanations are framed in weight or activation space \citep{nanda2023progress, varma2023explaining,
liu2022towards, barak2022hidden} or in a lazy-to-rich transition \citep{kumar2024grokking}.
Closest to us, \citet{notsawo2023predicting} predict grokking from early loss oscillations
\citep{thilak2022slingshot} and \citet{merrill2023tale} track competing subnetworks. We estimate a
dynamical quantity with an independently measured ground truth.

\section{Problem statement}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Four quantities, and the one we estimate}\label{sec:three}

A \emph{window} $\gW$ is a block of $L$ consecutive optimiser steps. Everything below is defined on
one window, and a run is described by a sequence of them, because what the optimiser is doing
changes over training. Let $\vtheta_t \in \R^P$ be the parameter vector at step $t$, confined by
construction or by the dynamics to an affine subspace $\vtheta = \vtheta_0 + \mV^{\top}\vc$ with
$\mV \in \R^{k \times P}$ orthonormal, so that $\vc_t \in \R^k$ holds the coordinates that can
move. Three dimensions attach to $\gW$, and they are different numbers.

The \emph{available} dimension is $k$: how many directions the parameterisation permits. The
\emph{functional} dimension is the rank of $\partial \vf(\vc)/\partial \vc$, with $\vf$ the outputs
on a fixed probe set; it bounds how many of those $k$ directions can change the computed function,
and we report it rather than estimate it. The \emph{active} dimension is the subject of this paper:
how many independent directions the optimiser actually explores inside the window. Let
$\gM_{\gW} = \{\vc_t : t \in \gW\}$ be the set of points the trajectory visits there, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:act}
  d_{\mathrm{act}}(\gW) \;=\; \dim\nolimits_{\varepsilon} \gM_{\gW}
\end{equation}
its box-counting dimension at resolution $\varepsilon$: cover $\gM_{\gW}$ with boxes of side
$\varepsilon$, and $d_{\mathrm{act}}$ is the exponent $d$ for which the number needed grows like
$\varepsilon^{-d}$, so that a curve needs $\varepsilon^{-1}$ boxes and a surface $\varepsilon^{-2}$.

Two features of that definition are deliberate. First, \emph{$\varepsilon$ stays finite} where the
textbook definition would let it go to zero. It has to: the estimator only ever sees the set
through distances between nearby samples, so what it can report is the dimension at the scale of
its own neighbourhoods. Second, \emph{the window must be long}: any orbit looks like a curve over a
short enough stretch, so $d_{\mathrm{act}} = 1$ whatever the motion unless $\gW$ outlasts the time
the trajectory takes to return near itself.

The case the validation systems build is $\vc_t = F(\varphi_1(t), \dots, \varphi_r(t))$, with $r$
independent quasiperiodic phases: the orbit closes onto an $r$-torus and $d_{\mathrm{act}} = r$ at
every $\varepsilon$ below the weakest mode's amplitude. Above that scale the weakest modes are
invisible and $d_{\mathrm{act}}$ declines, which is a fact about the set at that resolution rather
than an error (\cref{sec:aniso}).

A fourth quantity is measured throughout and is not the active dimension. For a non-negative
spectrum $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ the participation ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pr}
  \PR(\lambda) \;=\; \frac{\bigl(\sum_i \lambda_i\bigr)^2}{\sum_i \lambda_i^2}
\end{equation}
equals $m$ exactly when $m$ of the $\lambda_i$ are equal and the rest vanish. On
$\operatorname{spec} \Cov\{\vc_t : t \in \gW\}$ it gives the \emph{effective rank}
$\PR^{\mathrm{pos}}(\gW)$, which is what that term means below, with $\PR^{\mathrm{det}}$ the same
after the per-window linear detrend a drifting trajectory needs and $\PR^{\mathrm{upd}}$ the same
on the increments. It is not that number: it equals $r$ when $r$ directions carry comparable variance
and falls below $r$ when they do not, while $d_{\mathrm{act}}$ stays at $r$ (\cref{sec:aniso}). We
use it to check that a construction excites the $r$ directions it claims, and \cref{sec:direct}
measures it on a real run. That the four come apart is not hypothetical: in \cref{sec:digits} an
intervention that leaves the available and functional dimensions and the injected rank untouched
still lowers the effective rank (\cref{tab:gt}).

\subsection{Observation model and estimator}\label{sec:estimator}

We observe a scalar series $x_t = \phi(\vs_t)$, with $\vs_t$ the full optimiser state and $\phi$
fixed before the run, and reconstruct $\vy_t = (x_t, \dots, x_{t-(E-1)\tau}) \in \R^E$. For a
generic pair of dynamics and observation on a compact invariant manifold of integer dimension $d$
this map is an embedding once $E > 2d$ \citep{takens1981detecting}; the prevalence form replaces
$d$ by the box-counting dimension, which need not be an integer, and adds conditions on periodic
orbits \citep{sauer1991embedology}. Genericity is not verifiable for a $\phi$ fixed in advance, so
every result below is reported over a family of observers. We estimate the intrinsic dimension of
$\{\vy_t\}$ by maximum likelihood on nearest-neighbour distances \citep{levina2004maximum}. Let
$r^{(i)}_1 \le \dots \le r^{(i)}_m$ be the distances from point $i$ to its $m$ nearest neighbours,
subject to a Theiler exclusion $W_T$ on their separation in time \citep{theiler1986spurious,
kantz2004nonlinear}. Pooling the likelihood over the $N$ points before inverting, as
\citet{mackay2005comments} argue one should since the reciprocal is the unbiased quantity, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg}
  \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}^{\,-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{N(m-1)} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}
  \log \frac{r^{(i)}_m}{r^{(i)}_j} ,
\end{equation}
while averaging the per-point estimates instead, as \citet{levina2004maximum} propose, gives the
variant we write LB. We call what MG returns \emph{the estimate} and the procedure \emph{the
estimator}; \cref{tab:alts} reports both and \cref{app:alg} gives the algorithm as implemented.
Three further statistics accompany every estimate, each naming an effect that can produce a
dimension signal with no geometry: the \emph{roughness}
$\operatorname{std}(\Delta x)/\operatorname{std}(x)$, the \emph{linear participation ratio}
$\PR_{\mathrm{delay}}$ of \cref{eq:pr} on $\Cov\{\vy_t\}$, and the \emph{spectral participation
ratio} on the power spectrum of $x$.

\subsection{Dynamical regimes}\label{sec:classes}

Two conditions must hold, and they are not the same. The theorems require a deterministic flow on a
compact invariant set, which licenses the delay map as an embedding. The neighbour statistic needs
more: the record must visit that set densely enough for a point's nearest neighbours to be returns
rather than its own temporal neighbours, which the Theiler exclusion enforces and
\cref{sec:diagnostics} tests. Recurrence enters here and only here --- not as a requirement of the
estimator, which would accept points drawn from independent runs, but as one of a protocol whose
whole sample is a single trajectory. Neither condition is needed for $d_{\mathrm{act}}$ to be
the optimiser's; both are needed to read it off one scalar record. An optimiser near a solution may
be in any of the three regimes of \cref{tab:classes}, and only the first meets them.

The third regime is what mini-batch gradient noise produces, and theory serves it worse than it
looks. The embedding theorems for forced systems \citep{stark1999delay, stark2003delay} cover it
in part: the update may depend on the state arbitrarily, and their iterated-function-system form
admits a driver selecting among finitely many maps, as sampling a finite dataset does. But they
deliver an embedding \emph{fibre by fibre}, one per realisation of the drive, and a single run
crosses fibres, so no invariant measure exists there to sample. Coloured noise alone yields a
finite reproducible dimension set by its spectral exponent \citep{osborne1989finite}, or at least
an estimate that behaves like one \citep{theiler1991some}. The second regime is less often noticed:
a decaying transient traces a curve it never returns to, so it has no $\gM_{\gW}$, and its
effective rank remains near unity (\cref{tab:gt}) only because that curve is nearly straight.

\subsection{Admissibility diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostics}

Two statistics computed from the series alone separate the two failure regimes. The
\textbf{identifiability ratio}
$\rho_{\mathrm{ident}} = \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}(2E_{\max}) / \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}(E_{\max})$ is
near unity when a dimension is resolvable and grows when the estimate is a property of the
embedding space rather than of the data, which is the stochastic regime. On a transient
$\rho_{\mathrm{ident}} \approx 1$ as well, for the wrong reason, and the \textbf{trend-crossing
count} distinguishes the two: the number of sign changes of the window's residual about its
least-squares line, which is near zero there. It tests non-monotonicity of the series, not
recurrence in the reconstructed space, so the pair must be read together. A third statistic, the
\textbf{degeneracy indicator}, tests no regime: it is raised when too many neighbour distances or
log-ratio sums reach their numerical floors, and guards against quantised or constant observers
(\cref{app:alg}). Reference values are in \cref{sec:diagnostics-validated}.

\section{Validation protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

Six requirements were fixed before any result, and each is named so that \cref{tab:ladder} can say
which a system fails.

\emph{(1) Constructed truth.} Every system is built with $r$ independent quasiperiodic phases, so
$d_{\mathrm{act}} = r$ by construction, and the recorded trajectory's effective rank confirms that
all $r$ are comparably excited.

\emph{(2) Frozen configuration.} The estimator is chosen once, by a search over estimator settings
only. Searching the system's settings at the same time --- the drive period, say --- would choose
the data along with the method, and the winning cell would report how favourable a system we found
rather than how well the estimator works.

\emph{(3) Disjoint split}, in seed and in rank. Withholding seeds alone is not enough: whenever the
excitation geometry is a function of $r$, every seed at a given rank shares it.

\emph{(4) Silence control.} Re-run each system with the learning rate set to zero. The parameters
then never move, so an observer of the optimiser must be flat: a constant log is exactly the right
answer here. If instead the log keeps varying and returns the same estimate as before, the observer
was never reading the optimiser. That is what happens in two of our systems, where the drive acts
on the targets, so the loss continues to move through the residual while the parameters are fixed.

\emph{(5) Companion statistics.} The roughness, the linear participation ratio and the spectral
participation ratio of \cref{sec:estimator} are reported with every estimate, and where one of them
does as well as the estimator we say so.

\emph{(6) Matched bandwidth} across ranks. In a construction where each added direction also adds a
higher frequency, the roughness alone orders the rank, and no embedding is being tested.

\section{Evaluation on systems with a known active dimension}\label{sec:ladder}

Six systems of increasing realism are used, and \cref{tab:ladder} collects the outcome: an
oscillating diagonal matrix, online linear and logistic regression, a frozen nonlinear decoder, a
perceptron in a $k$-subspace, and a linear head on a network trained on image data. In each the
orbit closes onto an $r$-torus by construction, the effective rank confirms it, and the estimator
sees one scalar series.

\subsection{An oscillating diagonal matrix}\label{sec:matrix}

The first system has no optimiser and no feedback, so a negative result there would be decisive. In
$\mW(t) = \operatorname{diag}(b_i + \delta_i(t))$, $r$ coordinates oscillate at rationally
independent frequencies over non-zero offsets, and the estimate tracks $r$ across the whole range
on both held-out seeds (\cref{tab:ladder}). It also supplies the geometry control. Drive several coordinates from a single phase and the set they trace has one degree of
freedom rather than several, though it is no smoother for it; the estimate separates that case from
the same number of independent oscillators several-fold, while the roughness of the series
distinguishes them by a few per cent. Smoothness alone cannot produce that separation.

\subsection{The ceiling}\label{sec:ceiling}

The estimator saturates above about eight directions. Sweeping the embedding dimension against the
record length separates two things we had been treating as one (\cref{app:ceiling}). The
\emph{level} returned at high rank belongs to
the embedding: widening the delay window raises it, lengthening the record does not. The \emph{rank
up to which the estimate still tracks the truth} belongs to neither knob --- it never passes about
ten components, and reaches that at the \emph{shortest} record examined.

Both candidate explanations therefore fail: a finite-record bound \citep{eckmann1992fundamental}
would allow far fewer components than we recover, and $E > 2d$, right in direction, is wrong in
form, the ceiling climbing with $E_{\max}$ an order of magnitude more slowly than that rule
requires, so $E_{\max}/2$ matched only at the value we happened to freeze. One caution is
substantial: a purely linear statistic of the delay cloud follows the same curve, so much of what
saturates may be how many components the delay window resolves linearly, not geometry.

\subsection{The silence control}\label{sec:silence}

Requirement~4 asks that a zero learning rate silence the observer; most published analyses of
dimension on training logs do not run it. It invalidated two of our six systems: read naively both
succeed, yet with the parameters frozen each reproduces its reported result at every rank. It also
rejects the instantaneous mini-batch loss of \cref{sec:digits}, otherwise among the better
observers; two further systems fail requirement~2 (\cref{tab:ladder}, \cref{app:nulls}).

\subsection{A constrained head on a network trained on image data}\label{sec:digits}

The sixth system uses real inputs: a tanh network trained on the scikit-learn handwritten digits
and frozen, with a linear head confined to a $k$-dimensional affine subspace under softmax
cross-entropy, so the curvature is data-dependent and all the dynamics occur in $\vc$. The
excitation is made $r$-dimensional by modulating the loss weights of $r$ of twelve fixed data
groups and orthogonalising the resulting gradient directions (\cref{app:constructions}); the
measured effective rank then matches $r$ (\cref{tab:gt}). Nine excitation cases are run, spanning
the three regimes of \cref{tab:classes}, and \cref{fig:regimes} shows five of them.

Twelve observers are recorded (\cref{app:obs}), and the estimator's parameters were frozen on four
of them and on reserved seeds and ranks, so what follows is out-of-sample in rank and seed
throughout and in observer for most of what is scored. In the deterministic recurrent regime the
estimate recovers the active dimension to within about one component, and every observer orders
the withheld ranks correctly (\cref{tab:ladder}; \cref{app:nulls} scores them one by one). One caveat attaches
to the observers a run already records: the two parameter norms are also the only observers whose
roughness by itself orders the withheld ranks, so for them a smoothness explanation cannot be
excluded. Twenty available directions give the same picture with a
lower ceiling; that configuration is exploratory, having no seed split, a Theiler exclusion
smaller than its own embedding span, and $E = 2d$ exactly, one short of \cref{sec:estimator}'s condition
(\cref{app:theiler}).

\subsection{Dynamical regime}\label{sec:regime}

\Cref{fig:regimes} evaluates the estimator in each regime, at one nominal rank, observer and
matched octave. Under stochastic excitation the estimate is flat in the rank and
well above the true value, responding to $E_{\max}$ and the record rather than to anything in the
data. Weak noise on an otherwise recurrent trajectory is less damaging: it destroys the absolute
value and leaves the ordering.

A decaying transient returns near no state it has left, so the set it visits is a curve and
$d_{\mathrm{act}} = 1$. The estimate exceeds that by more than an order of magnitude
(\cref{tab:classes}), and the Theiler exclusion is why. Remove it and the estimator returns, at
every rank, seed and observer, the value maximum likelihood must return on a uniformly sampled
curve, while the recurrent cases do not move (\cref{app:exclusion}). Nor is what it returns a level:
it is a divergent quantity read at the exclusion cap, and that it exceeds $E_{\max}$ is the
expected behaviour of a maximum likelihood fit whose model is violated rather than a bound failing.
No clamping is applied (\cref{app:alg}).

Every system here is driven and training is not, so we test the one undriven candidate the
literature offers: the edge of stability \citep{cohen2021gradient}. The perceptron of
\cref{sec:grokking} reaches it, its sharpness self-limiting while the loss rises on half of all
steps. But oscillation is not recurrence, and here the diagnostics invert the classification: they
admit the runs that never return near a state they have left, and refuse the minority that do
(\cref{app:eos}).

\subsection{The delay lag}\label{sec:tau}

The delay window spans $(E_{\max}-1)\tau$ samples, and it must cover a real fraction of the
oscillation period; otherwise the delay coordinates are collinear and the torus never unfolds. That
the window rather than the lag is what matters is standard \citep{casdagli1991state}. On a fixed
torus, varying $\tau$ alone moves the estimate over more than an order of magnitude, and it passes
through the truth only where the delay window covers a few tenths of a period (\cref{tab:tau},
\cref{fig:tau}).

The right lag follows from the oscillation period, which a controlled experiment knows and a
training log does not, and we do not solve this. The lag used in \cref{sec:grokking} is the frozen
one, transplanted from a calibration system whose drive period is far shorter than the
autocorrelation time of those logs, which by itself forbids reading an absolute value there. The
identifiability ratio inherits the problem: doubling $E_{\max}$ doubles the delay span, so a large
value means either that the estimate belongs to the embedding space or that the lag is mismatched,
and on the grokking logs both readings are live.

\subsection{Nuisance factors}\label{sec:nuisance}

At a fixed active dimension of four we vary seven factors that leave it unchanged, over five seeds
and twelve observers, and read each against a real four-component change (\cref{tab:controls}). Two
matter. Smoothness passes on most observers: shifting the drive band by an octave
changes the roughness sharply and moves the estimate no further than seed-to-seed scatter does,
though it fails on the two parameter norms, whose roughness orders the withheld ranks perfectly.
The second factor is the observer's gain. A constant rescaling leaves the estimate unchanged, the
scorer standardising it away, but a \emph{ramped} gain moves it by about a component --- so a run
whose parameter norm grows will drift by that much with nothing dimensional occurring. That is the
nuisance that matters most in \cref{sec:grokking}.

\subsection{The estimand is a dimension, not an effective rank}\label{sec:aniso}

Every construction in \cref{sec:ladder} equalises the drive amplitudes, which makes the active
dimension and the effective rank of \cref{sec:three} numerically equal, and an experiment in which
two candidate estimands agree cannot say which the estimator reports. Driving mode
$l$ at amplitude $q^{l}$ separates the two: the orbit still closes onto an $r$-torus, so
$d_{\mathrm{act}} = r$, while the effective rank decays with $q$.

The estimate follows the dimension (\cref{tab:aniso}, \cref{fig:aniso}): as the drive is made
anisotropic the effective rank decays while the estimate stays with the number of modes, and across
the grid it is closer to that number than to the effective rank. It registers only the modes
it can resolve, though: once the weakest lies far enough below the strongest, the estimate decays
with the rank, the torus it can see having fewer dimensions than the one built.

\paragraph{The diagnostics.}\label{sec:diagnostics-validated} With no ground truth, the
identifiability ratio separates the accurate cases from the inaccurate ones without overlap, within
a regime as well as between them. It is not sufficient alone --- on a transient
$\rho_{\mathrm{ident}} \approx 1$ as well, for the wrong reason --- and must be paired with the
trend-crossing count, which is near zero there. Both are calibrated on these five cases only, which is why \cref{sec:grokking} treats the
admissible band as a description and not a decision rule.

\section{Application to grokking}\label{sec:grokking}

We work in both settings of \cref{sec:related}, which differ in every relevant respect
(\cref{app:runs} lists every run and every departure from the sources). The transformer runs are
regularised, trained with AdamW under cross-entropy on mini-batches where the source is full-batch,
which puts them in the stochastic regime of \cref{tab:classes}. The perceptron runs are
unregularised full-batch descent on mean squared error; we take the tasks and representability
criterion of \citet{doshi2024grokking} but not their optimiser (\cref{app:repr}). The diagnostics
refuse the level both settings' logs report, so we pay for the trajectory instead, and then return
to the logs with the one question a refused level still permits.

\subsection{The diagnostics refuse both settings}\label{sec:grok-diagnostics}

The frozen configuration of \cref{sec:digits}, with only the window and stride shortened to fit
these records, gives \cref{tab:grok-diagnostics} and \cref{fig:map}. The two settings fail in
different corners, and neither failure appeals to any label. The regularised mini-batch runs occupy
a band of the identifiability ratio the deterministic regimes never reach, and their estimates
scatter widely across seeds of one configuration, set by $E_{\max}$ and the record rather than by
any rank, exactly as \cref{fig:regimes} predicts for stochastic excitation. The full-batch runs
carry a transient's signature instead: $
ho_{\mathrm{ident}} pprox 1$ with almost no trend
crossings. In neither setting may the value be read as a dimension.

A change inside a run might still be read, since the offset a fixed configuration imposes is
constant within a run, and the floor is the drift a ramped gain alone produces
(\cref{sec:nuisance}). Not at this window: it spans tens of thousands of optimiser steps against a
transition hundreds wide, a want of resolution and not of signal, so \cref{sec:matched} shortens it
(\cref{app:window}).

\paragraph{Label-matched pairs.}\label{sec:pairs} The perceptron setting supplies the control the
first cannot: both members of a pair fit the training set completely, under identical optimisation,
and differ by one monomial. The estimate comes out higher for the non-generalising member in every
pair, with no overlap between the groups --- and it is still not a dimension. All eight runs are
transient, the separation is already visible in the raw loss curves, the direct measurement does
not reproduce it, and a fall is not specific to generalisation: one occurs in a long run that never
leaves chance accuracy (\cref{app:pairs,app:fb,app:falls}).

\subsection{The trajectory simplifies at generalisation}\label{sec:direct}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_dip.pdf}

A stored trajectory delivers what a log cannot: the effective rank of \cref{sec:three} is defined
whether or not the orbit recurs. We sketch it in parameter and in function space at every logged
step of a transformer trained to grok, and report $\PR^{\mathrm{det}}$ (\cref{app:sketch}). Near
generalisation it collapses (\cref{fig:dip}): in function space by close to an order of magnitude,
in parameter space to near unity, each within a few hundred steps of the transition, and both then
re-expand (\cref{tab:dip}). Two things support the reading. The collapse occurs at generalisation in
seeds whose generalisation steps differ several-fold, and in a second task, so it is not tracking
training progress; and the displacement reaches its floor thousands of steps later and never
recovers, where the rank does, so the trajectory has not simply stopped moving.

The effective rank measures the variation inside the window, not the extent of the set the orbit
visits, so what concentrates on one direction is that variation and not $d_{\mathrm{act}}$.

\paragraph{The controls.}\label{sec:nowd} In the transition window the controls decline
much less than the generalising runs, but over their whole budget one of them declines just as far
(\cref{tab:dip}): depth does not separate the groups, timing and shape do. Both controls peak early
and then settle, and their parameter norm never returns to its earlier level, where every
generalising run gives back much of its growth. A low rank is reached both by a run that has come
to rest and by one about to generalise, and that growing norm is the ramped gain of
\cref{sec:nuisance}, the mechanism behind the scalar-log decline of \cref{sec:pairs}.

\subsection{The log estimate falls where the trajectory does}\label{sec:matched}

Setting the window in units of the transition instead of the record makes the test of
\cref{sec:grok-diagnostics} possible. We re-run the estimator on the same logs, at the window and
midpoints the direct measurement uses, so the two statistics are sampled at the same instants of
the same runs. The frozen configuration will not fit in a window that short, and choosing a
replacement on the outcome is what requirement~2 exists to prevent, so we report every cell of a
grid and take as headline the one a rule blind to the outcome selects (\cref{app:window}).

On the parameter norm the estimate halves at the generalisation step in all four generalising runs,
with its floor on the same side of the transition as the direct measurement's (\cref{fig:dip}d),
while the controls show no comparable movement. That drop is among the largest each generalising
run ever shows, and in the controls it lies within their ordinary range; all but one of the usable
grid cells separate the groups (\cref{tab:matched}). Nor is it the observer's shape, the nuisance \cref{sec:nuisance}
identifies: against surrogates that keep the log's shape and its fluctuation spectrum, every
generalising run's $p$ lies below every control's, at every smoothing length and seeding tried
(\cref{app:window}).

Two signatures of one event, then, one paid for and one already written by the run. Three things
keep the second modest. Its level cannot be read as a dimension, the identifiability ratio being uncomputable at
this window, so only the change is read. The observer is the parameter norm, for which
\cref{sec:nuisance} cannot exclude a smoothness explanation, so the surrogate control and not the
level carries the claim. And a simpler statistic registers it more strongly: the roughness
of the same windows collapses by an order of magnitude where the estimate merely halves, so most of
what this log carries about the transition is the collapse of its innovation, which needs no
embedding at all.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

In a deterministic recurrent system with a matched delay lag, one scalar log of a suitable observer
counts the independent components of the set the optimiser visits, to within about one component
and up to about eight of them. Outside that regime the number it returns is not that dimension: a
transient's visited set is a curve, and stochastic forcing leaves no invariant set to have one.
What makes the instrument usable is therefore the pair of diagnostics beside it, and on grokking
they refuse both standard settings. Measured directly instead, the trajectory
effective rank collapses at generalisation and re-expands in the regularised runs, much less in
their controls, and at a matched window the log estimate follows it down.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The measurement needs a recurrent regime and a lag matched to a
period a log does not reveal, it is ordinal above about eight components, and the configuration is
a choice as much as a measurement (\cref{app:config}). The collapse rests on four runs against two
controls differing in the variable that also drives the parameter norm, is a signature and not an
early warning, and does not reproduce in the second setting. The matched-window drop is close to
what a ramped gain alone produces, one control has little range to move through, and the
edge-of-stability evidence is seven runs on one task (\cref{app:fb,app:window,app:eos}).

\section{The estimator}\label{app:alg}

\Cref{alg:mg} is the estimator as it was run. It computes \cref{eq:mg} with three departures that
affect what is reported, and it fixes the constants the main text leaves unnamed.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Pooled Levina--Bickel intrinsic dimension of a delay reconstruction}
\label{alg:mg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require series $x_{1:n}$; lag $\tau$; embedding dimension $E$; neighbours $m$; Theiler
exclusion $W_T$; dither $\sigma$
\State $z_t \gets (x_t - \operatorname{mean} x) / \operatorname{std} x + \sigma \epsilon_t$,\quad
       $\epsilon_t \sim \gN(0,1)$ \Comment{breaks exact ties}
\State $\vy_t \gets (z_t, z_{t-\tau}, \dots, z_{t-(E-1)\tau})$ for $t = (E-1)\tau+1, \dots, n$
\State $S \gets 0$; $N \gets 0$; $n_{\mathrm{dist}} \gets 0$; $n_{\mathrm{ratio}} \gets 0$
\For{each $\vy_i$}
  \State $r^{(i)}_1 \le \dots \le r^{(i)}_m \gets$ distances to the $m$ nearest $\vy_j$
         with $|i-j| > W_T$
  \State $r^{(i)}_j \gets \max(r^{(i)}_j, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{dist}})$ for each $j$; count those
         clipped into $n_{\mathrm{dist}}$
  \State $S_i \gets \max\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1} \log(r^{(i)}_m / r^{(i)}_j),\,
         \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ratio}}\bigr)$; count clips into $n_{\mathrm{ratio}}$
  \State $S \gets S + S_i$; \quad $N \gets N + 1$
\EndFor
\State mark the window degenerate if $n_{\mathrm{dist}}$ or $n_{\mathrm{ratio}}$ exceeds
       $1\,\%$ of its denominator
\State \Return $\bigl(N(m-1) - 1\bigr) / S$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The three departures are these. \emph{The returned value is $(N(m-1)-1)/S$ rather than
$N(m-1)/S$}, the exactly unbiased pooled maximum likelihood estimate for a $\Gamma(N(m-1), 1/d)$
sample, which differs from \cref{eq:mg} by less than $10^{-5}$ at the sizes used here.
\emph{A window marked degenerate is returned rather than discarded}: the ladder pipelines drop
marked windows when summarising a series over its sliding windows, the two grokking pipelines take the
median over all of them, and no published cell of \cref{tab:grok-diagnostics} contains one.
\emph{The returned value is never clamped to $E$}, so a divergent estimate is reported as
divergent; clamping would convert the transient regime of \cref{tab:classes} into a plausible
number.

The floors are $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dist}} = 10^{-8}$ on a neighbour distance and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ratio}} = 10^{-5}$ on the per-point log-ratio sum, with $\sigma =
10^{-9}$ on the standardised series. The Theiler exclusion is
$W_T = \max\bigl((E-1)\tau,\, t_{\mathrm{acf}}\bigr)$, where $t_{\mathrm{acf}}$ is the first
lag at which the autocorrelation falls below $1/e$, capped at $150$ samples because the
neighbour query cost is linear in it. \Cref{app:theiler} measures what that cap costs.

The selection objective in both cases is the error of the median estimate against the median

Five properties of these choices should be stated with them.

\emph{Both lie at a grid boundary.} The eight-direction configuration is at the top of all
five swept axes and the twenty-direction one at the top of both. That is less serious than it
appears for the first: four of its five axes have only two levels, so every configuration in that
grid is at a boundary in four of them, and $\tau$, the only axis with an interior value, has its
winner at the top end. But neither configuration is interior, and a wider grid could move the
level.

\emph{The selection error spans a factor of five on identical data.} Across the
eight-direction grid the error runs from $0.32$ to $1.50$ on the same logs, so the level is a
choice as much as a measurement, and only the withheld numbers of \cref{sec:digits} may be read
as recovery.

\emph{The Theiler axis is inert on this data.} The autocorrelation rule returns the larger of
the embedding span $(E_{\max}-1)\tau$ and the measured autocorrelation time, and on these logs
the autocorrelation time is one to three samples against an embedding span of nine or more. The
two Theiler settings therefore return bit-identical values wherever they are compared, and the
$48$-point grid contains $24$ distinct measurements.

caps the Theiler exclusion at $150$ samples, which binds unconditionally at the twenty-direction
configuration and on five of the seven training logs of \cref{sec:grokking}.
\Cref{app:theiler} states the departure and measures what it costs.

no estimator field, and \cref{tab:geometry} states what each of them uses. The constructed-system
setting is the one behind the $0.87$ of \cref{sec:digits}, so ``at the frozen configuration'' is true
there of the estimator and not of the stride. Every other field --- $E_{\max}$, $\tau$, $m$, the
Theiler rule, the dither --- is used as frozen throughout, so what moves between experiments is how
the record is cut up, not how a window is scored.

\Cref{tab:alts} should be read with three things in mind, and none of them favours MG.

First, MG is not the most accurate statistic at either range: the uncorrected per-point average
LB is ahead of it at both. The correction of \cref{sec:estimator} removes a positive bias, and
on a saturating estimate that bias was partly cancelling the saturation, so removing it costs
accuracy exactly where the estimate is already saturating.

Second, MG is not the best-ordering statistic at twenty directions. The spectral participation
ratio at $256$ and $1024$ bands orders the ranks more faithfully than MG does, at three times
the error. If only the ordering is wanted, a statistic requiring no embedding and no neighbour
search will supply it.

Third, MG's advantage over the linear delay participation ratio is a factor of about two at
eight directions, not the factor of six that the selection split of \cref{app:config}
reports. That larger figure is computed on the ranks and seeds the configuration was chosen on
and is a property of the selection, not a recovery result.

\paragraph{Dependence on the observer.} Running the same torus through two scalar
observers that differ only in how evenly they weight the modes settles which statistic is
preferable, and the answer is that neither is, in general (\cref{tab:obsdep}). Under an observer
that weights all modes equally the spectral participation ratio is the more accurate; under a
generic observer with random weights, which is what a random projection of a network's parameters
actually is, MG is. Both orderings are perfect in three of those four cells, so what separates the
statistics is absolute recovery and not rank agreement.

The size of each advantage also depends on the delay lag, which a controlled experiment can choose
and a training log cannot. Held at a lag common to both observers rather than at the one best for
each, the direction of each advantage survives and its size does not.

\section{System constructions}\label{app:constructions}

Three constructions are described here: the image-data system of \cref{sec:digits}, the
function-space variant scored beside it, and the same system at twenty available directions.

\paragraph{The image-data system of \cref{sec:digits}.} The data is partitioned into twelve
fixed groups, the same partition at every rank so that the partition is not itself a function
of $r$, and the loss weights of $r$ of them are modulated by $r$ rationally independent
sinusoids filling one octave. Modulating group $j$ tilts the gradient along a direction
$\vphi_j$. These directions are neither orthogonal nor of equal effect, since random data
groups have correlated gradients, so a mixing matrix $\mathrm{pinv}(\Phi) Q$ with $Q$ an
orthonormal basis of $\operatorname{range}(\Phi)$ orthogonalises them, and the per-mode scalar
response gain of the linearised update is divided out. Without this correction the trajectory
participation ratio falls well below $r$.

\Cref{tab:gt} verifies that the construction achieves what it intends, and two of its rows are
results rather than settings. A decaying transient does not recur whatever $r$ is, since a
converging trajectory is a curve; and plain mini-batch descent remains at $6.2$ regardless of $r$,
because its noise rank is fixed by the per-example gradient covariance of the data and is not a
free parameter of the experiment. Projecting real mini-batch noise onto $r$ directions recovers
only part of the intended rank, which is why that regime is scored against its measured value
and not against $r$.

Two properties of the backbone bear on the construction. The functional rank is $10$ at every
rank, seed and regime, with a participation ratio between $8.40$ and $9.03$; it belongs to the
frozen backbone and does not depend on the excitation. And the condition number of the
drive-direction matrix is $1.00$ at $r=1$ by construction and has a median of $13.3$ at $r=8$,
which is the anisotropy the mixing matrix exists to remove.

\paragraph{The function-space construction.} A local adapter is built around a trained network of
widths $64$, $16$ and $10$, and its function-space Jacobian is QR-whitened, so that its first
$r$ columns have rank $r$ at equal local scale. A quasiperiodic teacher excites every adapter
coordinate and the student tracks it by exact backpropagation through both layers. The
functional, trajectory-covariance and update-covariance ranks were all verified to equal $r$.

\paragraph{The twenty-direction system.} The same construction at twenty available directions
reproduces the nominal rank to within $0.008$ components over $r = 1, \dots, 20$ in the
recurrent regime, and to three decimals up to $r = 10$. The other regimes behave as at ten
directions: injected noise tracks $r$ closely, projected mini-batch noise reaches only $9.4$
at $r = 20$, and the decaying transient stays between $1.02$ and $1.09$ throughout.

\section{The trajectory sketch}\label{app:sketch}

The direct measurement of \cref{sec:direct} needs the trajectory itself, which is the whole
parameter vector at each of two to three thousand logged steps. Every logged step is therefore
compressed by a CountSketch \citep{charikar2002finding, weinberger2009feature} to $\R^{1024}$,
with two independent hash families. Two spaces are recorded: the parameters, and the probe logits
after centring each example over classes and normalising to unit length. The normalisation is
necessary: the raw logit scale is mechanically coupled to weight decay, so a signal read off it
could not be distinguished from a detector of weight decay.

We do not verify a spectral guarantee for this. The elementary bound for a sketch of this kind is
on pairwise inner products \citep{weinberger2009feature}, and a participation ratio is a
functional of the whole spectrum, on which an entrywise Gram bound is weak. A subspace-embedding
guarantee does exist for CountSketch \citep{clarkson2013low} and would apply here, since a
sixty-sample window has rank at most sixty, but it requires a rank we do not measure per window.
What the two hash families supply instead is a direct estimate of the error: every reported value
is the mean of the two, and their disagreement is recorded with it. Over the published
transformer windows that disagreement is $1.1\,\%$ of the value at the median and $8.4\,\%$ at
its worst, which is the number the reader should use, and it is smaller than every effect
\cref{sec:direct} reads.

Three checks stand behind the numbers. \emph{The sketch is accurate on synthetic trajectories of
known rank.} \Cref{tab:sketch} pushes such trajectories through it and compares against the same
statistic computed on the uncompressed trajectory: the sketch is accurate to $0.04$ components
throughout, and the shortfall against the nominal rank at $5$ and $10$ is present without any
compression, so it belongs to the participation ratio on a sixty-sample cloud and not to the
sketch. Nothing above rank ten is tested, however, and the plateaus in \cref{fig:dip} are higher
than that.

\emph{It agrees with exact arithmetic on a real run.} On the perceptron architecture, where the
parameter count is small enough to allow it, the participation ratio computed on all $145\,500$ raw
parameters agrees with the sketched value to three decimals.

\emph{The observer does not perturb training.} The log is required to be bit-identical with and
without it, over four hundred steps at reduced probe size. That check matters because the task
module seeds one global random stream that the train--validation split, the initialisation and the
mini-batch order all continue: a single stray draw changes the initial weights and can destroy
grokking.

Windows are sixty logged rows, which is $600$ optimiser steps for the modular runs and $300$

\section{The full-batch setting}\label{app:fb}

Repeating the direct measurement on the perceptron setting gives a result that must be read as
a limit of the measurement rather than as a negative. Under full-batch gradient descent every
window of every run has a participation ratio between $1.000$ and $1.362$ on the four
statistics the comparison uses, and between $1.000$ and $2.171$ over all eight recorded
columns. This is not an artefact of the sketch, which \cref{app:sketch} checks against exact
arithmetic on the same architecture.

A deterministic update makes the trajectory a smooth curve, and over a $600$-step window a smooth
curve is straight to within the measurement precision, so what the statistic reports is the
curvature of the trajectory at the scale the window sets, and at that scale there is almost none.
The statistic has no range in which a collapse could appear, so the absence of a feature is not
evidence of its absence: the window must be set in units of the curvature scale of the trajectory.

Repeating the measurement against window length confirms the diagnosis and withdraws the
opening it appeared to leave. Two runs of $150\,000$ steps, a generalising one and its
label-matched control, are measured over windows spanning $600$ to $120\,000$ optimiser
steps, with the window lengthened by spreading sixty samples further apart rather than by
adding samples, so that the bound on the statistic and its noise floor stay exactly where
the published measurement put them and only the span changes. \Cref{tab:prwindow} gives
the result. The statistic does leave the floor, at a scale of about $10^4$ steps, two
orders of magnitude above the window that produced the $1.000$--$1.362$: the absence of a
feature there was a resolution limit and not a fact about the run.

It is not, however, a hidden signal. What appears at $12\,000$ steps is one localised maximum, and
both runs have one of the same size (\cref{tab:prwindow}); function space agrees, with more range
and no more separation, $2.618$ against $2.145$. Nor can the maximum be placed
relative to either milestone: the two centres are adjacent points of a $3\,450$-step grid and
their windows overlap over $6\,900$ to $15\,250$ steps. Measured against memorisation the two
offsets are $+3\,600$ and $-450$, a difference this design cannot resolve --- a $12\,000$-step
window cannot place a feature to better than the interval between memorisation and generalisation.

The claim the experiment does support is the negative one: at the window where the statistic
has range, the generalising run and its label-matched control are not separated by it. Beyond
$30\,000$ steps the ordering reverses and the control moves further than the generalising run,
because the window is then a large fraction of a run whose weight norm is still climbing and
the statistic is reading that drift. The full-batch trajectory therefore carries no rank
signature of generalisation at any window length between $600$ and $120\,000$ steps: the
collapse of \cref{sec:direct} belongs to the regularised stochastic setting, and a trajectory
participation ratio must be controlled for window length as well as for batch size before
anything is read from it.

\section{Declines of the estimate that are not generalisation}\label{app:falls}

A decline of the estimate is not specific to generalisation. In a $200\,000$-step run at $p = 211$
with no weight decay, which memorises at step $1\,800$ and ends at $3.6\,\%$ validation accuracy,
a decline is detected $49\,250$ steps after memorisation. The amplitude control of
\cref{sec:nuisance} supplies a mechanism that needs no dimension at all: a ramped observer gain
moves the estimate by $1.16$ components, and this run's parameter norm grows from $43$ to $126$
over the budget. It is the growth \cref{sec:nowd} reports in the two weight-decay-zero controls,
acting through the observer.

What that run cannot show is that its trajectory simplified. Its log lies in the transient
regime of \cref{tab:grok-diagnostics}, where the estimate is not a dimension, so a decline in it is
a decline of a quantity we have argued should not be read. The two must be kept apart: the direct
measurement of \cref{sec:direct} is an effective rank of the trajectory itself, and it declines in
four generalising runs and not in their controls, whereas the estimate on a log, at the frozen
window and in these settings, is neither a dimension nor an effective rank and declines in at least
one run that does not generalise. What follows is the weaker and defensible claim, that a decline
on a log is not evidence of generalisation.

\Cref{sec:matched} does not overturn it. There the log estimate declines where the trajectory does,
in four runs and in neither control, but at a window two orders of magnitude shorter than this one
and under a configuration chosen for that window; the level is no more readable there than here,
and what is read is a change against the run's own distribution of changes. Nor is the absence of
generalisation within a budget evidence that a decline was a false alarm: two reduced-data
configurations against which earlier specificity figures were computed generalise once the budget
is extended, so such runs are censored and no specificity figure may be computed against them.

\section{Resolution of a windowed estimate on a training log}\label{app:window}

The claim of \cref{sec:grok-diagnostics} that a change within a run could be read against a fixed
configuration is testable on the seven extended runs, and the test returns a resolution limit
rather than an answer. The frozen configuration takes a window of a third of each record: $4\,000$
logged samples, which at the logging stride is $39\,990$ optimiser steps, advanced by $10\,000$
steps. That is nine windows per run and sixty-three weight-norm windows in all, and it localises
a feature only to $\pm 19\,995$ steps, sixty-seven times coarser than the direct measurement of
\cref{sec:direct}.

The consequence is that the alignment \cref{fig:dip} performs cannot be performed here. Of the
twenty-seven window centres belonging to the three generalising runs, one lies within $5\,000$
steps of its own generalisation step and two within $10\,000$; no two runs share an offset, so no
offset has more than one run defined and no pooled mean exists. For the positive
control the entire pre-generalisation phase is thirteen per cent of one window; for the
reduced-data run that generalises at $110\,940$, no window centre falls after the transition at
all.

What the runs do show is consistent with that. The one run with a centre near its generalisation
step falls by $3.54$ components over the next stride, three times the floor --- but the step lies
inside four of its nine windows, and the falling windows are those in which its parameter norm is
itself moving fastest, which is the ramped gain of \cref{sec:nuisance}. The other two runs show
nothing.

Nor is any of this specific. Across the fifty-six stride-to-stride changes, six of twenty-four
clear the floor in the runs that generalise and six of thirty-two in the runs that do not. The
largest change in the set, $6.36$ components, is in a run that does generalise, but it falls
nearly ninety thousand steps after its generalisation step; the next largest, $5.20$, is in a run
that ends at seven per cent validation accuracy. The tightest comparison available is three runs
of one configuration, of which one generalises: their ranges are $0.77$, $0.78$ and $0.67$
components, and nothing separates them.

This is the argument of \cref{app:fb} in a second place. At the window the configuration provides,
the statistic has no range in which a feature localised to the transition could appear, so its
absence is not evidence of absence. Testing the claim needs a window set in units of the width of
the transition, which for these runs is hundreds of steps and not tens of thousands: a re-run of
the estimator, not a re-reading of what we have.

\paragraph{The matched-window re-run.} \Cref{sec:matched} performs it, on the six runs of \cref{fig:dip} rather
than the seven of this appendix, because those are the runs whose trajectory was stored and only a
paired comparison answers the question. The window is $60$ samples throughout, which is $600$
optimiser steps for the modular runs at their stride-$10$ logging and $300$ for the $S_5$ runs at
stride $5$, centred on the midpoints of the windows of \cref{sec:direct}; since those are $600$ and
$295$ steps wide, every log window matches the one it is paired with to within two per cent. Four
things bear on how the result should be read.

\emph{The configuration is not the frozen one, and cannot be.} At $60$ samples the frozen delay
span, $(20-1) \times 4 = 76$, exceeds the window. The grid crosses
$E_{\max} \in \{4, 6, 10\}$, $\tau \in \{1, 2, 4\}$, $k \in \{5, 20\}$ and both Theiler rules,
thirty-six cells, of which twenty-six are long enough to return a value. Twenty-five of those
separate the four generalising runs from the two controls; the exception is $E_{\max} = 6$,
$\tau = 1$, $k = 5$ under the embedding rule. The headline cell is named by the rule of
\cref{sec:matched} and is not the best cell: $E_{\max} = 4$, $\tau = 4$, $k = 20$ gives a
shallowest generalising fall of $2.12$ against its $1.91$.

\emph{The admissibility diagnostics do not survive the shortening.} The identifiability ratio needs
$2E_{\max}$. At $E_{\max} = 10$ the doubled delay span does fit the window, but the Theiler
exclusion then leaves two usable points against $k = 20$, so the headline cell has no ratio. Where
it can be computed, at $E_{\max} \le 6$, it reads $0.97$ to $1.00$ under the frozen Theiler rule
and $1.29$ to $1.62$ under the other, and settles nothing. That is why \cref{sec:matched} reads a
change and not a level.

\emph{Linear detrending removes the effect, and the surrogate control does not.} Repeating every
cell on the linearly detrended window --- the removal $\PR^{\mathrm{det}}$ performs per coordinate
--- leaves no cell separating the two groups. The two removals are not comparable: on a
$1024$-dimensional sketch a per-coordinate linear detrend takes one direction of many, while on a
scalar it takes most of the series. The surrogate control keeps the shape and destroys only the
fine structure, and it is the one that discriminates. Both are reported in \cref{tab:matched}.

\emph{The observer matters.} The result above is the parameter norm. On the training loss ten of
the twenty-six cells separate the groups and the surrogate control passes in the $S_5$ run only;
on the validation loss fifteen do, and that observer defines the event being aligned on, so it is
listed for completeness and not read.

\emph{The controls have almost no dynamic range.} At this window the baseline belongs to the
configuration and not to the run: on a standardised $60$-sample straight line \cref{eq:mg} returns
$2.7733$, and every run's pre-transition level lies within $0.28$ of that. The modular control's
parameter-norm log is straight to $0.7\,\%$ of its own spread across the whole comparison
interval, so its estimate cannot move --- a span of $0.0029$ components over fifty windows, against
$2.05$ to $2.56$ in the generalising runs. Its $D = 1.00$ therefore records that the observer
offered the estimator no local structure to respond to, not that a dimension was measured and
found unchanged: a statistic with three thousandths of a component of range could not have shown a
two-fold fall whatever the trajectory did. The $S_5$ control is a weaker case of the same thing,
$3.2\,\%$ residual and a $0.22$-component span.

What this removes is any reading of the two controls' $D$ and quantile columns as informative
dimensional negatives. It leaves the surrogate control untouched, since there each run is compared
only against surrogates of itself, and it leaves the four generalising runs untouched; and it
reinforces the point already made, that the separation between the groups is mechanically a
separation in local roughness.

\section{The representation dimension in closed form}\label{app:repr}

The perceptron setting of \cref{sec:grokking} makes the learned representation analytically
available, and three different numbers can be read off it. They are not commensurable and the
literature does not always separate them.

The \emph{mode count} is $(p+1)/2$, or $49$ at $p = 97$: the number of distinct Fourier
frequencies the generalising solution of \citet{gromov2023grokking} uses. The \emph{order
parameter} is the mean inverse participation ratio of the first layer's Fourier spectrum, which
is $1.000$ for that solution because each neuron carries a single frequency, and about $0.041$ at
random initialisation; it measures how concentrated each neuron is, not how many modes exist. The
\emph{effective rank} is the participation ratio of the singular values of the first-layer weight
matrix, which for the analytic solution is $148.8$: that matrix is $500 \times 194$ and full rank,
because each non-zero frequency contributes a sine and a cosine direction in each of the two input
blocks, so $148.8$ says how evenly those $194$ directions are used and does not count anything.
Only the first is a dimension in the sense the closed form fixes. Two things follow from measuring
the other two, and both qualify readings of the representation dimension elsewhere.

\emph{The representation is not complete at the transition.} \Cref{tab:ipr-trajectory} follows the
order parameter through a modular addition run. It does not jump at the transition: it begins
rising at memorisation, passes smoothly through, and goes on rising for tens of thousands of steps
afterwards without reaching the analytic value. Generalisation therefore arrives well before the
representation is complete, and requires it only to be periodic enough.

\section{The exclusion cap at the twenty-direction configuration}\label{app:theiler}

The Theiler exclusion is meant to remove neighbours that are close in time rather than in phase
space, and the rule this study asks for is $W_T = \max((E-1)\tau,\, t_{\mathrm{acf}})$: at least
the full span of a delay vector, so that two neighbours cannot be near each other merely because
their windows overlap. The implementation caps $W_T$ at $150$ samples, because the neighbour
query cost is linear in it.

At the eight-direction configuration the embedding span is $(20-1) \times 4 = 76$, so the cap
binds only where the autocorrelation time exceeds $150$: never on the calibration systems, whose
autocorrelation time is one to three samples, but on five of the seven parameter-norm records of
\cref{sec:grokking}, where it is $161$ to $858$. Those five were scored at $W_T = 150$ rather than
at the value the rule asks for, in the direction that inflates the estimate. At the
twenty-direction configuration the span is $(40-1) \times 16 = 624$, so the cap binds
unconditionally and every row of those scores records $W_T = 150$, under a quarter of what the
rule asks for. This is a departure from the stated protocol, not a choice we made deliberately,
and it is why \cref{sec:digits} marks that result exploratory.

The same cap has a second effect that the identifiability ratio inherits. The exclusion actually
applied is $W_T = \min\bigl(\max((E-1)\tau,\, t_{\mathrm{acf}}),\, 150\bigr)$, and the ratio's
numerator is computed at $2E_{\max}$ with $\tau$ held fixed. At the eight-direction configuration
the numerator's span is therefore $(40-1) \times 4 = 156$ and the denominator's $76$: the
numerator is capped at $150$ whatever the data does, while the denominator is capped only once
$t_{\mathrm{acf}}$ reaches $150$. The two are treated identically exactly when
$t_{\mathrm{acf}} \ge 150$ and differently when it is smaller.

asymmetric cases are the other two and, more consequentially, the calibration systems of
\cref{sec:ladder}, whose autocorrelation time is one to three samples, so that the reference band
of \cref{sec:diagnostics-validated} was itself measured with $76$ against $150$. The effect is
small at this span and the diagnostic separates the regimes of \cref{sec:validity} without
overlap, but a ratio built from two differently excluded estimates is not the clean quantity
\cref{sec:diagnostics} describes, and lifting the cap is the first thing to change in any
follow-up.

error of a few tenths of a per cent, and the step is resolved only to the logging stride, which

instead require the threshold to hold over a sustained block, which is the stricter rule; the two
rules put memorisation within $70$ steps of each other on every run here and differ by $1\,080$

estimator. The claim is testable on the excitation cases of \cref{sec:digits} by moving one setting and

Three things follow. \emph{The number at $W_T = 0$ is the right one.} On a uniformly sampled curve
the $20$ nearest neighbours lie at offsets $\pm 1 \dots \pm 10$, for which \cref{eq:mg} returns
$1.19928$ exactly; the transient case returns $1.1971 \pm 0.0010$ across all forty-eight cells. The
excess over unity is the lattice bias of a deterministically sampled curve, not an error, and the
measured trajectory participation ratio of the same case is $1.03$ to $1.07$.

\emph{The mechanism is the loss of near neighbours, not of neighbours.} The estimator fills its
twenty slots at every exclusion; what changes is how far away they are. Before any exclusion the
median temporal separation of a point's twenty nearest neighbours is $2546$ samples on the fast
case and $6$ on the transient --- there, the nearest neighbours in space simply \emph{are} the
temporal ones. Excluding $150$ samples costs the fast case almost nothing, $138\,048$ close pairs
surviving of $138\,761$, and costs the transient all $131\,849$ of its own. Its twenty retained
distances then span a factor of $1.07$, their log-ratio sum falls from $15.86$ to $0.63$, and the
reciprocal in \cref{eq:mg} diverges. No cell is marked degenerate, so this is not a numerical
floor. An exponential decay with no optimiser and no data reproduces both ends, $1.196$ and
$26.4$.

\emph{Two qualifications.} Recurrence is not always needed even here: in the fast case $W_T = 0$
already returns $r$, because it is not oversampled, and it is the slow case --- nearest
neighbours $574$ samples away --- that the exclusion rescues, from a rank-blind $2.1$ to its
published $3.5$. And a transient need not have dimension one: a decaying \emph{oscillation}
returns $2.36$ at every exclusion, correctly, because at $k = 20$ the neighbourhood spans several
turns of the spiral and the two-dimensional sheet they lie on is what there is to see at that
resolution. What is general is the asymmetry, not the value.

\paragraph{A recurrence-based diagnostic.} $\hat d_{MG}(W_T{=}150)/\hat d_{MG}(W_T{=}20)$ is $1.000$ on
the fast case and $1.001$ on the slow, against $5.25$ on the transient, with no overlap over
forty-eight cells per case (largest recurrent $1.012$, smallest transient $4.71$); the less expensive
$\hat d_{MG}(100)/\hat d_{MG}(50)$ separates as cleanly. Unlike the trend-crossing count it tests
recurrence in the reconstructed space rather than non-monotonicity of the series, which is the gap
\cref{sec:diagnostics} leaves open and which \cref{app:eos} shows costs something on real runs. We
report it rather than adopt it: it is calibrated on the same systems that produced the two
diagnostics and has not been validated out of sample. The principled alternative is older than
either. A space--time separation plot \citep{provenzale1992distinguishing} shows directly how
neighbour distance depends on temporal separation, and reading $W_T$ off one would replace the rule
of \cref{sec:estimator} with a measurement; the variation above is a coarse version of that plot, and
choosing the exclusion this way rather than by rule is the obvious improvement to the protocol.

\section{Descent at the edge of stability}\label{app:eos}

Every system of \cref{sec:ladder} is driven, so the ladder establishes a conditional, and
\cref{sec:regime} asks whether anything undriven occupies the admissible regime. The one candidate
the literature offers is the edge of stability \citep{cohen2021gradient}: gradient descent at a
fixed rate sharpens until the top Hessian eigenvalue reaches $2/\eta$ and then hovers, and while it
hovers the loss descends on long scales and not on short ones. That is deterministic and not a
plain transient, which is the pair of conditions \cref{sec:classes} asks for.

\paragraph{Setting.} Full-batch descent, no weight decay, on the quadratic perceptron of
\cref{app:constructions}: modular addition at $p = 97$, width $500$, half the pairs held out, eight
rates by two seeds, $30\,000$ steps, summarised in \cref{tab:eos}. Two departures are deliberate.
The log is written at \emph{every} optimiser step, because the oscillation is the two-cycle of the
unstable mode and its period is a few steps, while every other full-batch log here is strided at
ten or more. And the sharpness is measured along the run by power iteration on Hessian-vector
products, taking the largest \emph{algebraic} eigenvalue: the Hessian is indefinite early in
training, when the most negative direction is the steeper, and plain power iteration then returns
a negative sharpness. The routine agrees with an exact eigendecomposition on a $120$-parameter
model to $6 \times 10^{-5}$ relative at the setting used.

\paragraph{Oscillation without recurrence.} The oscillation is real and not round-off:
the largest single-step rise is $0.99$ to $4.1$ times the loss itself, against a float32 relative
epsilon of $1.2 \times 10^{-7}$. But oscillation is not recurrence. Two of the seven
edge-of-stability runs return near states they have left; five trace a curve past the exclusion
exactly as the monotone runs do, and the return column of \cref{tab:eos} separates the two groups
by an order of magnitude with nothing between them.

\paragraph{Performance of the diagnostics.} On the training loss, the observer this
paper's full-batch analysis uses, the admissible rectangle of \cref{sec:diagnostics} admits all
five runs that do not recur and refuses both that do. The estimate agrees with the recurrence
measurement and not with the rectangle: $3.49$ and $2.44$ in the two runs that return, against
$29.2$ to $30.8$ --- the transient value of \cref{app:exclusion} --- in the five that do not.

The cause is visible in the inputs. Every run at the edge has a rise fraction of $0.50$, so the
trend-crossing count, which tests non-monotonicity and not recurrence, passes all of them, while
$\rho_{\mathrm{ident}}$ happens to equal unity in the five and at $1.23$ and $1.30$ in the two.
On the parameter norm the rectangle admits all seven. This is seven runs of one configuration on
one task and we draw no rule from it, but it is the failure mode \cref{sec:diagnostics} names,
occurring.

\paragraph{Dependence on the logging stride.} Read at the stride every other full-batch log here uses, the
phenomenon is largely erased: over these seven runs the median trend-crossing count falls
from $80$ to $2$ and the median rise fraction from $0.500$ to $0.000$ exactly, and where five of
the seven were admissible at every step, none is at a stride of ten and one is at fifty. The
collapse is not uniform --- the run with the most intermittent oscillation keeps a rise fraction
near $0.45$ under decimation, because its cycle is not a clean two-cycle to alias --- but whether a
log looks like a transient is in part a property of how often it was written.

\section{The ceiling: embedding dimension against record length}\label{app:ceiling}

\Cref{sec:ceiling} reports that the estimator saturates. Two explanations were available and the
paper could not choose between them: the embedding condition $E > 2d$, which at $E_{\max} = 20$
allows ten components, and the finite-record bound $D \lesssim 2\log_{10} N$
\citep{eckmann1992fundamental}, which at $N = 8000$ allows $7.8$. They are separable because each
is insensitive to the other's knob. We vary $E_{\max} \in \{10,14,20,28,40,56\}$ at $N = 8000$, and
$N \in \{10^3 \dots 6.4\times10^4\}$ at $E_{\max} = 20$, over $r \in \{2 \dots 20\}$ and three
seeds with everything else frozen; \cref{tab:ceiling} gives both. Each $N$ is a \emph{prefix} of
one longer record, not a resampling of a fixed span, so the delay lag keeps its meaning in periods;
all twenty-seven trajectories are bit-identical to the published ones over their common prefix, and
rescoring the published twenty-direction configuration returns $15.09$ against its published
$15.1$.

\paragraph{Outcome of the two scans.} The level at high rank is the embedding's: sixty-four times the
record adds $1.7$ components, where a factor of $5.6$ in $E_{\max}$ adds $7.2$. The tracking
ceiling is neither's. It never passes $10.4$ over the sixteen distinct $(E_{\max}, N)$ cells --- the
two scans above plus a third that repeats the record scan at $E_{\max} = 56$ --- and the cell
that attains it is $E_{\max} = 56$ at $N = 1000$, the \emph{shortest} record in the study, where
$2\log_{10}N$ allows $6.0$. A record-length bound cannot produce that, so
\citet{eckmann1992fundamental} is not the explanation here. Nor is $E > 2d$ in the form we used
it: the ceiling rises by $0.047$ components per unit of $E_{\max}$, not by a half, so $E_{\max}/2$
happens to be right at $E_{\max} = 20$ and is wrong at both ends --- which is
\citeauthor{sauer1993how}'s point in the other direction, that how many delay coordinates one
needs does not follow from the embedding theorem alone \citep{sauer1993how}.

Fitting the level at $r \ge 16$ separates the candidates outright (\cref{tab:ceilingfit}). Neither
hypothesis fits; their pointwise minimum fits no better, so they do not jointly bind either; and a
form logarithmic in both knobs fits an order of magnitude inside the data's own spread. Both
dependences are therefore logarithmic: $2.6$ components per doubling of $E_{\max}$, $1.0$ per
decade of record.

\paragraph{Two qualifications.} First, $E_{\max}$, $\tau$ and the delay span
$(E_{\max}-1)\tau$ are algebraically locked, and a third scan holding the span at $76$ while $\tau$
falls from $8$ to $1$ finds that $E_{\max}$ then adds nothing (regression slope $0.000$). The
operative variable looks like the temporal extent of the delay window, which is not what $E > 2d$
is about either; that scan is defective at both endpoints, so it does not settle the converse.

Second and more seriously, \emph{the linear null reproduces the whole dependence}.
$\PR_{\mathrm{delay}}$, a
spectral statistic of the delay covariance with no geometry in it and capped only by $E$, rises
from $5.33$ to $13.53$ across the $E_{\max}$ scan where MG rises from $6.94$ to $13.55$, and is
flat to two decimals across the record scan. It is fitted by $11.09\log_{10}E_{\max}$ with a
root-mean-square error of $0.09$, the tightest fit anywhere in this appendix. Most of what we have
been calling a ceiling is therefore how many components the delay window resolves \emph{linearly},
which is a spectral reading of the saturation and not a geometric one.

\paragraph{The unexplained tracking limit.} Why the tracking ceiling lies near ten components. It is not
$E_{\max}/2$, since it is $10.4$ at $E_{\max} = 56$; not $2\log_{10}N$, since it is $10.4$ at
$N = 1000$; and not the measured effective rank, which equals $r$ to $0.01$ at every $N \ge 2000$.
No form we fitted accounts for it, and the controls are clean --- the Theiler axis is inert (a
fixed-$76$ scan reproduces the frozen setting to two decimals at every cell), and none of the $3240$
cells is marked degenerate. This is a real limit of the instrument that we can bound but not name.

\Cref{sec:diagnostics}'s own ratio does see it: $\rho_{\mathrm{ident}}$ at $r = 20$ is $1.31$ to
$1.40$ at every record length, a longer record never making an unidentifiable estimate
identifiable, and falls from $1.22$ to $1.02$ as $E_{\max}$ grows, crossing the band at about the
rank where tracking stops.

\section{Compute cost and code availability}\label{app:compute}

\paragraph{Compute cost.} Everything here runs on one consumer GPU and one desktop CPU, in hours
rather than days. The largest GPU item is the edge-of-stability campaign of \cref{app:eos}:
sixteen runs of $30\,000$ full-batch steps, logged at every step and with the sharpness measured
three hundred times per run, in $1770$ seconds total on a single T4. The analysis is the more
expensive half and is all CPU, a few core-hours per experiment, the ceiling scan of
\cref{app:ceiling} the largest single item. Nothing in this paper needs a cluster, which is
deliberate: the instrument is meant to be inexpensive enough to run on a log one already has.

\paragraph{Cost of the trajectory sketch.} The claim of \cref{sec:direct} that measuring the
trajectory directly is affordable should carry a number. In storage it is exact arithmetic: at
each logged step the sketch keeps $1024$ coordinates in each of two spaces under two hash
families, $4096$ floats, against the full parameter vectors of \cref{app:runs} --- a factor of
$36$ for the perceptron and $55$ for the transformer. Over the $151$ logged steps of a
$1500$-step run that is $2.5$ against $88$ megabytes.

In time the overhead is below what we can measure. Training the same configuration with and without
the observer attached, alternating the order so that warm-up cannot favour either, and checking that
the two agree on every logged loss to the last bit, gives $41.75$ seconds bare against
$40.44$ probed --- a nominal overhead of $-3.1\,\%$. A negative overhead is not a result but a
bound: at the logging stride the transformer runs use, the cost of the sketch is under the
run-to-run variation of the machine, which here is a few per cent. \texttt{sketch\_cost.py}
records the measurement.

\paragraph{Code availability.} All code, configurations and result files behind every number and
figure in this paper will be released publicly on acceptance: the estimator and its frozen
configurations, the six constructed systems, the trajectory sketch, the training scripts for both
grokking settings, and the analysis scripts named throughout these appendices. The figures
regenerate from the committed result files with a single command.
