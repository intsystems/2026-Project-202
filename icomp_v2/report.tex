\begin{abstract}
Over a window of training, a neural network's parameter trajectory moves on a set far smaller
than the parameter space. We call the dimension of that set the active dimension of the window: a
count of independent components, distinct from the number of directions the optimiser may move
in, from the rank of the map to the outputs, and from the effective rank of the trajectory
covariance. A stored trajectory measures it directly but is expensive; a scalar log is free, and
we ask what survives the projection onto one. The instrument is standard, delay reconstruction
followed by a maximum likelihood intrinsic dimension, and its hazard is that it returns a
plausible number when no invariant set exists. On six constructed systems, ending with a
linear head on a frozen image classifier, recovery on withheld ranks and seeds reaches a mean
absolute error of $0.87$ components up to eight directions, and two diagnostics computed from the
series alone flag the two regimes in which the value may not be read as a count. Applied to
grokking they place both standard settings outside the admissible regime, for two different
reasons. Paying instead for the trajectory, the effective
rank collapses by a median factor of seven near generalisation in four regularised runs and then
re-expands, which is the simplification the mechanistic accounts of grokking lead one to expect;
their controls fall too, so what marks the transition is not its depth but that it is sharp,
locked to the transition and reversed. Re-run at a window matched to that transition rather than
to the record, the log estimate falls at the same step in the same four runs and in neither
control, by more than surrogates preserving the observer's shape produce: two signatures of one
event, one of them free.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A neural network is trained in a parameter space of dimension $P$, but the trajectory the optimiser
follows does not fill it. Over a window it moves on a much smaller set whose size changes over a
run, and we call its dimension the \emph{active dimension} of the window, defined in
\cref{eq:act}. It is a count of independent components: if the optimiser is carried by $r$
independent quasiperiodic phases the orbit closes onto an $r$-torus and the active dimension is
$r$, with no tunable scale. It is neither the dimension of the subspace the optimiser is confined
to, which the parameterisation fixes \citep{li2018measuring}, nor the rank of the map from it to
the outputs, nor the effective rank of the trajectory covariance, which anisotropy depresses below
the count; \cref{sec:three} separates the four.

Knowing it would settle questions currently argued with whichever of those four quantities is
cheapest to compute: whether an apparent simplification of training is a real reduction in degrees
of freedom or an artefact of a shrinking scale, and whether a phase transition can be seen without
instrumenting the run. Measuring it directly means storing the trajectory, which is expensive; a
scalar log costs nothing, and a run already keeps several, so we ask how much of the active
dimension survives the projection onto one. The instrument is standard, delay reconstruction
\citep{takens1981detecting, sauer1991embedology} followed by the pooled Levina--Bickel estimator
\citep{levina2004maximum, mackay2005comments}, and its hazard is that it returns a plausible number
when no invariant set exists: power-law noise yields a finite reproducible dimension set by its
spectral exponent \citep{osborne1989finite}, and a decaying transient, which is what a converging
run looks like, yields a large one. Most of the work below goes into deciding which case one has,
beginning with a validation protocol fixed before any result (\cref{sec:protocol}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item We separate four quantities frequently identified with one another: the dimension of the
subspace the optimiser is confined to, the rank of the map from it to the outputs, the number of
independent components of the set it visits, and the effective rank of its covariance
(\cref{sec:three}). Only the third is estimated here; the fourth is measured beside it on every
system so the two cannot be conflated.
\item We evaluate the estimator on six systems of increasing realism, ending with a constrained
head on a network trained on image data (\cref{sec:ladder}). It recovers the active dimension to a
mean absolute error of $0.87$ components up to eight directions, without per-system calibration,
on withheld ranks and seeds, and locates the ceiling above which it cannot (\cref{sec:ceiling}).
\item We measure the conditions the absolute value requires (\cref{sec:validity}) and validate two
diagnostics that flag the two failure regimes from the series alone.
\item Applied to grokking (\cref{sec:grokking}), the diagnostics place both published settings
outside the regime in which a scalar log could be read as a count, while the trajectory effective
rank, measured directly, collapses near generalisation and re-expands in four regularised runs.
At a window matched to that transition the log estimate falls with it, in the same runs and not in
their controls, and survives a surrogate control on the observer's shape (\cref{sec:matched}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reconstruction and intrinsic dimension.} Delay reconstruction rests on Takens' theorem
and its extensions \citep{takens1981detecting, sauer1991embedology, stark1999delay,
stark2003delay}, is used on empirical series \citep{sugihara1990nonlinear, sugihara2012detecting},
and takes its lag and dimension from mutual information, false nearest neighbours or singular
spectra \citep{fraser1986independent, kennel1992determining, cao1997practical,
broomhead1986extracting}. We use pooled maximum likelihood \citep{levina2004maximum,
mackay2005comments}, not the alternatives \citep{grassberger1983measuring, facco2017estimating,
camastra2016intrinsic}; a finite record bounds them all \citep{eckmann1992fundamental}.
Oversampling \citep{theiler1986spurious} and power-law noise \citep{osborne1989finite} produce
spurious dimensions; surrogate tests \citep{theiler1992testing, schreiber1996improved} detect
nonlinear structure but not its amount, missing the transient regime entirely.

\paragraph{Dimension in optimisation.} Random-subspace training \citep{li2018measuring,
aghajanyan2021intrinsic} reports the smallest $k$ that still solves a task, a property of the task
and not of a window; we borrow the parameterisation and measure something else inside it. Nearest
to the active dimension is the finding that descent occurs largely in a small subspace spanned by
the top Hessian eigenvectors \citep{gurari2018gradient, sagun2018empirical}. The intrinsic
dimension of data or of representations \citep{ansuini2019intrinsic, pope2021intrinsic} is a point
cloud's, not a trajectory's, and the participation ratio we adopt \citep{gao2017theory} is not the
spectral-entropy quantity of the same name \citep{roy2007effective}. The one prior measurement of
our object we know of is incidental: two principal components carry over $98\,\%$ of a grokking
trajectory's variance \citep{notsawo2023predicting}. All of these need weights, gradients or
activations; the nearest precedent for a scalar computed during training and acted on is the
gradient noise scale \citep{mccandlish2018empirical}. Mini-batch descent near a minimum is modelled
as a stochastic process \citep{mandt2017stochastic}, our third regime; whether full-batch descent
at the edge of stability \citep{cohen2021gradient} recurs as the first regime needs, we measure
(\cref{app:eos}).

\paragraph{Grokking.} Delayed generalisation \citep{power2022grokking} is studied in two settings
and we use both. The first is regularised and, in our version, stochastic: the transformer
configuration of \citet{nanda2023progress}, also used by \citet{liu2022omnigrok}, on modular
addition and on the $S_5$ task of \citet{power2022grokking}. The second is the unregularised
full-batch gradient descent of \citet{gromov2023grokking}, whose closed form supplies an analytic
reference, on the modular polynomials and representability criterion of \citet{doshi2024grokking},
which supply label-matched pairs; \cref{app:runs} states where each departs from its source.
Explanations sit in weight or activation space \citep{nanda2023progress, varma2023explaining,
liu2022towards, barak2022hidden} or in a lazy-to-rich transition \citep{kumar2024grokking}.
Closest to us, \citet{notsawo2023predicting} predict grokking from early loss oscillations
\citep{thilak2022slingshot} and \citet{merrill2023tale} track competing subnetworks. We estimate a
dynamical quantity with an independently measured ground truth.

\section{Problem statement}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Four quantities, and the one we estimate}\label{sec:three}

Let $\vtheta_t \in \R^P$ be the parameter vector at step $t$, confined by construction or by the
dynamics to an affine subspace $\vtheta = \vtheta_0 + \mV^{\top}\vc$ with
$\mV \in \R^{k \times P}$ orthonormal. Over a window $\gW$ the \emph{available} dimension is $k$,
fixed by the parameterisation. The \emph{functional} dimension, the rank of
$\partial \vf(\vc)/\partial \vc$ for $\vf$ the outputs on a fixed probe set, bounds how many of
those directions can change the computed function: a bound we report and do not estimate. The
\emph{active} dimension is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:act}
  d_{\mathrm{act}}(\gW) \;=\; \dim \gM_{\gW},
\end{equation}
where $\gM_{\gW}$ is the set the trajectory visits inside the window: the support of the empirical
measure of $\{\vc_t : t \in \gW\}$, coarsened to a scale $\varepsilon$, with $\dim$ the
box-counting dimension at that scale. The resolution is part of the definition and does not go to
zero, since a neighbour statistic sees the set at the scale of its own neighbourhoods. If the
parameters depend on $r$ independent quasiperiodic phases,
$\vc_t = F(\varphi_1(t), \dots, \varphi_r(t))$, and the window is long compared with the recurrence
time, the visited set is an $r$-torus and $d_{\mathrm{act}} = r$ for every $\varepsilon$ below the
smallest mode amplitude, where the box-counting and manifold dimensions agree. Above that scale the
count falls, a property of the estimand and not a defect of the estimator (\cref{sec:aniso}); below
the recurrence time it is $1$, since the closure of a short orbit segment is a curve whatever $r$
is. So defined, $d_{\mathrm{act}}$ is fixed by the dynamics in both deterministic regimes of
\cref{sec:classes} and by the realisation of the drive in the third. What that section settles is
therefore whether the quantity is the optimiser's at all and, where it is, whether one scalar
record identifies it --- which is why the diagnostics of \cref{sec:diagnostics} come before any
number.

A fourth quantity is measured throughout and is not the active dimension. For a non-negative
spectrum $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ the participation ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pr}
  \PR(\lambda) \;=\; \frac{\bigl(\sum_i \lambda_i\bigr)^2}{\sum_i \lambda_i^2}
\end{equation}
equals $m$ exactly when $m$ of the $\lambda_i$ are equal and the rest vanish. On
$\operatorname{spec} \Cov\{\vc_t : t \in \gW\}$ it gives the \emph{effective rank}
$\PR^{\mathrm{pos}}(\gW)$, which is what the term means below: $\PR^{\mathrm{det}}$ is the same
after the per-window linear detrend a drifting trajectory needs, $\PR^{\mathrm{upd}}$ the same on
the increments. It is not a count, equalling $r$ when $r$ directions carry comparable variance and
falling below $r$ when they do not, while $d_{\mathrm{act}}$ stays at $r$ (\cref{sec:aniso}); it
verifies that a construction excites the $r$ directions claimed, and \cref{sec:direct} measures
it.
All four differ measurably: in \cref{sec:digits} the available and functional dimensions are both
$10$, and removing the preconditioner leaves them and the injected rank intact while stochastic
forcing takes the effective rank from $7.98$ to $6.43$.

\subsection{Observation model and estimator}\label{sec:estimator}

We observe a scalar series $x_t = \phi(\vs_t)$, with $\vs_t$ the full optimiser state and $\phi$
fixed before the run, and reconstruct $\vy_t = (x_t, \dots, x_{t-(E-1)\tau}) \in \R^E$. For a
generic pair of dynamics and observation on a compact invariant manifold of integer dimension $d$
this map is an embedding once $E > 2d$ \citep{takens1981detecting}; the prevalence form replaces
$d$ by the box-counting dimension, which need not be an integer, and adds conditions on periodic
orbits \citep{sauer1991embedology}. Genericity is not verifiable for a $\phi$ fixed in advance, so
every result below is reported over a family of observers. We estimate the intrinsic dimension of
$\{\vy_t\}$ by maximum likelihood on nearest-neighbour distances \citep{levina2004maximum}. With
$r^{(i)}_1 \le \dots \le r^{(i)}_m$ the distances from point $i$ to its $m$ nearest neighbours,
subject to a Theiler exclusion $W_T$ on their separation in time
\citep{theiler1986spurious, kantz2004nonlinear}, the local estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb}
  \hat{d}_i \;=\; \Biggl[ \frac{1}{m-1} \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}
  \log \frac{r^{(i)}_m}{r^{(i)}_j} \Biggr]^{-1},
\end{equation}
and the $N$ local estimates combine in two ways. Averaging them, as \citet{levina2004maximum}
propose, gives $\hat{d}_{\mathrm{LB}} = N^{-1} \sum_i \hat{d}_i$. Pooling the likelihood first, as
\citet{mackay2005comments} argue one should, averages the reciprocals,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg}
  \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}^{\,-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{N(m-1)} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}
  \log \frac{r^{(i)}_m}{r^{(i)}_j} .
\end{equation}
We write LB and MG for the two, \emph{the estimate} for what MG returns, \emph{the estimator} for
the procedure. Under the local Poisson model behind both, $\hat{d}_i^{\,-1}$ is unbiased where
$\hat{d}_i$ is not, so LB should exceed MG by $(m-1)/(m-2)$, under six per cent at the $m = 20$ used
here; \cref{tab:alts} reports both and \cref{app:alg} gives the algorithm as implemented. Three
further statistics accompany every estimate, since each names an effect that can produce a
dimension signal with no geometry: the \emph{roughness}
$\operatorname{std}(\Delta x)/\operatorname{std}(x)$, the \emph{linear participation ratio}
$\PR_{\mathrm{delay}}$ of \cref{eq:pr} on $\Cov\{\vy_t\}$, and the \emph{spectral participation
ratio} of \cref{eq:pr} on the power spectrum of $x$.

\subsection{Dynamical regimes}\label{sec:classes}

Two conditions must hold and they are not the same. Neither is a condition for $d_{\mathrm{act}}$
to be the optimiser's; both are conditions for reading it off one scalar record. The theorems require a
deterministic flow on a compact invariant set, which licenses the delay map as an embedding. The
neighbour statistic needs more: that the record visit that set densely enough for a point's nearest
neighbours to be returns rather than its own temporal neighbours, which the Theiler exclusion
enforces and \cref{sec:diagnostics} tests. Recurrence enters here and only here. It is no
requirement of the estimator, which asks only for points drawn locally from the set and would take
them from independent runs; it is a requirement of a protocol whose whole sample is one trajectory
and which then discards every temporally adjacent point. An optimiser near a solution may be in any
of the three regimes of \cref{tab:classes}, and only the first meets both conditions.

The third regime is what mini-batch gradient noise produces, and theory serves it worse than it
looks. The embedding theorems for forced systems \citep{stark1999delay, stark2003delay} cover it
in part: the update may depend on the state arbitrarily, and their iterated-function-system form
admits a driver selecting among finitely many maps, as sampling a finite dataset does. But they
deliver an embedding \emph{fibre by fibre}, one per realisation of the drive, and a single run
crosses fibres, so its cloud is not confined to the image of any one and no invariant measure
exists there to sample. Coloured noise alone yields a finite reproducible dimension set by its
spectral exponent \citep{osborne1989finite}. The second regime is less often noticed. A decaying
transient traces a curve it never returns to, so it has no $\gM_{\gW}$; its effective rank
measures $1.03$ to $1.06$ in \cref{sec:digits} only because the curve is nearly straight.

\subsection{Admissibility diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostics}

Two statistics computed from the series alone flag the two failure regimes. The
\textbf{identifiability ratio}
$\rho_{\mathrm{ident}} = \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}(2E_{\max}) / \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}(E_{\max})$ is
near unity when a dimension is resolvable and grows when the estimate is a property of the embedding space rather
than of the data, which catches the stochastic regime. On a transient it is
near unity too, for the wrong reason, and the \textbf{trend-crossing count} separates the two: the
number of sign changes of the window's residual about its least-squares line, near zero there. It
tests non-monotonicity of the series, not recurrence in the reconstructed space, so the pair must
be read together. A third quantity is reported beside them but tests no regime. The
\textbf{degeneracy flag} fires when more than one per cent of neighbour distances or per-point
log-ratio sums reach their numerical floors: a conditioning guard against quantised or constant
observers. Reference values are in \cref{sec:diagnostics-validated}.

\section{Validation protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

Six requirements were fixed before any result, and each is named so that \cref{tab:ladder} can say
which a system fails. (1)~\emph{Constructed truth}: every system is built with $r$ independent
quasiperiodic phases, so $d_{\mathrm{act}} = r$, and the effective rank is measured on the recorded
trajectory to confirm all $r$ are comparably excited. (2)~\emph{Frozen configuration}: the
estimator is selected once, on a grid of estimator parameters only, since a grid over system
parameters changes the data when one is chosen. (3)~\emph{Disjoint split}: in seed and in rank,
since withholding seeds alone leaves the excitation geometry shared whenever it is a function of
$r$. (4)~\emph{Silence control}: a zero learning rate must silence the observer, which otherwise
reads the drive through the residual and not the optimiser. (5)~\emph{Companion statistics}: the
three of \cref{sec:estimator} are reported on every call, and one performing comparably is reported
as such. (6)~\emph{Matched bandwidth}: across ranks, or a construction in which each added
direction adds a higher frequency lets the roughness alone order the rank.

\section{Evaluation on systems with a known active dimension}\label{sec:ladder}

Six systems of increasing realism are used, and \cref{tab:ladder} collects the outcome. In each the
orbit closes onto an $r$-torus by construction, the measured effective rank confirms it, and the
estimator sees one scalar series.

\subsection{An oscillating diagonal matrix}\label{sec:matrix}

The first system has no optimiser and no feedback, so a negative result is decisive. In
$\mW(t) = \operatorname{diag}(b_i + \delta_i(t))$, $r$ coordinates oscillate at rationally
independent frequencies over offsets that must be non-zero. On its two held-out seeds the mean
absolute error over $r = 1, \dots, 10$ is $0.30$ and $0.69$. It also supplies the geometry control: $r$ coordinates driven
from one phase have one degree of freedom, and the estimate separates them from four independent
oscillators by a factor of $3.4$, against $5\,\%$ for the roughness. Smoothness cannot do that
work.

\subsection{The ceiling}\label{sec:ceiling}

The estimator saturates, and the ladder locates where. The matrix error rises to $1.51$ at ten
directions; a $D = 64$ linear model and a logistic model order the rank to twenty but saturate
above about twelve; the image-data system at twenty directions saturates at $15.1$. The condition
of \cref{sec:estimator} is $E > 2d$, so $E_{\max} = 20$ cannot resolve more than ten components
and $E_{\max} = 40$ cannot resolve twenty, and the observed ceilings agree: above about nine at
$E_{\max} = 20$, and $15.1$ against a measured $19.99$ at $E_{\max} = 40$, where $E = 2d$ exactly
and the condition fails. The twenty-direction result is therefore not an anomaly; that design
cannot recover twenty components. The explanation is not settled, since a finite record also bounds
the measurable dimension \citep{eckmann1992fundamental}, comparably so at this window, and
separating the two needs the sweep of \cref{sec:concl}.

\subsection{The silence control}\label{sec:silence}

Requirement~4 asks that a zero learning rate silence the observer. Most published
dimension-on-training-logs analyses do not run it, and it invalidated two of our six systems: a
trainable $\vz$ through a frozen decoder, and a perceptron restricted to
$\vtheta = \vtheta_0 + \mU \vz$, both read naively succeed, yet at zero learning rate each
reproduces its reported result almost exactly, correlating above $0.98$ at every rank, because the
drive reaches the observers through the residual and not through the optimiser. It also rejects the
instantaneous mini-batch loss of \cref{sec:digits}, otherwise among the best three observers. Two
further systems fall to requirement~2, their configuration having been selected on a grid that
varied the system as well as the estimator (\cref{tab:ladder}, \cref{app:validity}).

\subsection{A constrained head on a network trained on image data}\label{sec:digits}

The sixth system uses real inputs: a tanh network trained on the scikit-learn handwritten digits
and frozen, with a linear head confined to a $k$-dimensional affine subspace under softmax
cross-entropy, so the curvature is data-dependent and all dynamics occur in $\vc$. The excitation
is made $r$-dimensional by modulating the loss weights of $r$ of twelve fixed data groups and
orthogonalising the resulting gradient directions (\cref{app:constructions}), after which the
measured effective rank equals $r$ to within $0.013$ components. Nine excitation arms are run,
spanning the three regimes of \cref{tab:classes}; \cref{fig:regimes} shows five.

Twelve observers are recorded (\cref{app:obs}); the estimator's five parameters were frozen on four
of them and on reserved seeds and ranks, so what follows is out-of-sample in rank and seed
throughout and in observer for six of the ten scored. In the deterministic recurrent regime the
mean absolute error is $0.87$ components run by run and $0.47$ after aggregating seeds, at a median
slope of $0.92$ and a perfect rank correlation for all ten. The two parameter norms, which are what
a run ordinarily logs, sit in the middle of that range and are also the only observers whose
roughness orders the withheld ranks, so for the cheapest observer a smoothness explanation cannot
be excluded. Twenty available directions give the same picture with a lower ceiling, saturating at
$15.1$ against a measured $19.99$ at Spearman $+0.97$; that arm is exploratory, having no seed
split and a Theiler exclusion smaller than its own embedding span (\cref{app:theiler}). A second
construction in function space is the most accurate row of \cref{tab:ladder}, its rank set by an
external teacher.

\subsection{Dynamical regime}\label{sec:regime}

\Cref{fig:regimes} evaluates the estimator in each regime of \cref{tab:classes} at the same nominal
rank, observer and matched octave. Under stochastic excitation the estimate is flat in the rank and
the error exceeds ten components, responding to $E_{\max}$, the correlation time and the record
length. Weak noise destroys the absolute value on a recurrent trajectory and leaves the ordering:
at a quarter of the pure noise amplitude, injected in the same $r$ directions and leaving the
effective rank at $r$, the error rises tenfold while the Spearman correlation stays above $+0.96$.
A decaying transient returns near no state it has left, so the set it visits is a curve and
$d_{\mathrm{act}} = 1$. The estimate reads $29$, and the exclusion is why: removing it returns
$1.197$ at every rank, seed and observer, against the $1.19928$ maximum likelihood must give on a
uniformly sampled curve, while the recurrent arms do not move (\cref{app:exclusion}). Nor is $29$ a
level; it is a divergent quantity read at the exclusion cap. Exceeding $E_{\max}$ is not a bound
failing: no clamping is applied (\cref{app:alg}), and a maximum likelihood fit exceeds the
embedding dimension when its model is violated. Every system here is driven and training is not, so
whether anything undriven is admissible we test at the edge of stability
\citep{cohen2021gradient}: the perceptron of \cref{sec:grokking} reaches it, the sharpness
self-limiting at $2/\eta$ while the loss rises on half of all steps, but oscillation is not
recurrence --- two of seven such runs return near states they have left, and the diagnostics admit
the five that do not and refuse the two that do (\cref{app:eos}).

\subsection{The delay lag}\label{sec:tau}

The delay window spans $(E_{\max}-1)\tau$ samples and must cover a real fraction of the
oscillation period, or the delay coordinates are collinear and the torus does not unfold. That the
window rather than the lag matters is standard \citep{casdagli1991state}. On a fixed torus of
period $400$ at eight active directions, sweeping $\tau$ alone moves the estimate from $2.60$ to
$63.35$, with $8.53$ at the span of four tenths of a period that is right (\cref{app:validity}).
The correct lag follows from the oscillation period, which a controlled experiment knows and a
training log does not, and we do not solve this. The lag applied in \cref{sec:grokking} is the
frozen $\tau = 4$ of \cref{app:config}, transplanted from a calibration system of drive period
$16$; the autocorrelation time of the parameter norm on those logs is $161$ to $858$, so a matched
lag would have been an order of magnitude larger, which alone forbids reading the absolute value
there whatever the regime. The identifiability ratio inherits this: doubling $E_{\max}$ doubles the
delay span too, so a large value means either that the estimate belongs to the embedding space or
that the lag is mismatched, and on the grokking logs both readings are live.

\subsection{Nuisance factors}\label{sec:nuisance}

At a fixed active dimension of four we vary seven factors that leave it unchanged, over five seeds
and twelve observers, against a real four-component change (\cref{app:validity}). Two matter. The
smoothness control passes on eight of ten observers --- an octave shift of the drive band changes
the roughness fourfold and moves the estimate only to the level of seed-to-seed variability --- and
fails on the two parameter norms, whose roughness orders the withheld ranks perfectly. And a
\emph{ramped} observer gain, unlike a constant rescaling, moves the estimate by $1.16$ components,
so a run whose parameter norm grows drifts by about one component with nothing dimensional
occurring: the nuisance that matters most in \cref{sec:grokking}.

\subsection{The estimand is a count, not an effective rank}\label{sec:aniso}

Every construction in \cref{sec:ladder} equalises the drive amplitudes, making the active dimension
and the effective rank of \cref{sec:three} numerically equal, and an experiment in which they agree
cannot say which the estimator reports. Driving mode $l$ at amplitude $q^{l}$ separates them: the
orbit still closes onto an $r$-torus, so $d_{\mathrm{act}} = r$, while the effective rank falls to
$(\sum_l a_l^2)^2/\sum_l a_l^4$. The estimate follows the count. At four active directions the rank
falls from $4.00$ to $1.65$ as $q$ goes from $1$ to $0.5$ while the estimate stays between $3.82$
and $4.06$, and over the grid it is closer to the count than to the rank, $1.02$ against $1.67$
(\cref{app:validity}). It counts only what it can resolve: at eight directions with the weakest
mode under a hundredth of the strongest, the estimate falls with the rank from $8.88$ to $4.86$,
the torus it can see having fewer dimensions than the one built.

\paragraph{The diagnostics.}\label{sec:diagnostics-validated} With no ground truth, the
identifiability ratio separates the accurate regimes, $1.00$ to $1.09$, from the inaccurate, $1.32$
to $1.57$, and under the slow drive within a regime too, reading $0.99$ where the estimate is
correct and $1.52$ where it has saturated. It is not sufficient alone, reading $1.00$ on the
transient, and must be paired with the trend-crossing count, near zero there. Both are calibrated
on these five arms only. The degeneracy flag fires in a majority of windows on eleven of the
thirty-five cells of \cref{sec:grokking}.

\section{Application to grokking}\label{sec:grokking}

We work in both settings of \cref{sec:related}, which differ in every relevant respect
(\cref{app:runs} lists every run and every departure from the sources). The transformer runs use
AdamW and cross-entropy at weight decay $1.0$ on modular addition and $0.2$ on $S_5$, on
mini-batches of $256$ where the source is full-batch, which makes them the stochastic setting of
\cref{tab:classes}; the perceptron runs are unregularised full-batch descent on mean squared error,
and we take the tasks and representability criterion of \citet{doshi2024grokking} but not their
optimiser (\cref{app:repr}). The diagnostics refuse the level both settings' logs report, so we pay
for the trajectory, and then return to the logs with the one question a refused level permits.

\subsection{The diagnostics refuse both settings}\label{sec:grok-diagnostics}

The frozen configuration of \cref{sec:digits}, with only the window and stride shortened to fit
these records, to a third of each and $1\,000$, gives \cref{tab:grok-diagnostics} and
\cref{fig:map}. The regularised stochastic runs sit in a band of the identifiability ratio, $1.29$
to $1.60$, that the deterministic regimes never reach, and their estimates span a factor of four:
set by $E_{\max}$ and the record, not a rank, as \cref{fig:regimes} predicts for stochastic
excitation. The full-batch runs carry a transient's signature instead: identifiability ratio unity,
roughness of one part in a thousand, two crossings per window. In neither is the value a count.

A change inside a run might still be read, since the configuration and with it the level offset are
constant within a run; the floor such a change must clear is the observer's own drift, $1.16$
components by \cref{sec:nuisance}. At this window it cannot be tested, for want of resolution and
not of signal: the window spans $39\,990$ optimiser steps against a transition hundreds wide
(\cref{app:window}). \Cref{sec:matched} shortens it.

\paragraph{Label-matched pairs.}\label{sec:pairs} The perceptron setting supplies the control the
first cannot: both members of a pair fit the training set completely under identical optimisation
and differ by one monomial. The median estimate is about three units higher for the
non-generalising member in all four pairs with no overlap between the groups, but it is not a
count --- all eight runs are transient, the separation is already in the raw loss curves, and the
direct measurement does not reproduce it. Nor is a fall specific to generalisation: one occurs in
a $200\,000$-step run at $p = 211$ that never leaves chance accuracy
(\cref{app:pairs,app:fb,app:falls}).

\subsection{The trajectory simplifies at generalisation}\label{sec:direct}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_dip.pdf}

A stored trajectory delivers what a log cannot: the effective rank of \cref{sec:three} is defined
whether or not the orbit recurs. We sketch it in parameter and in function space at every logged
step of a transformer trained to grok and report $\PR^{\mathrm{det}}$ (\cref{app:sketch}). Near
generalisation it collapses (\cref{fig:dip}): in function space by a factor of $5.2$ to $12.2$,
median $7.0$, reaching bottom $235$ to $712$ steps after the generalisation step, and in parameter
space to between $1.09$ and $1.60$, $150$ to $800$ steps later again; both then re-expand
(\cref{tab:dip}).
Two things support the reading. It sits at generalisation in seeds whose generalisation step
differs by a factor of $3.7$ and in a second task, so it does not track training progress, and the
displacement reaches its floor four thousand steps later and never recovers while the rank does,
so the trajectory has not simply stopped.

None of this is $d_{\mathrm{act}}$: what concentrates on one direction is the variation inside the
window, not the set the orbit visits.

\paragraph{The controls settle instead.}\label{sec:nowd} In the transition window they fall by
$1.6$ and $1.3$, and over their whole budget by $3.2$ and $7.1$, the second inside the range of the
generalising runs, so the groups differ in the timing and shape of the fall and not its depth: both peak early and then settle, to $6.6$ in modular
addition and $8.1$ in $S_5$, while their parameter norm never comes back down, ending at $1.7$ and
$2.6$ times its initial value where every generalising run peaks and gives back a third to half of
its growth. A low rank is therefore reached both by a run that has stopped moving and by one about
to generalise. That growing norm is also the ramped gain of \cref{sec:nuisance}, the mechanism
behind the scalar-log fall of \cref{sec:pairs}.

\subsection{The log falls where the trajectory does}\label{sec:matched}

Setting the window in units of the transition instead of the record makes the test of
\cref{sec:grok-diagnostics} possible. We re-run the estimator on the same logs at the window the
direct measurement uses, $600$ steps, centred on its own window midpoints, so the two statistics
are sampled at the same instants of the same runs. That window will not carry $E_{\max} = 20$ at
$\tau = 4$, whose delay span alone exceeds it, so a configuration must be chosen; we report all
thirty-six cells of a grid and take as headline the one a rule blind to the outcome selects
(\cref{app:window}).

On the parameter norm the estimate falls at the generalisation step in all four generalising runs,
from $2.5$--$2.9$ components to $1.1$--$1.3$, its floor $35$ to $712$ steps after the transition,
the same side and order as the direct measurement's $235$ to $712$ (\cref{fig:dip}d); the controls
fall by $1.00$ and $1.05$. It is among the largest falls of its kind in each run --- evaluated at
every window centre after memorisation, the value at $t_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is at the $0.89$ to $0.99$
quantile of that run's own distribution against $0.46$ and $0.38$ in the controls --- and
twenty-five of the twenty-six admissible cells separate the groups. Nor is it
the observer's shape, the nuisance \cref{sec:nuisance} identifies: against thirty-nine surrogates
keeping the log's smooth shape and its fluctuation spectrum, and over five independent seedings,
every generalising cell's $p$ lies below every control cell's --- at most $0.125$ against at least
$0.500$ --- at each of three smoothing lengths.

Two signatures of one event, then, one paid for with a stored trajectory and one free. Three things
keep the second modest. Its level is not a count, the identifiability ratio being uncomputable at
this window, so only the change is read. The observer is the parameter norm, for which
\cref{sec:nuisance} cannot exclude a smoothness explanation, so the surrogate control carries the
claim. And a simpler statistic sees the event better: the roughness of the same windows falls by
$6.5$ to $55$ where the estimate falls by $2$, so most of what this log carries is that its
innovation collapses, which needs no embedding.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

In a deterministic recurrent system with a matched delay lag, one scalar log of a suitable observer
counts the independent components of the set the optimiser visits, to a mean absolute error of
$0.87$ components up to eight of them. Outside that regime what comes back is not that count, for
two different reasons: on a transient the visited set is a curve and the estimate is thirty times
its dimension; under stochastic forcing there is no invariant set to have one. What makes it usable
is therefore the pair of diagnostics beside it. Applied to grokking they place both standard
settings outside the admissible regime; measured directly instead, the trajectory effective rank
collapses by a median factor of seven near generalisation and re-expands in four regularised runs,
much less in their controls, and at a matched window the free scalar estimate falls with it in the
same four runs and neither control.

\paragraph{What this does not establish.} The count needs a recurrent regime and a lag matched to a
period a log does not reveal, it is ordinal above eight components, and the configuration is a
choice as much as a measurement (\cref{app:config}). The collapse rests on four runs against two
controls differing in the variable that also drives the parameter norm, is a signature and not an
early warning, and does not reproduce in the second setting; the matched-window fall is near the
$1.16$ a ramped gain alone produces, and one of its controls has almost no range to fall through
(\cref{app:fb,app:window}). The edge-of-stability runs are seven, of one configuration on one task.

\section{The estimator}\label{app:alg}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Pooled Levina--Bickel intrinsic dimension of a delay reconstruction}
\label{alg:mg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require series $x_{1:n}$; lag $\tau$; embedding dimension $E$; neighbours $m$; Theiler
exclusion $W_T$; dither $\sigma$
\State $z \gets (x - \operatorname{mean} x) / \operatorname{std} x$;\quad
       $z \gets z + \sigma \epsilon$, $\epsilon_t \sim \gN(0,1)$ \Comment{breaks exact ties}
\State $\vy_t \gets (z_t, z_{t-\tau}, \dots, z_{t-(E-1)\tau})$ for $t = (E-1)\tau+1, \dots, n$
\State $S \gets 0$; $N \gets 0$; $n_{\mathrm{dist}} \gets 0$; $n_{\mathrm{ratio}} \gets 0$
\For{each $\vy_i$}
  \State $r_1 \le \dots \le r_m \gets$ distances to the $m$ nearest $\vy_j$ with $|i-j| > W_T$
  \State $r_j \gets \max(r_j, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{dist}})$; count those clipped into
         $n_{\mathrm{dist}}$
  \State $S_i \gets \max\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1} \log(r_m / r_j),\,
         \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ratio}}\bigr)$; count clips into $n_{\mathrm{ratio}}$
  \State $S \gets S + S_i$; \quad $N \gets N + 1$
\EndFor
\State flag the window degenerate if $n_{\mathrm{dist}}$ or $n_{\mathrm{ratio}}$ exceeds
       $1\,\%$ of its denominator
\State \Return $\bigl(N(m-1) - 1\bigr) / S$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Three details of the implementation are not cosmetic. The returned value is
$(N(m-1)-1)/S$ rather than $N(m-1)/S$, which is the exactly unbiased pooled maximum
likelihood estimate for a $\Gamma(N(m-1), 1/d)$ sample and differs from \cref{eq:mg} by a
factor $1 - 1/(N(m-1))$, below $10^{-5}$ at the sizes used here. A flagged window is still
returned rather than discarded. The ladder pipelines drop flagged windows when a series is
summarised over its sliding windows; the two grokking pipelines take the median over all of
them, which changes nothing there because no published cell of
\cref{tab:grok-diagnostics} contains a flagged window. And no clamping of the
returned value to $E$ is applied, so a divergent estimate is reported as divergent: clamping
would convert the transient regime of \cref{tab:classes} into a plausible number.

The floors are $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dist}} = 10^{-8}$ on a neighbour distance and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ratio}} = 10^{-5}$ on the per-point log-ratio sum, with $\sigma =
10^{-9}$ on the standardised series. The Theiler exclusion is
$W_T = \max\bigl((E-1)\tau,\, t_{\mathrm{acf}}\bigr)$, where $t_{\mathrm{acf}}$ is the first
lag at which the autocorrelation falls below $1/e$, capped at $150$ samples because the
neighbour query cost is linear in it. \Cref{app:theiler} measures what that cap costs.

The selection objective in both cases is the error of the median estimate against the median
measured effective rank, over the four observers reserved for selection, one from each
observer family. The eight-direction objective adds a penalty on the rank correlation and on
the spread across seeds; at the twenty-direction configuration both penalties are identically
zero, so the objective there reduces to the error alone.

Four properties of these choices should be stated with them.

\emph{Both sit at a grid boundary.} The eight-direction configuration is at the top of all
five swept axes and the twenty-direction one at the top of both. That is less alarming than it
sounds for the first, since four of its five axes have only two levels, so every configuration
in that grid is at a boundary in four of them; $\tau$ is the only axis with an interior value,
and the winner is at its top end. It does mean neither configuration is interior, and that a
wider grid could move the level.

\emph{The selection error spans a factor of five on identical data.} Across the
eight-direction grid the error runs from $0.32$ to $1.50$ on the same logs, so the level is a
choice as much as a measurement, and only the withheld numbers of \cref{sec:digits} may be read
as recovery.

\emph{The Theiler axis is inert on this data.} The autocorrelation rule returns the larger of
the embedding span $(E_{\max}-1)\tau$ and the measured autocorrelation time. On these logs the
autocorrelation time is one to three samples while the embedding span is nine or more, so the
two settings return bit-identical values at all $24$ points of the grid where they are
compared. The $48$-point grid therefore contains $24$ distinct measurements, and the choice of
Theiler rule is not a choice at all here.

\emph{Neither configuration always uses the window its parameters name.} At
$E_{\max}=40$ and $\tau=16$ the requested exclusion is $624$ samples, which the implementation
truncates to its cap of $150$ for the cost reason given in \cref{app:alg}. The
eight-direction configuration requests $76$ and is uncapped on every system of
\cref{sec:ladder}, but on the training logs of \cref{sec:grokking} the autocorrelation branch of
the rule asks for $161$ to $858$ and is likewise truncated to $150$. \cref{app:theiler}
measures what the truncation costs.

\Cref{tab:alts} should be read with three things in mind, and none of them favours MG.

First, MG is not the most accurate statistic at either range. The uncorrected per-point average
is ahead of it at both, by $0.02$ components at eight directions and by $0.27$ at twenty. The
correction of \cref{sec:estimator} removes a positive bias, and on a saturating estimate that
bias was partly cancelling the saturation, so removing it costs accuracy exactly where the
estimate is already saturating.

Second, MG is not the best-ordering statistic at twenty directions. The spectral participation
ratio at $256$ and $1024$ bands orders the ranks more faithfully than MG does, at three times
the error. If only the ordering is wanted, a statistic requiring no embedding and no neighbour
search will supply it.

Third, MG's advantage over the linear delay participation ratio is a factor of about two at
eight directions, not the factor of six that the selection split of \cref{app:config}
reports. That larger figure is computed on the ranks and seeds the configuration was chosen on
and is a property of the selection, not a recovery result.

\paragraph{The advantage depends on the observer.} Running the same torus through two scalar
observers that differ only in how evenly they weight the modes settles which statistic is
preferable, and the answer is that it depends. Under an observer that weights all modes
equally, the spectral participation ratio at $256$ bands attains an error of $0.35$ against
MG's $1.19$, ahead by $3.4$ times. Under a generic observer with random weights, which is what
a random projection of a network's parameters actually is, MG attains $0.34$ against $1.61$,
ahead by $4.7$ times. Both orderings are perfect in three of those four cells, so what separates
the statistics is absolute recovery and not rank agreement. Both figures also use the delay lag
that is best for that observer, which a controlled experiment can choose and a training log
cannot. Held at a lag common to both observers the two factors are $6.6$ and $4.7$ at $\tau=4$,
and $3.4$ and $1.9$ at $\tau=1$: the direction of each advantage survives, its size does not.

\paragraph{Detecting a change.} Recovery of a static level does not establish that a change is detected. Running three
consecutive segments $r_{\mathrm{high}} \to r_{\mathrm{low}} \to r_{\mathrm{high}}$ with
nothing else altered, and re-measuring the ground truth on each segment, a true fall of $3.99$
components is observed as $3.95$ in the deterministic recurrent regime, is detected by every
usable observer in both directions, and recovers to the level before it to within $0.01$
components. Under stochastic excitation the same true change is observed as $0.15$.

The rule fires when the estimate falls below the median of the pre-switch segment by three
times that segment's window-to-window scatter, for two consecutive windows. The threshold uses
the
pre-switch segment alone, never the midpoint of the two observed levels, which would hand the
detector the answer and would decide the arm where the two levels coincide by a coin flip. On
the parameter norm the level pairs read $4.77 \to 1.22 \to 4.78$ for a true $4 \to 1 \to 4$ and
$6.90 \to 2.12 \to 6.91$ for a true $6 \to 2 \to 6$; at $8 \to 3 \to 8$ they read
$6.97 \to 3.64 \to 6.97$, under-reporting the high level by a component, which is the
saturation of \cref{tab:k20} seen from another direction. The level error is $0.89$ at the high
level and $0.22$ at the low one: the estimate is more accurate the fewer directions are active.

The response time cannot be quoted from this experiment. With a window of $8\,000$ samples and
a stride of $2\,000$ the two observed lags, $999$ and $1\,999$ steps, are the two floors of the
measurement grid rather than measured values. What the experiment does establish is that the
estimate crosses the threshold on a window still containing $87.5\,\%$ pre-switch data.

\section{System constructions}\label{app:constructions}

\paragraph{The image-data system of \cref{sec:digits}.} The data is partitioned into twelve
fixed groups, the same partition at every rank so that the partition is not itself a function
of $r$, and the loss weights of $r$ of them are modulated by $r$ rationally independent
sinusoids filling one octave. Modulating group $j$ tilts the gradient along a direction
$\vphi_j$. These directions are neither orthogonal nor of equal effect, since random data
groups have correlated gradients, so a mixing matrix $\mathrm{pinv}(\Phi) Q$ with $Q$ an
orthonormal basis of $\operatorname{range}(\Phi)$ orthogonalises them, and the per-mode scalar
response gain of the linearised update is divided out. Without this correction the trajectory
participation ratio falls well below $r$.

\Cref{tab:gt} verifies that the construction achieves what it intends, and two of its rows are
results rather than settings. A decaying transient does not recur whatever $r$
is, since a converging trajectory is a curve; and plain mini-batch descent sits at $6.2$
regardless of $r$, because its noise rank is fixed by the per-example gradient covariance of
the data and is not a free parameter of the experiment. Projecting real mini-batch noise onto
$r$ directions recovers only part of the intended rank, reaching $5.6$ at $r=8$, which is why
that regime is scored against its measured value and not against $r$.

\paragraph{The function-space construction.} A local adapter is built around a trained
$64 \to 16 \to 10$ network and its function-space Jacobian is QR-whitened, so that its first

coordinate and the student tracks it by exact backpropagation through both layers. The
functional, trajectory-covariance and update-covariance ranks were all verified to equal $r$.

reproduces the nominal rank to within $0.008$ components over $r = 1, \dots, 20$ in the
recurrent regime, and to three decimals up to $r = 10$. The other regimes behave as at ten
directions: injected noise tracks $r$ closely, projected mini-batch noise reaches only $9.4$
at $r = 20$, and the decaying transient stays between $1.02$ and $1.09$ throughout.

The direct measurement of \cref{sec:direct} needs the trajectory itself, which for the modular
transformer is $226\,816$ doubles at each of two to three thousand logged steps ($228\,608$ for
$S_5$). Every logged step is therefore compressed by a CountSketch
\citep{charikar2002finding, weinberger2009feature} to $\R^{1024}$, with two independent hash
families. Two spaces are recorded: the parameters, and the probe logits after centring each
example over classes and normalising to unit length. The normalisation is not cosmetic. The raw
logit scale is mechanically coupled to weight decay, so a signal read off it could not be
distinguished from a detector of weight decay.

We do not verify a spectral guarantee for this. The elementary bound for a sketch of this kind is
on pairwise inner products \citep{weinberger2009feature}, and a participation ratio is a
functional of the whole spectrum, on which an entrywise Gram bound is weak. A subspace-embedding
guarantee does exist for CountSketch \citep{clarkson2013low} and would apply here, since a
sixty-sample window has rank at most sixty, but it requires a rank we do not measure per window.
What the two hash
families supply instead is a direct estimate of the error: every reported value is the mean of
the two, and their disagreement is recorded with it. Over the published transformer windows
that disagreement is $1.1\,\%$ of the value at the median and $8.4\,\%$ at its worst, which is
the number the reader should use, and it is smaller than every effect \cref{sec:direct} reads.

Two further checks stand behind the numbers. \Cref{tab:sketch} pushes synthetic trajectories of
known rank through the sketch and compares against the same statistic computed on the
uncompressed trajectory. The sketch is accurate to $0.04$ components throughout; the shortfall
against the nominal rank at $5$ and $10$ is a property of the participation ratio on a
sixty-sample cloud and is present without any compression, which is what separates the two
questions. Nothing above rank ten is tested, and the plateaus in \cref{fig:dip} are higher than
that. On a real run of the perceptron architecture, where the parameter count is small enough
to allow it, the participation ratio computed exactly on all $145\,500$ raw parameters agrees
with the sketched value to three decimals. And the observer is verified not to perturb training
by requiring the log to be bit-identical with and without it, over four hundred steps at
reduced probe size, which matters because the task module seeds one global random stream that
the train--validation split, the initialisation and the mini-batch order all continue: a single
stray draw changes the initial weights and can destroy grokking.

Windows are sixty logged rows, which is $600$ optimiser steps for the modular runs and $300$
for $S_5$, and are labelled by their centre, so a feature at a given step is located to within

\section{The full-batch setting}\label{app:fb}

Repeating the direct measurement on the perceptron setting gives a result that must be read as
a limit of the measurement rather than as a negative. Under full-batch gradient descent every
window of every run has a participation ratio between $1.000$ and $1.362$ on the four
statistics the comparison uses, and between $1.000$ and $2.171$ over all eight recorded
columns. This is not an
artefact of the sketch: computed exactly on all $145\,500$ raw parameters the value is the
same to three decimals, and the sketch recovers synthetic trajectories of known rank up to
ten. A deterministic update makes the trajectory a smooth curve, and over a $600$-step window
a smooth curve is straight to within the measurement precision, so what the statistic reports
is the curvature of the trajectory at the scale the window sets, and at that scale there is
almost none. It has no range in which a collapse could appear, and
the absence of a feature is therefore not evidence of its absence: the window must be set in
units of the curvature scale of the trajectory.
Repeating the measurement against window length confirms the diagnosis and withdraws the
opening it appeared to leave. Two runs of $150\,000$ steps, a generalising one and its
label-matched control, are measured over windows spanning $600$ to $120\,000$ optimiser
steps, with the window lengthened by spreading sixty samples further apart rather than by
adding samples, so that the bound on the statistic and its noise floor stay exactly where
the published measurement put them and only the span changes. \Cref{tab:prwindow} gives
the result. The statistic does leave the floor, at a scale of about $10^4$ steps, two
orders of magnitude above the window that produced the $1.000$--$1.362$: the absence of a
feature there was a resolution limit and not a fact about the run.

It is not, however, a hidden signal. What appears at $12\,000$ steps is one localised maximum,
and both runs have one, of the same size: $1.877$ centred at step $12\,800$ for the run that
memorises at $9\,200$ and generalises at $11\,950$, and $1.843$ centred at $9\,350$ for the
control, which memorises at $9\,800$ and does not generalise within the budget. Function space
agrees with more range and no more separation, $2.618$ against $2.145$. What cannot be
concluded from this is where the maximum sits relative to either milestone. The two centres are
adjacent points of a $3\,450$-step grid and their windows overlap over $6\,900$ to $15\,250$
steps, so a $12\,000$-step window cannot localise a feature to better than the interval between
the two milestones; measured against memorisation the offsets are $+3\,600$ and $-450$, which
is a difference the design cannot resolve. The claim the experiment does support is the
negative one: at the window where the statistic has range, the generalising run and its
label-matched control are not separated by it. Beyond $30\,000$ steps
the ordering reverses and the control moves further than the generalising run, because the
window is then a large fraction of a run whose weight norm is still climbing and the
statistic is reading that drift. The full-batch trajectory therefore carries no rank
signature of generalisation at any window length between $600$ and $120\,000$ steps, which
is the stronger form of the same conclusion: the collapse of \cref{sec:direct} belongs to
the regularised stochastic setting, and a trajectory participation ratio must be controlled
for window length as well as for batch size before anything is read from it.

\section{What falls, and when}\label{app:falls}

A fall of the estimate is not specific to generalisation. In a $200\,000$-step run at $p = 211$
with no weight decay, which memorises at step $1\,800$, ends at $3.6\,\%$ validation accuracy
and grows its parameter norm from $43$ to $126$, a fall is detected $49\,250$ steps after memorisation, and the
amplitude control of \cref{sec:nuisance} supplies a mechanism that needs no dimension at all: a
ramped observer gain moves the estimate by $1.16$ components, or $29\,\%$ of a real
four-component change. It is the growth \cref{sec:nowd} reports in the two weight-decay-zero
controls, acting through the observer.

What that run cannot show is that its trajectory simplified. Its log sits in the transient
regime of \cref{tab:grok-diagnostics}, where the estimate is not the count, so a fall in it is
a fall of a quantity we have argued should not be read. The two must be kept apart: the direct
measurement of \cref{sec:direct} is an effective rank of the trajectory itself, and falls in four
generalising runs and not in their controls, whereas the estimate on a log, at the frozen window
and in these settings, is neither a dimension nor an effective rank and falls in at least one run
that does not generalise. What follows is the weaker and defensible claim, that a fall on a log is
not evidence of generalisation. \Cref{sec:matched} does not overturn it. There the log estimate
falls where the trajectory does, in four runs and in neither control, but at a window two orders of
magnitude shorter than this one and under a configuration chosen for that window; the level is no
more readable there than here, and what is read is a change against the run's own distribution of
changes. Nor is the absence of generalisation
within a budget evidence that a fall was a false alarm: two reduced-data configurations against
which earlier specificity figures were computed generalise once the budget is extended, at steps
$39\,600$ and $110\,940$, so such runs are censored and no specificity figure may be computed
against them.

\section{What a windowed estimate on a training log can resolve}\label{app:window}

The claim of \cref{sec:grok-diagnostics} that a change within a run could be read against a fixed
configuration is testable on the seven extended runs, and the test returns a resolution limit
rather than an answer. The frozen configuration takes a window of a third of each record: $4\,000$
logged samples, which at the logging stride is $39\,990$ optimiser steps, advanced by $10\,000$
steps. That is nine windows per run and sixty-three weight-norm windows in all, and it localises
a feature only to $\pm 19\,995$ steps, sixty-seven times coarser than the direct measurement of
\cref{sec:direct}.

The consequence is that the alignment \cref{fig:dip} performs cannot be performed here. Of the
twenty-seven window centres belonging to the three generalising runs, one lies within $5\,000$
steps of its own generalisation step and two within $10\,000$; no two runs share an offset, so no
offset has more than one run defined and no pooled mean exists. For the positive
control the entire pre-generalisation phase is thirteen per cent of one window; for the
reduced-data run that generalises at $110\,940$, no window centre falls after the transition at
all.

What the runs do show is consistent with that. The one run with a centre near its generalisation
step falls by $3.54$ components over the next stride, three times the floor --- but the step lies
inside four of its nine windows, and the falling windows are those in which its parameter norm is
itself moving fastest, downward by $10.5$ and $6.6$ per ten thousand steps, which is the ramped
gain of \cref{sec:nuisance}. The other two show nothing: one has a total range
of $0.77$ components over its whole record. Nor is any of this specific. Across the fifty-six
stride-to-stride changes, six of twenty-four clear the floor in the runs that generalise and six
of thirty-two in the runs that do not. The largest change in the set, $6.36$ components, is in a
run that does generalise, but it falls nearly ninety thousand steps after its generalisation step;
the next largest, $5.20$, is in a run that ends at seven per cent validation accuracy. The tightest comparison
available is three runs of one configuration, of which one generalises: their ranges are $0.77$,
$0.78$ and $0.67$ components, and nothing separates them.

This is the argument of \cref{app:fb} in a second place. At the window the configuration provides,
the statistic has no range in which a feature localised to the transition could appear, so its
absence is not evidence of absence. Testing the claim needs a window set in units of the width of
the transition, which for these runs is hundreds of steps and not tens of thousands: a re-run of
the estimator, not a re-reading of what we have.

\paragraph{The re-run.} \Cref{sec:matched} performs it, on the six runs of \cref{fig:dip} rather
than the seven of this appendix, because those are the runs whose trajectory was stored and only a
paired comparison answers the question. The window is $60$ samples throughout, which is $600$
optimiser steps for the modular runs at their stride-$10$ logging and $300$ for the $S_5$ runs at
stride $5$, centred on the midpoints of the windows of \cref{sec:direct}; since those are $600$ and
$295$ steps wide, every log window matches the one it is paired with to within two per cent. Four
things bear on how the result should be read.

\emph{The configuration is not the frozen one, and cannot be.} At $60$ samples the frozen delay
span, $(20-1) \times 4 = 76$, exceeds the window. The grid crosses
$E_{\max} \in \{4, 6, 10\}$, $\tau \in \{1, 2, 4\}$, $k \in \{5, 20\}$ and both Theiler rules,
thirty-six cells, of which twenty-six are long enough to return a value. Twenty-five of those
separate the four generalising runs from the two controls; the exception is $E_{\max} = 6$,
$\tau = 1$, $k = 5$ under the embedding rule. The headline cell is named by the rule of
\cref{sec:matched} and is not the best cell: $E_{\max} = 4$, $\tau = 4$, $k = 20$ gives a
shallowest generalising fall of $2.12$ against its $1.91$.

\emph{The admissibility diagnostics do not survive the shortening.} The identifiability ratio needs
$2E_{\max}$. At $E_{\max} = 10$ the doubled delay span does fit the window, but the Theiler
exclusion then leaves two usable points against $k = 20$, so the headline cell has no ratio. Where
it can be computed, at $E_{\max} \le 6$, it reads $0.97$ to $1.00$ under the frozen Theiler rule
and $1.29$ to $1.62$ under the other, and settles nothing. That is why \cref{sec:matched} reads a
change and not a level.

\emph{Linear detrending removes the effect, and the surrogate control does not.} Repeating every
cell on the linearly detrended window --- the removal $\PR^{\mathrm{det}}$ performs per coordinate
--- leaves no cell separating the two groups. The two removals are not comparable: on a
$1024$-dimensional sketch a per-coordinate linear detrend takes one direction of many, while on a
scalar it takes most of the series. The surrogate control keeps the shape and destroys only the
fine structure, and it is the one that discriminates. Both are reported in \cref{tab:matched}.

\emph{The observer matters.} The result above is the parameter norm. On the training loss ten of
the twenty-six cells separate the groups and the surrogate control passes in the $S_5$ run only;
on the validation loss fifteen do, and that observer defines the event being aligned on, so it is
listed for completeness and not read.

\emph{The control arm has almost no dynamic range.} At this window the estimate has a baseline
belonging to the configuration rather than to the run: on a standardised $60$-sample straight line
\cref{eq:mg} returns $2.7733$, and every run's pre-transition level lies within $0.28$ of that. The
modular control's parameter-norm log is straight to $0.7\,\%$ of its own spread across the whole
comparison interval, and the estimate is pinned accordingly --- a span of $0.0029$ components over
fifty windows, against $2.05$ to $2.56$ in the generalising runs. Its $D = 1.00$ therefore records
that the observer offered the estimator no local structure to respond to, not that a dimension was
measured and found unchanged: a statistic with three thousandths of a component of range could not
have shown a two-fold fall whatever the trajectory did. The $S_5$ control is a weaker case of the
same thing, $3.2\,\%$ residual and a $0.22$-component span. This leaves the surrogate control
untouched, since there each run is compared only against surrogates of itself, and it leaves the
four generalising runs untouched; what it removes is any reading of the two controls' $D$ and
quantile columns as informative dimensional negatives, and it reinforces the point already made,
that the separation between the groups is mechanically a separation in local roughness.

\section{The representation dimension in closed form}\label{app:repr}

The perceptron setting of \cref{sec:grokking} makes the learned representation analytically
available, and three different numbers can be read off it. They are not commensurable and the
literature does not always separate them. The \emph{mode count} is $(p+1)/2$, or $49$ at
$p = 97$: the number of distinct Fourier frequencies the generalising solution of
\citet{gromov2023grokking} uses. The \emph{order parameter} is the mean inverse participation
ratio of the first layer's Fourier spectrum, which is $1.000$ for that solution because each
neuron carries a single frequency and about $0.041$ at random initialisation; it measures how
concentrated each neuron is, not how many modes exist. The \emph{effective rank} is the
participation ratio of the singular values of the first-layer weight matrix, which for the
analytic solution is $148.8$: that matrix is $500 \times 194$ and full rank, because each
non-zero frequency contributes a sine and a cosine direction in each of the two input blocks,
so $148.8$ says how evenly those $194$ directions are used and is not a count of anything. Only
the first is a dimension in the sense the closed form fixes. Two things follow from measuring
the other two, and both qualify readings of the representation dimension elsewhere.

\emph{The representation is not complete at the transition.} For modular addition the order
parameter reads $0.067$ just after memorisation at step $9\,170$, $0.189$ just before
generalisation at step $11\,950$, $0.263$ when validation accuracy first reaches $100\,\%$, and
then rises for a further $87\,000$ steps to $0.595$ against an analytic $1.000$. It does not
jump at the transition; it begins rising at memorisation and passes smoothly through.
Generalisation therefore arrives well before the representation is complete, and requires it
only to be periodic enough. The effective rank of the same layer carries almost no signal at all: it reads $139.1$ at
random initialisation, within seven per cent of the analytic $148.8$ before any training, then
spans $132.6$ to $150.9$ over the run without being monotone and overshoots the reference
before falling back. Landing near the reference is therefore not evidence of anything.

\emph{The order parameter is only interpretable against its own reference.} \Cref{tab:ipr}
gives the measured value beside each task's own closed form. Two of the tasks that generalise
have a reference near the floor, because the representation is periodic in $g_1(n)$ while the
spectrum is taken over the raw index $n$, and $g_1$ is not linear there. Read against modular
addition's $1.000$, a measured $0.044$ says nothing was learned; read against that task's own
$0.062$, it says the run has essentially converged. The tasks that never generalise sit at the
initialisation floor, and the analysis returns no reference for them rather than substituting
another task's.

\section{The Theiler exclusion of the twenty-direction configuration}\label{app:theiler}

The Theiler exclusion is meant to remove neighbours that are close in time rather than in phase
space, and the rule this study asks for is $W_T = \max((E-1)\tau,\, t_{\mathrm{acf}})$: at least
the full span of a delay vector, so that two neighbours cannot be near each other merely because
their windows overlap. The implementation caps $W_T$ at $150$ samples, because the neighbour
query cost is linear in it. At the eight-direction configuration the embedding span is
$(20-1) \times 4 = 76$, so the cap binds only where the autocorrelation time exceeds $150$: never
on the calibration systems, whose autocorrelation time is one to three samples, but on five of the
seven parameter-norm records of \cref{sec:grokking}, where it is $161$ to $858$. Those five were
therefore scored at $W_T = 150$ rather than at the value the rule asks for, in the direction that
inflates the estimate. At the twenty-direction configuration the span is $(40-1) \times 16 =
624$, so the cap binds unconditionally and the exclusion used is $150$, under a quarter of what
the rule asks for. Every row of the twenty-direction scores records
$W_T = 150$; this is a departure from the stated protocol, not a choice we made deliberately,
and it is why \cref{sec:digits} marks that result exploratory.

exclusions. Nothing is regenerated, so the systems cannot drift between the arms.

flattering the result: enforcing the protocol costs accuracy and buys correctness. The
saturation above about six directions is present at both settings and is therefore not an
artefact of the exclusion.

The same cap has a second effect that the identifiability ratio inherits. The exclusion actually
applied is $W_T = \min\bigl(\max((E-1)\tau,\, t_{\mathrm{acf}}),\, 150\bigr)$. The numerator is
computed at $2E_{\max}$ with $\tau$ held fixed, so at the eight-direction configuration its span
is $(40-1) \times 4 = 156$ and the denominator's is $76$: the numerator is capped at $150$
whatever the data does, while the denominator is capped only once $t_{\mathrm{acf}}$ reaches
$150$. The two arms are therefore treated identically exactly when $t_{\mathrm{acf}} \ge 150$ and
differently when it is smaller. On the parameter-norm records of \cref{sec:grokking} five of seven
have $t_{\mathrm{acf}}$ between $161$ and $858$ and are clean by this test; the asymmetric cases
are the two short-correlation records and, more consequentially, the calibration systems of
\cref{sec:ladder}, whose autocorrelation time is one to three samples, so that the reference band
of \cref{sec:diagnostics-validated} was itself measured with $76$ against $150$. The effect is
small at this span and the diagnostic separates the regimes of \cref{sec:validity} by a wide
margin, but a ratio built from two differently excluded estimates is not the clean quantity
\cref{sec:diagnostics} describes, and lifting the cap is the first thing to change in any
follow-up.

\section{Run inventory}\label{app:runs}

\Cref{tab:runs} lists every training run the paper draws on. Two architectures appear:
\textbf{T} is the one-layer transformer of \citet{nanda2023progress}, $d_{\mathrm{model}} = 128$,
four heads, no layer normalisation, $226\,816$ parameters on modular addition at $p = 113$ and
$228\,608$ on $S_5$, trained in double precision with AdamW at learning rate $10^{-3}$ and
$\beta = (0.9, 0.98)$; \textbf{P} is the two-layer quadratic perceptron of
\citet{gromov2023grokking}, $145\,500$ parameters at $p = 97$ and width $500$, trained by
full-batch gradient descent with no regularisation.

Three departures from the source configurations should be stated, since two of them are what put
the runs in the regimes \cref{sec:grok-diagnostics} assigns them. \citet{nanda2023progress} train
full batch; we use mini-batches of $256$, which is what makes this setting stochastic rather than
deterministic. The $S_5$ weight decay of $0.2$ and the $S_5$ task itself
\citep{power2022grokking} are outside that configuration. And the two $S_5$ runs step the
optimiser twice per batch, reproducing a duplicated update in our own earlier released logs, so
their effective learning rate and decay are about double the nominal ones.

\paragraph{What counts as an event.} Memorisation and generalisation are the first logged step
at which the training and the validation accuracy respectively reach $95\,\%$, with no
persistence requirement. Training accuracy is measured on the current mini-batch and validation
accuracy on a fixed $512$-example subset of the validation split, so both carry a sampling
error of a few tenths of a per cent, and the step is resolved only to the logging stride, which
is $10$ steps for the modular runs and $5$ for $S_5$. The extended reruns of
\cref{sec:pairs} instead require the threshold to hold over a sustained block, which is the
stricter rule; the two put memorisation within $70$ steps of each other on every run here, and
differ by $1\,080$ steps on one generalisation step; no claim turns on either. A dash means the criterion was not met inside the budget.
Nothing here establishes that such a run would never generalise, and \cref{tab:runs} records
the final validation accuracy so that the reader can see how far from the threshold each one
ended.

\section{The exclusion, and what a transient's dimension is}\label{app:exclusion}

\Cref{sec:classes} claims that recurrence is a requirement of this protocol and not of the
estimator. The claim is testable on the arms of \cref{sec:digits} by moving one setting and
nothing else: the Theiler exclusion $W_T$, from zero to the frozen rule. Four observers, one per
family, three seeds, $r \in \{2,4,6,8\}$, seven windows each; the estimator is otherwise the frozen
configuration, and at the frozen setting every cell reproduces \cref{sec:digits} to every stored
digit.

Three things follow. \emph{The number at $W_T = 0$ is the right one.} On a uniformly sampled curve
the $20$ nearest neighbours sit at offsets $\pm 1 \dots \pm 10$, for which \cref{eq:mg} returns
$1.19928$ exactly; the transient arm returns $1.1971 \pm 0.0010$ across all forty-eight cells. The
excess over unity is the lattice bias of a deterministically sampled curve, not an error, and the
measured trajectory participation ratio of the same arm is $1.03$ to $1.07$.

\emph{The mechanism is the loss of near neighbours, not of neighbours.} The estimator fills its
twenty slots at every exclusion; what changes is how far away they are. Before any exclusion the
median temporal separation of a point's twenty nearest neighbours is $2546$ samples on the fast
arm and $6$ on the transient --- there, the nearest neighbours in space simply \emph{are} the
temporal ones. Of the $158\,480$ pairs in a window, those as close as the $k$th temporal neighbour
number $138\,761$ on the fast arm at $W_T = 0$ and $138\,048$ at $W_T = 150$; on the transient they
number $131\,849$ and then none at all. The twenty retained distances then span a factor of $1.07$,
their log-ratio sum falls from $15.86$ to $0.63$, and the reciprocal in \cref{eq:mg} diverges. No
cell is flagged degenerate, so this is not a numerical floor. An exponential decay with no
optimiser and no data reproduces both ends, $1.196$ and $26.4$.

\emph{Two qualifications.} Recurrence is not always needed even here: on the fast arm $W_T = 0$
already returns $r$, because that arm is not oversampled, and it is the slow arm --- nearest
neighbours $574$ samples away --- that the exclusion rescues, from a rank-blind $2.1$ to its
published $3.5$. And a transient need not have dimension one: a decaying \emph{oscillation}
returns $2.36$ at every exclusion, correctly, because at $k = 20$ the neighbourhood spans several
turns of the spiral and the two-dimensional sheet they lie on is what there is to see at that
resolution. What is general is the asymmetry, not the value.

\paragraph{A diagnostic falls out.} $\hat d_{MG}(W_T{=}150)/\hat d_{MG}(W_T{=}20)$ is $1.000$ on
the fast arm and $1.001$ on the slow, against $5.25$ on the transient, with no overlap over
forty-eight cells per arm (largest recurrent $1.012$, smallest transient $4.71$); the cheaper
$\hat d_{MG}(100)/\hat d_{MG}(50)$ separates as cleanly. Unlike the trend-crossing count it tests
recurrence in the reconstructed space rather than non-monotonicity of the series, which is the gap
\cref{sec:diagnostics} leaves open and which \cref{app:eos} shows costs something on real runs. We
report it rather than adopt it: it is calibrated on the same systems that produced the two
diagnostics and has not been validated out of sample.

\section{Descent at the edge of stability}\label{app:eos}

Every system of \cref{sec:ladder} is driven, so the ladder establishes a conditional, and
\cref{sec:regime} asks whether anything undriven occupies the admissible regime. The one candidate
the literature offers is the edge of stability \citep{cohen2021gradient}: gradient descent at a
fixed rate sharpens until the top Hessian eigenvalue reaches $2/\eta$ and then hovers, and while it
hovers the loss descends on long scales and not on short ones. That is deterministic and not a
plain transient, which is the pair of conditions \cref{sec:classes} asks for.

\paragraph{Setting.} Full-batch descent, no weight decay, on the quadratic perceptron of
\cref{app:constructions}: modular addition at $p = 97$, width $500$, half the pairs held out, eight
rates by two seeds, $30\,000$ steps. Two departures are deliberate. The log is written at
\emph{every} optimiser step, because the oscillation is the two-cycle of the unstable mode and its
period is a few steps, while every other full-batch log here is strided at ten or more. And the
sharpness is measured along the run by power iteration on Hessian-vector products, taking the
largest \emph{algebraic} eigenvalue: the Hessian is indefinite early in training, when the most
negative direction is the steeper, and plain power iteration then returns a negative sharpness. The
routine agrees with an exact eigendecomposition on a $120$-parameter model to $5 \times 10^{-12}$
relative at a tight tolerance and $6 \times 10^{-5}$ at the setting used.

\paragraph{The edge is reached, and it is not enough.} The oscillation is real and not round-off:
the largest single-step rise is $0.99$ to $4.1$ times the loss itself, against a float32 relative

edge-of-stability runs return near states they have left; five trace a curve past the exclusion
exactly as the monotone runs do, and the two groups are separated by an order of magnitude with
nothing between them ($0.026$ and $0.065$ against $1.008$ to $1.013$).

\paragraph{The diagnostics do not find the difference.} On the training loss, the observer this
paper's full-batch analysis uses, the admissible rectangle of \cref{sec:diagnostics} admits all
five runs that do not recur and refuses both that do. The estimate agrees with the recurrence
measurement and not with the rectangle: $3.49$ and $2.44$ in the two runs that return, against
$29.2$ to $30.8$ --- the transient value of \cref{app:exclusion} --- in the five that do not. The
cause is visible in the inputs: every run at the edge has a rise fraction of $0.50$, so the
trend-crossing count, which tests non-monotonicity and not recurrence, passes all of them, while
$\rho_{\mathrm{ident}}$ happens to sit at unity in the five and at $1.23$ and $1.30$ in the two.
On the parameter norm the rectangle admits all seven. This is seven runs of one configuration on
one task and we draw no rule from it, but it is the failure mode \cref{sec:diagnostics} names,
occurring.

\paragraph{And the stride hides it.} Read at the stride every other full-batch log here uses, the
whole phenomenon largely disappears: over these seven runs the median trend-crossing count falls
from $80$ to $2$ and the median rise fraction from $0.500$ to $0.000$ exactly, and where five of
the seven were admissible at every step, none is at a stride of ten and one is at fifty. The
collapse is not uniform --- the run with the most intermittent oscillation keeps a rise fraction
near $0.45$ under decimation, because its cycle is not a clean two-cycle to alias --- but whether a
log looks like a transient is in part a property of how often it was written.
