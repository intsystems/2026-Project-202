\begin{abstract}
Over a stretch of training in which the update rule is fixed and the parameters keep returning near
states already visited, the states they revisit form a set of dimension far below the number of
parameters: the active dimension of that regime. Measuring it needs the
stored trajectory, whereas a run already writes scalar logs: how much of it does one such scalar
carry? We reconstruct a state space by delay coordinates and estimate its
intrinsic dimension by maximum likelihood on nearest-neighbour distances. The procedure returns a
plausible number on any record, including one in which nothing recurs, so it estimates the
active dimension only under conditions we establish separately. On six constructed systems of known
active dimension it is accurate to about one component, up to a ceiling near eight. Statistics
computed from the log identify the records on which the value is not a dimension at all. They
reject both standard grokking settings, so we store a compressed record of the trajectory
instead. Its effective rank
collapses at generalisation in the four runs trained with weight decay, then re-expands, the
simplification that mechanistic accounts of grokking predict. A run that merely stops moving can
collapse as deeply. Only the timing of the collapse and its reversal mark the transition. Re-run over a window matched to the transition, the estimate on an ordinary log falls
at the same step in those four runs and in neither of the other two, more steeply than in
randomised surrogates of each run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A network is trained in a parameter space of dimension $P$, but over a stretch of training the
parameters usually move in far fewer dimensions. Is an apparent simplification of training a real
reduction in the number of degrees of freedom in use, or an artefact of a shrinking scale? Can a
phase transition such as delayed generalisation be detected without instrumenting the run? Both
questions are argued with whichever count of degrees of freedom is least expensive to compute. The counts in use disagree: the \emph{available} dimension of the
subspace the parameters are confined to \citep{li2018measuring}, the \emph{functional} dimension of
the map from that subspace to the outputs, and the \emph{effective rank} of the trajectory
covariance.

Our two questions concern a fourth quantity: the number of independent components of the set the
parameters occupy while the update rule is fixed. We call it the \emph{active dimension} of that
regime (\cref{eq:act}). If $r$ independent quasiperiodic phases
drive the motion, the path lies on an $r$-dimensional torus and the active dimension is $r$.
\Cref{sec:digits} moves the active dimension while the available and functional dimensions stay
fixed, and \cref{sec:aniso} separates it from the effective rank.

Measuring the active dimension means storing the parameter trajectory, which an ordinary run does
not do. A run already writes scalar logs: a loss, a gradient norm, a parameter norm.
\emph{How much of the active dimension can be recovered from one such scalar?} Nonlinear time
series analysis supplies a standard answer: reconstruct a state space from the scalar by delay
coordinates \citep{takens1981detecting, sauer1991embedology}, then estimate the intrinsic dimension
of the reconstruction by maximum likelihood on nearest-neighbour distances \citep{levina2004maximum,
mackay2005comments}.

The procedure returns a number on any record. Power-law noise yields a finite and reproducible
value fixed by its spectral exponent \citep{osborne1989finite, theiler1991some}; a decaying
transient yields a large value. Establishing which case a training log falls in is the substance of
the problem.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We evaluate the estimate against a constructed ground truth on six
systems of increasing realism, ending with a linear head trained inside a $k$-dimensional subspace
on top of a frozen image network (\cref{sec:ladder}); we establish the conditions its value depends
on, among them the identifiability ratio and the trend-crossing count, which identify the failure
regimes from the series alone (\cref{sec:validity}); and we apply the procedure to grokking, where
both published settings fall outside the regime the estimate needs and the result comes from the
stored trajectory instead (\cref{sec:grokking}).

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reconstruction and intrinsic dimension.} Delay reconstruction follows from Takens'
theorem and its extensions (\cref{app:theory}), is used on empirical series \citep{sugihara1990nonlinear,
sugihara2012detecting}, and takes its lag and dimension from mutual information, false nearest
neighbours or singular spectra \citep{fraser1986independent, kennel1992determining,
cao1997practical, broomhead1986extracting}. We use pooled maximum likelihood
\citep{levina2004maximum, mackay2005comments}; the alternatives \citep{grassberger1983measuring,
facco2017estimating, camastra2016intrinsic} are bounded alike by a finite record
\citep{eckmann1992fundamental}. Oversampling \citep{theiler1986spurious} and power-law
noise \citep{osborne1989finite} produce spurious dimensions; surrogate tests
\citep{theiler1992testing, schreiber1996improved} detect nonlinear structure but not how much is
present and miss a decaying transient entirely.

\paragraph{Dimension in optimisation.} Random-subspace training \citep{li2018measuring,
aghajanyan2021intrinsic} reports the smallest $k$ that still solves a task, a property of the task
and not of a window; we adopt the same parameterisation but measure a different quantity within it.
Closest to the active dimension is the finding that descent occurs largely in a small subspace
spanned by the top Hessian eigenvectors \citep{gurari2018gradient, sagun2018empirical}.
\Citet{ansuini2019intrinsic} and \citet{pope2021intrinsic} estimate the intrinsic dimension of a
data set or of a layer's representations, which are properties of a point cloud, whereas the active
dimension is a property of a path. The participation ratio we adopt \citep{gao2017theory} is not
the spectral-entropy quantity of the same name \citep{roy2007effective}; it has been measured on a
grokking trajectory once, in passing \citep{notsawo2023predicting}. All of these quantities need
weights, gradients or activations, whereas the nearest precedent for a scalar computed during
training and acted on is the gradient noise scale \citep{mccandlish2018empirical}. Mini-batch
descent near a minimum is modelled as a stochastic process \citep{mandt2017stochastic}.

\paragraph{Grokking.} Delayed generalisation \citep{power2022grokking} is studied in two settings
and we use both. The transformer setting is regularised and, in our version, stochastic: the
configuration of \citet{nanda2023progress}, also used by \citet{liu2022omnigrok}, on modular
addition and on the $S_5$ task of \citet{power2022grokking}. The perceptron setting is the
unregularised full-batch gradient descent of \citet{gromov2023grokking}, on the modular polynomials
and representability criterion of \citet{doshi2024grokking}, which supply label-matched pairs
(\cref{app:runs}). Explanations are framed in weight or activation space
\citep{nanda2023progress, varma2023explaining, liu2022towards, barak2022hidden} or in a
lazy-to-rich transition \citep{kumar2024grokking}. Closest to us, \citet{notsawo2023predicting}
predict grokking from early loss oscillations \citep{thilak2022slingshot} and
\citet{merrill2023tale} track competing subnetworks. We differ from all of these in estimating a
dynamical quantity whose ground truth is measured independently.

\section{Problem statement}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{The active dimension}\label{sec:three}

Let $\vs_t = (\vtheta_t, \vu_t)$ be the optimiser state at step $t$: the parameter vector
$\vtheta_t \in \R^P$ and whatever the optimiser carries beside it, two accumulated moments under
AdamW and nothing under plain gradient descent. Where the parameters are confined by construction
or by the dynamics to an affine subspace $\vtheta = \vtheta_0 + \mV^{\top}\vc$ with
$\mV \in \R^{k \times P}$ orthonormal, we work in the free coordinates $\vc_t \in \R^k$; that
change of coordinates is an isometry and preserves intrinsic dimension.

We call $k$ the \emph{available} dimension. The \emph{functional} dimension is the rank of
$\partial \vf(\vc)/\partial \vc$, with $\vf$ the outputs on a fixed probe set; it bounds how many of
the $k$ directions can change the computed function. The \emph{active} dimension, the subject of
this paper, is a property of a \emph{regime} $R$: a stretch of training over which the update rule
is fixed and the state carries an ergodic invariant measure. Carried to the free coordinates, that
measure gives the \emph{occupation measure} $\mu_R$, the long-run fraction of steps at which $\vc_t$
falls in a given set. The regime occupies the support $\gA_R$ of $\mu_R$, the points whose every
neighbourhood is visited with positive frequency. The active dimension is the exponent with which
$\mu_R$ fills a small ball about such a point,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:act}
  d_{\mathrm{act}}(R) \;=\; \lim_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}
  \frac{\log \mu_R\bigl(B(\vc, \varepsilon)\bigr)}{\log \varepsilon} ,
\end{equation}
with $B(\vc, \varepsilon)$ the ball of radius $\varepsilon$ about $\vc$. This defines
$d_{\mathrm{act}}(R)$ when the limit is the same at $\mu_R$-almost every $\vc$. Where $\gA_R$ is a smooth
$d$-dimensional manifold carrying a positive density the exponent is $d$; a point of it is then
fixed by $d$ local coordinates, the \emph{active components}. Another chart supplies
different ones, of the same number.

A window $\gW$ of $L$ consecutive optimiser steps holds $L$ points, whose own exponent is zero. A
window is therefore a sample from $\mu_R$ rather than the set itself. It estimates
$d_{\mathrm{act}}(R)$ only where it lies inside one regime and only over the radii its neighbour
distances span (\cref{eq:finitescale}): a phase of smaller amplitude than those goes unresolved
(\cref{sec:aniso}).

The systems of \cref{sec:ladder} set $\vc_t = F(\varphi_1(t), \dots, \varphi_r(t))$ with $r$
rationally independent quasiperiodic phases and $F$ a smooth embedding of the $r$-torus, giving
$\gA_R = F(\mathbb{T}^{r})$ and $d_{\mathrm{act}}(R) = r$. Rational independence makes the orbit
dense on the torus; the embedding keeps $F$ from merging or discarding a phase.

We also measure the participation ratio \citep{gao2017theory}, which for a non-negative spectrum
$\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pr}
  \PR(\lambda) \;=\; \frac{\bigl(\sum_i \lambda_i\bigr)^2}{\sum_i \lambda_i^2} .
\end{equation}
It equals $m$ exactly when $m$ of the $\lambda_i$ are equal and the rest vanish. Applied over a
window to the covariance of $\vc_t$ it gives the \emph{effective rank} $\PR^{\mathrm{pos}}$ and to
the covariance of the residual $\tilde{\vc}_t$ about the window's least-squares line the detrended
form $\PR^{\mathrm{det}}$. A drifting trajectory needs the detrended
form, since a straight drift dominates the spectrum and holds $\PR^{\mathrm{pos}}$ near unity
however the trajectory moves about that line. Written without a superscript, \emph{the effective
rank} means $\PR^{\mathrm{pos}}$.

The effective rank is not the active dimension. It equals $r$ when $r$ directions carry comparable
variance and falls below $r$ when they do not, while $d_{\mathrm{act}}(R)$ stays at $r$
(\cref{sec:aniso}). We use it to check that each system of \cref{sec:ladder} really does excite $r$
independent phases (\cref{tab:gt}); the ground truth is the phase construction, not this
measurement. It is also the quantity \cref{sec:direct} measures on a stored grokking trajectory.

\subsection{Observation model and estimator}\label{sec:estimator}

We observe a scalar series $x_t = \phi(\vs_t)$, in which $\vs_t$ is the full optimiser state and
$\phi$ is fixed before the run. From it we reconstruct
$\vy_t = (x_t, \dots, x_{t-(E-1)\tau}) \in \R^E$. For a
generic pair of dynamics and observation on a compact invariant set of box-counting dimension $d$,
this map is an embedding once $E > 2d$ (\cref{thm:takens,thm:sauer}). Genericity is not verifiable
for a $\phi$ fixed in advance, so every result below is reported over a family of observers. We
estimate the intrinsic dimension of $\{\vy_t\}$ by maximum likelihood on nearest-neighbour
distances (\cref{thm:lb}). Let
$r^{(i)}_1 \le \dots \le r^{(i)}_m$ be the distances from point $i$ to its $m$ nearest neighbours,
where a neighbour counts only if it is more than $W_T$ steps away from $i$ in time. Discarding the
near-in-time neighbours in this way is the \emph{Theiler exclusion} \citep{theiler1986spurious,
kantz2004nonlinear}: without it, the nearest points in $\R^E$ are the samples just before and after
$i$, so the statistic measures the sampling rate instead of the geometry. Pooling the likelihood over the $N$ points before inverting, as
\citet{mackay2005comments} recommend, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg}
  \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}^{\,-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{N(m-1)} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}
  \log \frac{r^{(i)}_m}{r^{(i)}_j} ,
\end{equation}
\emph{The estimate} is the value MG returns; \emph{the estimator} is the procedure.
\Cref{app:alg} gives the algorithm as implemented; \cref{tab:alts} scores the per-point average of
\citet{levina2004maximum} beside it.
The \emph{roughness} $\operatorname{std}(\Delta x)/\operatorname{std}(x)$, the \emph{linear
participation ratio} $\PR_{\mathrm{delay}}$ of \cref{eq:pr} on $\Cov\{\vy_t\}$ and the
\emph{spectral participation ratio} on the power spectrum of $x$ accompany every estimate.
Each can grow with $r$ for reasons that involve no geometry. If one of them recovers the truth as
well as the estimator does, the accuracy of the estimator is no evidence that a dimension has been
measured.

\subsection{Dynamical regimes}\label{sec:classes}

Two conditions must hold before an estimate computed from a log gives an active
dimension. The embedding theorems (\cref{thm:takens,thm:sauer}) require a deterministic map on a
compact invariant set; that licenses the delay reconstruction as a change of coordinates. The
neighbour statistic of \cref{eq:mg} requires more: the record must cover that set densely enough
that the Theiler exclusion still leaves each point a genuine return. The estimator itself would
accept points drawn from independent runs; recurrence is needed because our entire sample is one trajectory.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt, parsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Deterministic and recurrent.} The path returns close to states already passed through
and lies on an $r$-dimensional torus, so $\gA_R$ has $r$ independent components and
$d_{\mathrm{act}}(R) = r$. Here both conditions hold and the estimator recovers $r$.

\item \textbf{Deterministic and transient.} A converging run decays along a curve it never
revisits. The curve itself is one-dimensional, but a run that does not return samples no invariant
measure, so it has no $\gA_R$ and \cref{eq:act} has nothing to measure. The dimension of a path and
the dimension of a regime are different quantities. The value returned here is invalid rather than
a dimension of one (\cref{sec:regime}). The effective rank does stay near unity
(\cref{tab:gt}), but only because the curve is nearly straight.

\item \textbf{Stochastically driven.} A stationary mini-batch optimiser may have an occupation
measure of its own, whose dimension counts the directions the gradient noise excites. That is not
the quantity we are after. The embedding theorems for forced systems (\cref{thm:stark})
do cover a drive of this kind, but one realisation of it at a time, whereas a single run crosses
realisations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostics}

The series alone is enough to tell a transient from a stochastic drive. The \textbf{identifiability
ratio} $\rho_{\mathrm{ident}} = \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}(2E_{\max}) / \hat{d}_{\mathrm{MG}}(E_{\max})$
compares the estimate at a doubled embedding dimension with the estimate at the working one. It
stays near unity where a dimension can be measured. Under mini-batch noise the estimate follows the
embedding dimension rather than the data; the ratio then grows. A transient escapes
it: there the estimate is stable in the embedding dimension for reasons unconnected with any
dimension. A transient is caught by the \textbf{trend-crossing count} instead, the number of sign
changes of the residual of the window about its own least-squares line, which is near zero on a
monotone decay. The count tests only non-monotonicity, so it cannot replace the ratio; we report
both with every estimate.

\section{Validation protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

The requirements below were fixed before any result was computed; \cref{tab:ladder} records which
of them each system fails.

\emph{Constructed truth}: the ground truth follows from the construction rather than from a fit.
Every system is built with $r$ independent quasiperiodic phases, so that $d_{\mathrm{act}}(R) = r$;
the effective rank of the recorded trajectory must then confirm that all $r$ are comparably
excited.

\emph{Frozen configuration}: the estimator is chosen once, by a search over its own settings alone.
Searching the settings of the system at the same time would select the data together with the
method: the result would then report how favourable a system the search had found.

\emph{Withheld ranks}: ranks are held out as well as seeds, because the geometry of the excitation
depends on $r$ and every seed at a given rank shares that geometry.

\emph{Zero learning rate}: every system is re-run with the learning rate set to zero, so that the
parameters never move and any observer of the optimiser must be constant. A log that still varies
and still returns its earlier estimate was never a function of the optimiser.

\section{Evaluation on systems with a known active dimension}\label{sec:ladder}

We evaluate the estimator on six systems of increasing realism, from an oscillating diagonal matrix
to a linear head on a network trained on image data (\cref{tab:ladder}). In each the measured
effective rank confirms that all $r$ phases are excited; the estimator receives one scalar series
and nothing else.

\subsection{An oscillating diagonal matrix}\label{sec:matrix}

The first system has no optimiser and no feedback. In $\mW(t) = \operatorname{diag}(b_i +
\delta_i(t))$, the offsets $b_i$ are fixed and $r$ of the $\delta_i$ oscillate at rationally
independent frequencies. The estimate tracks $r$ across the whole withheld range to within a third
of a component (\cref{tab:ladder}).

\subsection{A constrained head on a network trained on image data}\label{sec:digits}

A tanh network is trained on the scikit-learn handwritten digits and then frozen. A linear head
confined to a $k$-dimensional affine subspace is trained on top of it under softmax
cross-entropy, so that the curvature is data-dependent and all the dynamics occur in $\vc$. The
excitation is made $r$-dimensional by modulating the loss weights of $r$ of twelve fixed data
groups (\cref{app:constructions}). We drive
the head quasiperiodically, with mini-batch noise, and by plain gradient descent, covering the
recurrent, stochastic and transient regimes of \cref{sec:classes}.

Twelve scalar observers are recorded (\cref{app:obs}). The estimator was frozen on four of them, on
seeds and ranks reserved for that purpose, so every result below is out of sample in rank, in seed,
and for most observers in the observer too. In the deterministic recurrent regime the estimate
recovers the active dimension to within about one component. Every observer orders the withheld
ranks correctly (\cref{tab:ladder}); for no scored observer does the roughness of the log order
them, so the agreement cannot be explained by the logs merely becoming rougher as $r$ grows
(\cref{tab:obs}).

\paragraph{The ceiling.}\label{sec:ceiling} The estimator saturates above about eight components.
At $k = 20$ it tracks the rank to about ten and then falls behind (\cref{tab:k20}). The largest $r$
it still tracks follows neither the embedding dimension nor the record length and never exceeds
about eleven (\cref{app:ceiling}). One caveat: the participation ratio of the delay covariance, a purely linear statistic, saturates along
the same curve, so the limit may be the number of components the delay window resolves linearly,
with no geometry involved.

\paragraph{The zero-learning-rate test.}\label{sec:silence} Most published analyses of dimension on
training logs do not run the check of \cref{sec:protocol}. It invalidated four of our six systems
and the function-subspace variant besides, each of which returns its reported estimate with the
parameters frozen, because the drive keeps the residual moving while the parameters do not. It also
rejects the instantaneous mini-batch loss of this system, otherwise among the more accurate
observers.

\section{Conditions of validity}\label{sec:validity}

\subsection{Recovery in each regime}\label{sec:regime}

In \cref{fig:regimes}(a) the fast quasiperiodic drive follows the diagonal over the whole withheld range. Under
mini-batch noise the estimate is flat in $r$ and well above the truth, responding to $E_{\max}$ and
to the length of the record instead of to the data. Weak noise on an otherwise recurrent trajectory
leaves the ordering intact and the absolute value wrong. The slow drive is the informative case,
because it changes behaviour partway along: it tracks the truth to about $r = 4$, then saturates.

Panel (b) plots the identifiability ratio on either side of that change. The regime labels alone
would have predicted the extreme cases, not the slow drive, which passes at $r = 2$, where panel
(a) puts the estimate near the truth, and fails at $r = 6$, where panel (a) puts it at little more
than half the truth. The ratio therefore locates the failure inside a single system, using nothing
the ground truth supplied.

The Theiler exclusion is the reason the estimator errs most on a transient. There it returns about
$29$, a divergent quantity evaluated at the cap on the exclusion. With the exclusion removed, it returns at every rank, seed and observer the value
maximum likelihood gives on a uniformly sampled curve, while the fast recurrent case is unchanged
(\cref{app:exclusion}).

Every system evaluated above is externally driven, whereas training is not, so the evaluation
establishes only a conditional result. The full-batch runs of \cref{sec:grokking} do reach the edge
of stability \citep{cohen2021gradient}, where an undriven run might still recur, but oscillation is
not recurrence and the diagnostics reject every one of them (\cref{app:eos}).

\subsection{The delay lag}\label{sec:tau}

The delay window spans $(E_{\max}-1)\tau$ samples. Below an appreciable fraction of the oscillation
period the delay coordinates are nearly collinear and the torus never unfolds
\citep{casdagli1991state}. Varying $\tau$ alone on a fixed torus moves the estimate over
more than an order of magnitude, through the truth only where the window covers a few tenths of a
period (\cref{fig:tau}). The lag must therefore be set from the oscillation period, which a
constructed system supplies and a training log does not. From the grokking logs we accordingly
take changes and never a level (\cref{sec:grokking}).

\subsection{Nuisance factors}\label{sec:nuisance}

At $r = 4$ we vary factors that leave $r$ unchanged over five seeds and twelve observers,
measuring each shift against the shift a real change of four components produces
(\cref{tab:controls}). A ramped observer gain moves the estimate by a component and a half, so a run
whose parameter norm grows steadily will drift by that much at fixed $r$. This is the confound that
matters most in \cref{sec:grokking}. Smoothness matters less: shifting the drive band by an octave
changes the roughness sharply while moving the estimate by about half as much. Roughness tracks the
estimate closely here and again in \cref{sec:matched}; \cref{app:clocks} holds each fixed in turn
while the other moves.

\subsection{The estimand is a dimension, not an effective rank}\label{sec:aniso}

Every construction in \cref{sec:ladder} drives its $r$ phases at equal amplitude, which makes the
active dimension and the effective rank of \cref{sec:three} numerically equal. Where two candidate
estimands agree, an experiment cannot say which of them the estimator reports. Driving phase $l$ at
amplitude $q^{l}$ separates them: the path still lies on an $r$-dimensional torus, while the
effective rank falls as $q$ falls. The estimate follows the active dimension (\cref{fig:aniso}),
staying closer to $r$ than to the effective rank and registering only the phases
it can resolve.

\paragraph{The diagnostics.}\label{sec:diagnostics-validated} Across this grid the identifiability
ratio again separates the accurate cases from the inaccurate ones without overlap, now within a
single regime as well as between regimes. The ratio and the trend-crossing count are calibrated on
these cases alone, so the band drawn in \cref{sec:grokking} records where the accurate cases fell
and carries no authority beyond them.

\section{Application to grokking}\label{sec:grokking}

We apply the procedure to the grokking settings of \cref{sec:related}: a transformer trained with
weight decay and AdamW on mini-batches, which places it in the stochastic regime of \cref{sec:classes}, and a two-layer
perceptron trained by unregularised full-batch gradient descent (\cref{app:runs} lists every run).

\subsection{The diagnostics reject both settings}\label{sec:grok-diagnostics}

We run the frozen configuration of \cref{sec:digits} on these logs, shortening the window and
stride to fit the records and changing nothing else (\cref{tab:grok-diagnostics}, \cref{fig:map}).
The regularised transformer runs are unstable in the embedding dimension, their estimates ranging
from four to thirteen components across configurations. The perceptron
runs and the transformer runs without weight decay carry the signature of a transient instead: two
trend crossings in a whole record, at a ratio still far outside the band. Those undecayed runs are
trained on mini-batches, so the regime label assigned in advance does not decide the outcome. The level is therefore not a dimension in either setting. A change
within a single run could still be one, since a fixed configuration adds the same offset to every
window of that run. This window cannot show it: it spans tens of thousands of optimiser steps
against a transition a few hundred steps wide.

\paragraph{Label-matched pairs.}\label{sec:pairs} The perceptron targets come in pairs differing by
a single monomial, both members of which fit the training set completely under identical
optimisation. The estimate does separate them, but every one of these runs is transient and the
separation is already visible in the raw loss curves (\cref{app:pairs,app:fb,app:falls}).

\subsection{The trajectory simplifies at generalisation}\label{sec:direct}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_dip.pdf}
\caption{Simplification of the trajectory at generalisation. Three regularised seeds of modular
addition and one $S_5$ composition, against the same configurations trained without weight decay,
aligned on the generalisation step of the run each matches. Thick line, the mean of the four
generalising runs; band, the spread between the hash families of \cref{app:sketch}. Panels (a) and

on the log of (b).}
\label{fig:dip}
\end{figure}

The effective rank is defined whether or not the path returns on itself, so a stored trajectory
allows a measurement that a log does not. We store the trajectory of a transformer trained to
grok, compressed at every logged step by a random projection of the
parameter vector and of the probe logits (\cref{app:sketch}). Near generalisation the effective
rank collapses in both spaces, reaching near unity in parameter space. In each space the minimum
falls after the transition and within two thousand steps of it. Both then re-expand (\cref{fig:dip},
\cref{tab:dip}). The collapse might follow training
progress, but it occurs at generalisation in seeds whose generalisation steps differ several-fold
and in a second task. It might record a trajectory that has come to a halt, but the total
displacement reaches its floor thousands of steps later and never recovers, whereas the effective
rank does recover. One caveat: the effective rank measures variation inside the window.
Only that variation collapses; $d_{\mathrm{act}}(R)$ itself may be unchanged.

\paragraph{The runs without weight decay.}\label{sec:nowd} The same two configurations trained
without weight decay memorise and stay at chance accuracy. Within the transition window they
decline much less than the generalising runs, but somewhere in training one of them declines just
as far (\cref{tab:dip}). Depth therefore does not separate the generalising runs from the
memorising ones; the timing of the decline separates them, as does whether it reverses. A low effective rank is reached both by a run that has come
to rest and by one about to generalise.

\subsection{The log estimate falls where the trajectory does}\label{sec:matched}

The test \cref{sec:grok-diagnostics} could not perform becomes possible once the window is set in
units of the transition, not as a fraction of the record. We re-run the estimator on the
same logs, at the window length and midpoints the direct measurement uses, so that both statistics
are sampled at the same instants. The frozen configuration does not fit a window that short, so we
report every cell of a grid and take as headline the cell named by a rule blind to the outcome
(\cref{app:window}).

On the parameter norm the estimate halves at the generalisation step in all four generalising runs
(\cref{fig:dip}f). That drop is among the largest any of the four ever shows, while the other two runs show nothing
remarkable there; all but two of the usable grid cells separate the generalising runs from the
other two (\cref{tab:matched}). We also compare each run against randomised series built from it
that keep its slow shape and its fluctuation spectrum while removing its fine structure. The fraction of those series falling as steeply as the run itself is
smaller for every generalising run than for either of the other two (\cref{app:window}).

The same event is thus recorded twice: once by storing the trajectory, once in a log a run writes
anyway. The second record is the weaker. A window as short as the transition need not lie inside one
regime, so the fall is a signal that tracks the transition and not a measurement of \cref{eq:act}. Its observer is the parameter norm, for which \cref{sec:nuisance}
leaves a smoothness explanation open, so the comparison against randomised series carries the claim
alone. The roughness of these windows falls by a far larger factor than the estimate, which merely
halves: a simpler statistic registers the change more strongly.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

In a deterministic recurrent system with a matched delay lag, one scalar log of a suitable observer
counts the active components of the regime, to within about one component and up to about eight. Outside that regime it does not: a transient supplies no returns;
mini-batch noise contributes directions of its own. The estimator is usable only alongside the
identifiability ratio and the trend-crossing count, which place both grokking settings outside the
regime it needs. Our result there comes from the stored trajectory, whose effective rank collapses
at generalisation and then re-expands.
\paragraph{Limitations.} The measurement needs a recurrent regime and a lag matched to a period no
log reveals. Above about eight components it is only ordinal. The collapse comes from four runs
against two, distinguished by weight decay, which also drives the parameter norm; it marks the
transition only once the transition has happened. It does not reproduce in the full-batch
setting. The drop at the matched window is close to the drop a ramped gain alone produces. The
edge-of-stability evidence comes from one task (\cref{app:config,app:fb,app:window,app:eos}).

\section{The results behind the estimator}\label{app:theory}

The results below are stated without proof and in the form the paper uses. Every condition
they impose is an assumption about the run rather than a property we can verify from the log.

Throughout, $f$ is the map advancing the system by one step, $\phi$ the scalar observation, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delaymap}
  \Phi_{f,\phi}(\vs) \;=\; \bigl(\phi(\vs),\, \phi(f(\vs)),\, \dots,\, \phi(f^{E-1}(\vs))\bigr)
  \;\in\; \R^{E}
\end{equation}
the delay map.

\begin{theorem}[Delay embedding; \citealp{takens1981detecting}]\label{thm:takens}
Let $M$ be a compact manifold of dimension $d$ and let $E > 2d$. For a generic pair $(f, \phi)$
with $f \in C^2(M, M)$ a diffeomorphism and $\phi \in C^2(M, \R)$, the delay map
\cref{eq:delaymap} is an embedding of $M$ into $\R^{E}$.
\end{theorem}

An embedding is one-to-one and an immersion, so distances in $\R^E$ between reconstructed points
are a faithful, though distorted, image of distances between states. The set they fill is the
object the neighbour statistic below measures (\cref{fig:shapes}). \Cref{thm:takens} requires a
manifold and the sets a training run occupies need not be one; the extension below is the version
the paper relies on.
\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_shapes.pdf}
\caption{The set the neighbour search measures, in the plane of the first two delay coordinates.
One window of the image-data system of \cref{sec:digits} under each excitation, reconstructed at
the frozen configuration. A single phase closes an orbit, two fill a surface, a converging run
never returns, and mini-batch noise fills the space it is given.}
\label{fig:shapes}
\end{figure}

\begin{theorem}[Fractal delay embedding prevalence; \citealp{sauer1991embedology}]\label{thm:sauer}
Let $A \subset \R^{n}$ be compact with box-counting dimension $d$, invariant under a smooth
diffeomorphism $f$ defined on an open neighbourhood of $A$, and let $E > 2d$ be an integer. Assume
that for each $p \le E$ the set of periodic points of $f$ of period $p$ has box-counting dimension
less than $p/2$, and that the linearisation of $f$ at each such orbit has distinct eigenvalues.
Then for almost every smooth $\phi$, in the sense of prevalence, the delay map \cref{eq:delaymap}
is one-to-one on $A$ and an immersion on every compact subset of a smooth manifold contained
in $A$.
\end{theorem}

The dimension in the condition $E > 2d$ is the box-counting dimension of the occupied set $\gA_R$,
which need not be an integer, so the criterion applies to the sets a training run occupies and not
only to tori. Prevalence, in turn, is a measure-theoretic notion of ``almost every'' on an
infinite-dimensional space.

Mini-batch sampling makes the update depend on a draw from the data as well as on the state, so
neither theorem above applies as stated.

\begin{theorem}[Delay embedding for forced systems;
\citealp{stark1999delay, stark2003delay}]\label{thm:stark}
Let $\vs_{t+1} = f(\vs_t, w_t)$ with the drive $w_t$ generated by a measure-preserving system on a
space $\Omega$, either deterministically or as an independent draw selecting among finitely many
maps. Under genericity conditions on $f$ and $\phi$ analogous to those of \cref{thm:takens}, and
for $E$ exceeding twice the dimension of a fibre, the map sending a state to its delay vector
\cref{eq:delaymap} is an embedding \emph{of each fibre} $M_{\omega}$, that is, of the set of states
compatible with one realisation $\omega$ of the drive.
\end{theorem}

The guarantee holds fibre by fibre, whereas the sample available in practice is one trajectory,
which lies in a different fibre at every step. No invariant measure on a fibre is being sampled, so
the neighbour statistic below estimates nothing.

\begin{theorem}[Maximum likelihood intrinsic dimension;
\citealp{levina2004maximum, mackay2005comments}]\label{thm:lb}
Let points be drawn from a smooth density supported on a $d$-dimensional manifold embedded in
$\R^{E}$. Model the points falling within a small ball about $\vy_i$ as a homogeneous Poisson
process in the $d$-dimensional radial variable, with the density constant over the ball. Writing
$r^{(i)}_j$ for the distance from $\vy_i$ to its $j$-th nearest neighbour, the maximum likelihood
estimate of $d$ from the first $m$ neighbours of that point is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lbpoint}
  \hat{d}_m(\vy_i)^{-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{m-1} \sum_{j=1}^{m-1} \log \frac{r^{(i)}_m}{r^{(i)}_j} .
\end{equation}
Under this model the reciprocal is unbiased and the estimate itself is not.
\end{theorem}

The estimator is therefore a local statistic: it reports the exponent over the range of radii the
$m$ nearest neighbours span rather than the limit \cref{eq:act} takes. For
$0 < \varepsilon_1 < \varepsilon_2$ that exponent at a point $\vc$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finitescale}
  d_R(\vc; \varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2) \;=\;
  \frac{\log \mu_R\bigl(B(\vc, \varepsilon_2)\bigr)
        - \log \mu_R\bigl(B(\vc, \varepsilon_1)\bigr)}
       {\log \varepsilon_2 - \log \varepsilon_1} .
\end{equation}
\Cref{eq:mg} pools it over the $N$ points of the record. It agrees with $d_{\mathrm{act}}(R)$ when
the same power of $\varepsilon$ continues below $\varepsilon_1$. A phase of smaller amplitude than
$\varepsilon_1$ is invisible to it however long the record. A record of $N$ points resolves a dimension of at most about
$2 \log_{10} N$ under the argument of \citet{eckmann1992fundamental}, a bound \cref{app:ceiling}
finds does not explain the ceiling we measure.

\section{The estimator, its configuration and the observers}\label{app:method}

\subsection{The estimator}\label{app:alg}

\Cref{alg:mg} is the estimator as it was run, with the departures from \cref{eq:mg} that affect
the values reported.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Pooled Levina--Bickel intrinsic dimension of a delay reconstruction}
\label{alg:mg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require series $x_{1:n}$; lag $\tau$; embedding dimension $E$; neighbours $m$; Theiler
exclusion $W_T$; dither $\sigma$
\State $z_t \gets (x_t - \operatorname{mean} x) / \operatorname{std} x + \sigma \epsilon_t$,\quad
       $\epsilon_t \sim \gN(0,1)$ \Comment{breaks exact ties}
\State $\vy_t \gets (z_t, z_{t-\tau}, \dots, z_{t-(E-1)\tau})$ for $t = (E-1)\tau+1, \dots, n$
\State $S \gets 0$; $N \gets 0$; $n_{\mathrm{dist}} \gets 0$; $n_{\mathrm{ratio}} \gets 0$
\For{each $\vy_i$}
  \State $r^{(i)}_1 \le \dots \le r^{(i)}_m \gets$ distances to the $m$ nearest $\vy_j$
         with $|i-j| > W_T$
  \State $r^{(i)}_j \gets \max(r^{(i)}_j, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{dist}})$ for each $j$; count those
         clipped into $n_{\mathrm{dist}}$
  \State $S_i \gets \max\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1} \log(r^{(i)}_m / r^{(i)}_j),\,
         \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ratio}}\bigr)$; count clips into $n_{\mathrm{ratio}}$
  \State $S \gets S + S_i$; \quad $N \gets N + 1$
\EndFor
\State mark the window degenerate if $n_{\mathrm{dist}}$ or $n_{\mathrm{ratio}}$ exceeds
       $1\,\%$ of its denominator
\State \Return $\bigl(N(m-1) - 1\bigr) / S$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\emph{The returned value is $(N(m-1)-1)/S$}, the exactly unbiased pooled form, which differs from
\cref{eq:mg} negligibly at the sizes used here. \emph{A window marked degenerate is
returned rather than discarded.} No published cell of \cref{tab:grok-diagnostics} contains a marked
window. \emph{The returned value is never clamped to $E$}, so a divergent estimate is reported as
divergent.

The Theiler exclusion is $W_T = \max\bigl((E-1)\tau,\, t_{\mathrm{acf}}\bigr)$, where
$t_{\mathrm{acf}}$ is the first lag at which the autocorrelation falls below $1/e$, capped at $150$
samples; \cref{app:theiler} measures the effect of that cap. The floors $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dist}}$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ratio}}$ and
the dither $\sigma$ are $10^{-8}$, $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-9}$.

\section{System constructions}\label{app:constructions}

\paragraph{The image-data system of \cref{sec:digits}.} The data is partitioned into twelve fixed
groups, the same partition at every rank. The
loss weights of $r$ of those groups are modulated by $r$ rationally independent sinusoids filling
one octave. Modulating group $j$ tilts the gradient along a direction $\vphi_j$. These
directions are neither orthogonal nor of equal effect, since random data groups have correlated
gradients, so a mixing matrix $\mathrm{pinv}(\Phi) Q$ with $Q$ an orthonormal basis of
$\operatorname{range}(\Phi)$ orthogonalises them. The per-phase scalar response gain of the
linearised update is then divided out.

per-example gradient covariance of the data, so projecting it onto $r$ directions recovers only
part of the intended rank and the estimates in that regime are scored against the measured
value.

\section{The direct trajectory measurement}\label{app:direct}

\subsection{The trajectory sketch}\label{app:sketch}

The direct measurement of \cref{sec:direct} needs the trajectory itself: the whole parameter vector
at each of two to three thousand logged steps. Every logged step is therefore
compressed to $\R^{1024}$ by a CountSketch \citep{charikar2002finding, weinberger2009feature}, a
sparse random projection drawn twice from two independent hash families so that the two copies can
be compared. Two spaces are recorded: the parameters, and the probe logits after centring each
example over classes and normalising to unit length, without which a signal taken from the logits
could not be told from a measurement of the weight decay its scale is coupled to.

We do not verify a spectral guarantee for the compression. The two hash families supply a direct
estimate of the error instead: every reported value is the mean of the two projections, with the
disagreement between them recorded beside it. That disagreement is a per cent of the value at the
median and under nine per cent at its worst, smaller than every effect \cref{sec:direct}
reports.

\emph{The sketch is accurate on synthetic trajectories of known rank.} It agrees to within $0.11$
components throughout (\cref{tab:sketch}). Nothing above rank ten is tested, however, whereas the
plateaus in \cref{fig:dip} are higher.

\emph{It agrees with exact arithmetic on a real run.} On the perceptron architecture the
participation ratio computed on all the raw parameters agrees with the sketched value to three
decimals.

\emph{Recording the trajectory does not perturb training.} The log is required to be bit-identical
with and without the recording, over four hundred steps at reduced probe size. One global random
stream supplies the split, the initialisation and the mini-batch order in turn, so a single stray
draw changes the initial weights and can destroy grokking.

\section{The resolution of a windowed statistic}\label{app:resolution}

\subsection{The full-batch setting}\label{app:fb}

Repeating the direct measurement on the perceptron setting gives a limit of the measurement rather
than a negative finding. Under full-batch gradient descent every
window of every run has a participation ratio between $1.00$ and $1.36$ on the statistics the
comparison uses.

A deterministic update makes the trajectory a smooth curve. Over a $600$-step window such a curve
is straight to within the measurement precision, so the statistic reports the curvature of the
trajectory at the scale the window sets, where there is almost none. The statistic has no range
in which a collapse could appear.

A range of window lengths settles the question (\cref{tab:prwindow}). Two runs of $150\,000$ steps,
a generalising one and its label-matched partner, are measured over windows spanning $600$ to
$118\,000$ optimiser steps, each holding the same sixty samples spread further apart as the window
lengthens, so that the bound on the statistic and its noise floor stay where the published
measurement put them. The statistic does leave its floor, at a scale of about $10^4$ steps, more than an order of magnitude above the published window. The absence of a feature at the shorter window was
therefore a limit of resolution rather than a fact about the run.

The feature that appears there separates nothing. One localised maximum appears, of the same size
in both runs (\cref{tab:prwindow}); function space agrees, with more range and no more separation.
Nor can a window this long place that maximum to better than the interval between memorisation and
generalisation. Beyond $30\,000$ steps the ordering even reverses and the non-generalising run
moves further, because the window is then a large fraction of a run whose weight norm is still
climbing, so that the statistic tracks that drift. The full-batch trajectory therefore carries no
signature of generalisation in its effective rank at any window length between $600$ and
$118\,000$ steps: the collapse of \cref{sec:direct} belongs to the regularised stochastic
setting.

\subsection{Declines of the estimate that are not generalisation}\label{app:falls}

A decline of the estimate is not specific to generalisation. In a long run at $p = 211$ with no
weight decay, which memorises early and ends at chance validation accuracy, a decline is detected
tens of thousands of steps after memorisation. The ramped-gain experiment of \cref{sec:nuisance}
supplies a mechanism for it that needs no dimension at all: the parameter norm of this run roughly
triples, acting on the estimate through the observer. No trajectory was stored for that run, so nothing here says
whether it simplified; at the frozen window and in these settings the estimate on a log is in any
case neither a dimension nor an effective rank.

\Cref{sec:matched} does not make the decline specific either. The estimate on a log does decline where the
trajectory does, in four runs and in neither of the other two, but at a window some seventy times
shorter than the one used here, under a configuration chosen for that window.

\subsection{Resolution of a windowed estimate on a training log}\label{app:window}

The change \cref{sec:grok-diagnostics} leaves open is testable on the seven extended runs. The
frozen configuration takes a window of a third of each record, $39\,990$ optimiser steps, advanced by $10\,000$ steps at a time. That gives nine windows
per run, localising a feature only to $\pm 19\,995$ steps. The runs therefore cannot be aligned on
their generalisation steps as \cref{fig:dip} aligns them: no two share an offset, so no mean over
runs can be formed.

The one run with a window centre near its generalisation step \emph{rises} by $2.93$ components
over the following stride and by $2.71$ over the one after. Those are the only two of the
fifty-six stride-to-stride changes to exceed the $1.53$-component floor a ramped gain alone
produces (\cref{sec:nuisance}). The other two generalising runs stay inside it throughout, as do
all four that never generalise (\cref{fig:window}). A rise is not the fall \cref{sec:matched}
reports, and at $\pm 19\,995$ steps neither could be assigned to the transition.

As in \cref{app:fb}, the statistic has no range in which a feature confined to the transition could
appear. Testing the claim requires a window set in units of the width of the transition, which for
these runs is hundreds of steps.

\paragraph{The matched-window re-run.} \Cref{sec:matched} performs that re-run on the six runs of
\cref{fig:dip} rather than the seven of this appendix: the question needs a paired comparison and
only those six had their trajectory stored. Each window is $60$ samples, centred on the midpoint of
a window of \cref{sec:direct}. The significance of the fall is assessed against the surrogates of
\cref{sec:matched} \citep{theiler1992testing, schreiber1996improved}.

\emph{The frozen configuration does not fit.} At $60$ samples its delay span of $76$ exceeds the
window. The grid crosses $E_{\max} \in \{4, 6, 10\}$,
$\tau \in \{1, 2, 4\}$, $m \in \{5, 20\}$ and both Theiler rules, giving thirty-six cells, of which
twenty-six are long enough to return a value. Twenty-four of those separate the four generalising
runs from the other two. The headline cell is named by the rule of \cref{sec:matched}, which does
not choose the best cell: a neighbouring cell separates the runs more sharply.

\emph{The diagnostics do not survive the shortening.} The identifiability ratio needs an embedding
of $2E_{\max}$, so at the headline cell the Theiler exclusion leaves too few usable points to
compute one. Where the ratio can be computed at all, the two Theiler rules disagree about whether
it passes; it settles nothing.

\emph{Linear detrending removes the effect.} Repeating every cell on a linearly detrended window
leaves none that separates the generalising runs from the rest. Detrending a sketch and
detrending a scalar are not comparable: on a $1024$-dimensional sketch a per-coordinate linear

\emph{The observer matters.} On the training loss rather than the parameter norm, twelve of the
twenty-six cells separate them, though the surrogate comparison passes in the $S_5$ run
only.

returns about $2.77$, close to the pre-transition level of every run. The parameter-norm log of the

runs; the $S_5$ run is a weaker case of the same thing. Their $D$ and quantile columns therefore
record an observer that offered the estimator no local structure to respond to. They are not
dimensional negatives. The four generalising runs are untouched, and so is the surrogate
comparison, in which each run is measured only against surrogates of itself.

\section{The Theiler exclusion}\label{app:theilerall}

\subsection{The exclusion cap at the twenty-component configuration}\label{app:theiler}

The rule of \cref{app:alg} asks for at least the full span of a delay vector, so that two
neighbours cannot be near each other merely because their windows overlap; the implementation caps
that at $150$ samples.

At the eight-component configuration the embedding span is $76$, so the cap binds only where the
autocorrelation time exceeds $150$. That never happens on the constructed systems, whose
autocorrelation time is a few samples, but it happens on five of the seven parameter-norm records
of \cref{sec:grokking}. Those five were scored at $W_T = 150$ rather than at the value the rule
asks for, in the direction that inflates the estimate. At the twenty-component configuration the
span is $624$ and the cap binds unconditionally. It is why \cref{sec:digits} marks that result
exploratory.

\emph{The value at $W_T = 0$ is the right one.} On a uniformly sampled curve the nearest
neighbours of a point are its immediate temporal neighbours, for which \cref{eq:mg} returns
$1.199$; the transient returns that value in every cell. The excess over unity is the lattice bias
of a deterministic sampling.

\emph{The exclusion removes the near neighbours, not the neighbours.} The estimator fills its
twenty slots at every exclusion; only the distances change. Before any exclusion the nearest
neighbours of a point lie thousands of samples away under the fast quasiperiodic drive, but six
samples away on the transient (\cref{fig:traces}). Excluding $150$ samples therefore costs the fast
drive almost none of its close pairs and the transient all of them (\cref{fig:exclusion}). The distances the transient retains are then
nearly equal, their log-ratio sum collapses, and the reciprocal in \cref{eq:mg} diverges. An
exponential decay with no optimiser and no data
behind it reproduces both ends.

\emph{The asymmetry is general; the value $29$ is not.} Under the fast drive $W_T = 0$ already
returns $r$, because that case is not oversampled; it is the slow drive that the
exclusion repairs, its nearest neighbours lying some seven hundred samples away. A transient also
need not have dimension one: a decaying \emph{oscillation} correctly returns $2.36$ at every
exclusion.

\paragraph{A recurrence-based diagnostic.} The ratio
$\hat d_{MG}(W_T{=}150)/\hat d_{MG}(W_T{=}20)$ is unity under both recurrent drives and above five
on the transient, so it tests recurrence in the reconstructed space where the trend-crossing count
tests only non-monotonicity of the series: the gap \cref{sec:diagnostics} leaves open, which
\cref{app:eos} shows to matter on real runs. The principled version is older than either: a
space--time separation plot \citep{provenzale1992distinguishing} shows directly how neighbour
distance depends on separation in time, and the exclusion should be chosen from one.

\section{Descent at the edge of stability}\label{app:eos}

Descent at the edge of stability \citep{cohen2021gradient} is gradient descent at a fixed rate: the
top Hessian eigenvalue sharpens until it reaches $2/\eta$ and then stays there, while the loss
descends over long stretches and not over short ones. Such a run is deterministic and is not a
plain transient, so an undriven trajectory could satisfy the conditions of \cref{sec:classes}.

\paragraph{Setting.} We run full-batch descent without weight decay on the quadratic perceptron of
\cref{app:runs} (\cref{tab:eos}). The log is written at \emph{every} optimiser step, because the oscillation is the two-cycle of the unstable
mode and its period is a few steps. The sharpness is measured by power iteration on Hessian-vector
products, taking the largest \emph{algebraic} eigenvalue, since the Hessian is indefinite early in
training.

\paragraph{Oscillation is not recurrence.} Eight runs reach the edge and all eight oscillate, the
loss rising on one step in two, but only five of them return close to states already passed
through. The return column of \cref{tab:eos} separates those two groups by a factor of ten with
nothing in between.

runs at the edge, on the training loss and on the parameter norm alike, whether or not
they recur, $\rho_{\mathrm{ident}}$ lying far outside it in both groups while the trend-crossing
count, which tests non-monotonicity and not recurrence, passes every one of them. Nor does the
estimate separate the runs that return from those that do not: their ranges on the training loss
overlap.

uses, most of the phenomenon disappears (\cref{fig:eos}): over these eight runs the median
trend-crossing count falls from $71$ to $6$. Whether a log looks like a transient is thus in part a
property of how often it was written.

\paragraph{A purely linear statistic reproduces the whole dependence.} Across the $E_{\max}$ sweep
$\PR_{\mathrm{delay}}$ rises over the same range as MG; across the record sweep it is flat; and a
single logarithm in $E_{\max}$ fits it more tightly than any form fits anything else in this
appendix. Most of the ceiling is therefore the number of components the delay window resolves
\emph{linearly}.

\paragraph{The unexplained tracking limit.} The ceiling lies near eleven components for no reason
we can give. It does not follow the measured effective rank, which equals $r$ throughout, nor does
any form we fitted account for it.

The identifiability ratio of \cref{sec:diagnostics} does register it. At $r = 20$ the ratio stays
above the band at every record length, so a longer record never makes an unidentifiable estimate
identifiable; it falls into the band as $E_{\max}$ grows, at about the rank where tracking
stops.

\section{Two controls separating the estimator from roughness}\label{app:clocks}

\emph{The number of components changes and the roughness does not.} Both systems in this control
are a sum of four sinusoids. In the first the four frequencies are rationally independent, so the path
lies on a four-dimensional torus and $d_{\mathrm{act}} = 4$. In the second they are harmonics of one
irrational base frequency: a single phase fixes all four sinusoids at once, the path is a circle,
and $d_{\mathrm{act}} = 1$. The base frequency of the second system is then set by bisection so
that the two have the \emph{same} roughness, to a part in $10^{15}$. A scheduled run passes between
the two through ramped envelopes, leaving unscored every window a ramp reaches into
(\cref{fig:switch}).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_switch.pdf}
\caption{A change in the number of independent phases, tracked as it happens. The harmonic control
of this appendix, scheduled from four phases to one and back; median over eight seeds, band their
range. Dashed in (a), the number of phases, drawn only where no ramp reaches into the window. The
linear statistic of (c) moves in the opposite direction.}
\label{fig:switch}
\end{figure}

\emph{The roughness changes and the number of components does not.} Five monotone observers
$g(x) = x + a x^{p}$ are applied to one four-phase system. Each is invertible, so it may bend the
recorded set but cannot add or remove a coordinate. Each also changes the local scale $g'(x)$ and
with it the spacing of consecutive samples.

Both criteria were fixed before the runs, the second of them as a tenth of a component about four.
\Cref{tab:clocks} shows both hold.

Both controls run on a constructed system whose phases are known, so they say nothing about a
training log.

Two findings count against the estimator. The linear participation ratio is higher on the circle
than on the four-torus (\cref{tab:clocks}), so anyone treating it as a count of phases would order
the two backwards. On the circle the estimate is also not geometric: every neighbour surviving the
exclusion there is a sample from another pass around the circle. The local speed of the curve
multiplies all of a point's neighbour distances alike; speed cancels from the log-ratios of
\cref{eq:mg}. The
residue depends on the arithmetic of the base frequency: two of the eight seeds drew a frequency
whose multiples cluster into near-duplicates; their estimate is reproducible from the fractional
parts of those multiples alone, with no signal and no neighbour search.

\section{Compute cost and code availability}\label{app:compute}

\paragraph{Compute cost.} Everything here runs in a few hours on one consumer GPU and one desktop
CPU. The largest GPU item is the edge-of-stability experiment of \cref{app:eos},
which takes half an hour on a single T4; the analysis is the more expensive half and runs entirely
on CPU, a few core-hours per experiment.

\paragraph{Cost of the trajectory sketch.} At each logged step the sketch keeps $4096$ floats
against the full parameter vectors of \cref{app:runs}: a run whose raw trajectory would occupy $88$
megabytes occupies $2.5$. We train the same configuration with and without the sketch attached,
alternating the order so that warm-up cannot favour either. At a logging stride of ten steps the
sketch adds about six per cent to the training time. \texttt{sketch\_cost.py} records the
measurement.

\paragraph{Code availability.} All code, configurations and result files behind every number and
figure in this paper will be released publicly on acceptance. The figures regenerate from the
committed result files with a single command.
