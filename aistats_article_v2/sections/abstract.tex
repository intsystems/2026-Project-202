\begin{abstract}
\claude{Neural network training can restrict parameter motion, reduce information stored in a representation, or produce more regular behavior. Tracking these changes through full network states can be expensive. We propose a method for detecting such simplification from a single scalar time series. The method combines delay reconstruction, a pooled nearest-neighbor dimension estimator, and a calibrated change detector. Delay-embedding theory motivates its interpretation in terms of active components. Controlled experiments test this interpretation: in trained recurrent generators, one neuron's activity distinguishes independent phases from harmonics that spectral peak counts cannot separate. In applied tasks, we validate changes in the estimate against independent measurements. A detector calibrated on a small CNN identifies 27 of 28 strong training interventions with no alarms in 16 control records; the same threshold detects backbone freezing in ResNet-18. In a text VAE, the estimate tracks both latent information loss and its partial preservation across nine seeds. Analysis takes 9.5\,ms per CNN log window and is 14--19 times faster than a full Lyapunov spectrum in a recurrent-network benchmark. These results establish a low-cost diagnostic of several forms of learned simplification, with a component interpretation in suitable recurrent regimes.}
\end{abstract}
