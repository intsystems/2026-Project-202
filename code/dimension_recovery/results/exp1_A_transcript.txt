  Two distinct failures, at opposite ends, and one in the middle:
   * too few cycles -- the window sees an arc. Ordering can survive but the
     spread collapses to ~0.07, which no finite sample could resolve. This is
     the regime the training logs are in.
   * too much noise -- neighbours sit inside the noise ball and every k
     returns something near the embedding dimension. At SNR 1e2 the ordering
     is destroyed at every window length.
   * too many cycles at k=1 -- a single sinusoid observed over 30+ periods
     has near-exact recurrences, the log-ratios blow up and MG returns 0.3.
     The estimator is least trustworthy exactly where the motion is simplest.

  Under 'widening' the roughness ratio tracks k as well as any dimension
  estimator, because it is reading bandwidth. Any experiment built that way
  cannot distinguish geometry from smoothness and proves nothing. Under
  'matched' roughness is flat and MG still moves, which is the comparison
  that has content. Everything after this uses 'matched'.

  The largest k that can be resolved rises with cycles per window, which is
  what a coverage argument predicts: to see a k-torus locally the window must
  contain returns in all k directions, and that needs of order c^k points.
  At the top of the range low k breaks down instead -- a single sinusoid seen
  over a thousand periods has near-exact recurrences and MG collapses. There
  is no single setting that is correct for every k, only a band.
