===============================================================================
2  Detection lag at each change
===============================================================================
  A change is called at the first window whose estimate crosses the midpoint
  between the two regimes' own medians and stays across for two windows. Lag
  is measured from the true switch. One window is the floor: a window
  straddling the switch is a mixture, so nothing can beat 2000 samples.

      change   seed 0   seed 1   seed 2   median  in windows
        1->2     1399     1199     1199     1199         0.6
        2->3      599      599      199      599         0.3
        3->4      399      399      399      399         0.2
        4->3     1799     1999     1799     1799         0.9
        3->2     1599     1599     1999     1599         0.8

  The asymmetry runs the opposite way to the one I expected when writing
  this file, and the measurement is what counts. Rises are detected in 0.2 to
  0.6 of a window, falls in 0.8 to 0.9. A new oscillator widens the local
  neighbour structure as soon as it appears, so a rise shows up while the
  window is still mostly old data. A fall cannot show up until the old,
  higher-dimensional data has left the window, so it is floored near one
  window length. A criterion built on a dimension DROP therefore pays the
  worse half of this asymmetry -- roughly a full window of lag -- and the
  published 3000-step window already exceeds most plateaus in this project.

===============================================================================
3  Abrupt switch against a ramp
===============================================================================
  With a ramp the true dimension is undefined inside it, because an
  oscillator below the noise floor is not a degree of freedom the data has.
  This is not a limitation of the estimator, and any experiment that reports
  a detection lag against a ramped ground truth is measuring its own ramp.

     ramp  seg 0 (k=1)  seg 3 (k=4)  seg 5 (k=2)  range over regimes
        0         1.22         4.20         2.20                2.98
      500         1.22         4.20         2.20                2.98
     2000         1.22         4.25         2.21                3.02
     6000         2.11         4.26         3.16                2.14

  A ramp comparable to the segment length flattens the whole trace, because
  no regime is ever stationary for a full window.
