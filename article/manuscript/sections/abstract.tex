\begin{abstract}
\claude{\cx{It is easy to record the loss or weight norm at each step of neural network training.} Can \cx{one such time series} reveal what \cx{is changing} inside the network?
For a scalar quantity $x$ recorded over time, we form delay vectors from samples taken at fixed time lags. We estimate \cx{the local intrinsic dimension of their distribution} with the MacKay--Ghahramani (MG) variant of the Levina--Bickel estimator, which pools log ratios of nearest-neighbor distances across the delay vectors. We call this estimate the \emph{MG of $x$}.
After a recurrent network learns periodic motion, \cx{MG from the activation time series of one hidden neuron is close to one, as expected for a periodic orbit.}
In ResNet-18, \cx{freezing the feature extractor lowers the MG of the weight norm and concentrates weight updates in fewer directions.}
In a text variational autoencoder, \cx{the MG of reconstruction loss on fixed sentences is lower under settings where the decoder relies less on the latent code.}
\cx{We also use MG to select among candidate encoders trained on unlabeled MNIST digits with SimCLR, SimSiam, BYOL, and VICReg. For each frozen encoder, we fit a logistic regression classifier to predict digit labels from its features. In initial experiments, the MG of the gradient norm late in training correlated positively with classifier accuracy across encoders. When we selected the encoder with the highest MG of the gradient norm in each held-out run, its test accuracy averaged only 0.41 percentage points below the maximum for that set of encoders.}}
\end{abstract}
