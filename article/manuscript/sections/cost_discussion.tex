\section{Discussion}\label{sec:cost}
MG describes the geometry of the recorded signal. Noise and temporal dependence can affect this geometry, so interpretation requires evidence about what the signal captures. In a modular-addition control, MG changes while validation accuracy stays low (\appref{app:additional}).

Monitoring cost includes recording the log. In the VAE scheduling test, measuring loss on fixed sentences accounts for most of the time (\appref{app:cost}). On existing logs, roughness detects more loss spikes at the same false-positive rate, and querying fewer delay vectors speeds up forecast selection.

\section[Conclusion]{Conclusion}
We propose a common way to track dynamical simplification from a single scalar log. In recurrent networks trained to produce prescribed oscillations, MG captures differences in the structure of repeated motion. In our training experiments, changes in MG from window to window track independently measured simplification, including in feedforward networks.
